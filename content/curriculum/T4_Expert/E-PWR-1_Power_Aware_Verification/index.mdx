## Quick Take

- **What it is:** Running functional tests while the design's power intent is live, so that a power domain really switches off, loses its state and wakes up again, and building the UVM environment so that it drives those power events and stays correct through them. The power intent is the IEEE 1801 (UPF) description you met in [I-SV-8](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-8_Power_Intent_and_UPF/index); this lesson is about the testbench (TB) around it.
- **Why it matters:** A testbench that ignores power events fails in three ways. It **hangs**, because a driver waits for a response from a domain that has no power. It **cries wolf**, because the scoreboard counts the responses an off domain never sends as errors. Or it **passes a broken chip**, because nothing checks that state was saved, restored and reset in the right order, or that unknown values (X) never leaked into the logic that stays on.
- **The Analogy:** Closing an airport runway for maintenance. You stop new departures (quiesce the traffic), deal with the planes already rolling (abort or finish them, and count them), close the runway (isolate, save, power off), and reopen it only after the lights, the radar and the inspection are all done (power on, reset, restore, and only then release the isolation). *Where it breaks:* a runway closure is announced in advance, but a domain can lose power abruptly; the testbench must learn of the event from the power controller or a monitor, and must cope with transfers that were cut off half-way.

**You will be able to:**
1. **Predict** what drivers, monitors and scoreboards observe during a power cycle (isolation clamps, X, reset values and restored values), and **adapt** them: pause checking, flush in-flight expectations with accounting, and expect reset or restored values after wake-up.
2. **Write** a power-cycle virtual sequence that quiesces traffic, drives clock stop, isolation, save, power, reset and restore through a power-management agent, and resumes traffic.
3. **Write** power-aware assertions (isolation before power-down, isolation held until power, reset and restore are complete, save and restore only while powered, no X into the always-on domain) and power-sequence coverage with transition and illegal bins.
4. **Diagnose** power bugs from their symptoms and logs: X leakage, lost retention, a wake-up that skipped or misordered the reset, and hung phases.
5. **Choose** how to stop stimulus on a power event (quiesce, a cooperative flag, a driver abort, `kill()`, `stop_sequences()`), and **explain** why a callback cannot abort a sequence.

## Build Your Mental Model

**How to read this lesson.** The core path is the first four subsections: the words, the design's power intent, what the testbench sees during a power cycle, and where power lives in a UVM environment. Practitioner depth follows, one testbench component at a time: the virtual sequence, the scoreboard, the driver, stopping stimulus, objections, assertions and coverage. Make It Work assembles all of it into one bench you can compile and run in any 4-state simulator, and Push Further holds the expert layer.

### Words you need first

- **Power domain:** a group of design instances that share one primary supply, so they switch on and off together. This lesson's design has two: **PD_TOP**, which is **always on**, and **PD_CPU**, which is **switchable**.
- **Power management unit (PMU):** the controller, itself in the always-on domain, that drives a switchable domain's control signals in the right order: clock enable, isolation, retention save and restore, power switch and reset.
- **Power switch:** the cell that connects a domain's supply to the main supply. Here `cpu_pwr_en` asks it to switch, and `cpu_pwr_ack` (the **acknowledge**) says it has finished switching.
- **Power-aware simulation:** simulation with the UPF power intent loaded, so the simulator models what happens when a supply goes off. When PD_CPU loses power, the simulator **corrupts** its state: every 4-state register and net driven from inside it reads X.
- **Isolation cell and clamp:** a gate at the domain boundary, powered from the always-on supply, that forces an output of the switchable domain to a fixed value, the **clamp**, while isolation is enabled. It protects the always-on logic from the X of an off domain.
- **Retention register, save, restore:** a register with a shadow element on the always-on supply. A **save** copies the register into the shadow before power-down; a **restore** copies it back after power-up. Registers without retention come back as X until they are reset.
- **Quiesce:** stop issuing new traffic and let the transfers already in progress finish, so that nothing is in flight when the power changes.
- **Transfer, response:** in this lesson's design, PD_TOP sends a request to PD_CPU, and PD_CPU answers one cycle later with a **response**. The driver treats the response as its acknowledge.
- **UVM and SystemVerilog terms used here:** virtual sequence and virtual sequencer ([I-UVM-3B](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-UVM-3B_Advanced_Sequencing_and_Layering/virtual-sequences)); agent, driver, monitor and sequencer ([I-UVM-2A](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-UVM-2A_Component_Roles/index)); analysis port and `uvm_analysis_imp_decl` ([I-UVM-2B](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-UVM-2B_TLM_Connections/index)); objection and phase ([I-UVM-1C](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-UVM-1C_UVM_Phasing/index)); `uvm_event` ([A-UVM-8](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-8_Multi_Agent_Topologies/index)); clocking block ([F4C](/curriculum/T1_Foundational/F4C_Clocking_Blocks/index)); `bind` ([I-SV-4C](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-4C_Checkers/index)); callback, a hook object a component calls at a fixed point in its own code ([A-UVM-5](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-5_UVM_Callbacks/index)); time slot, all the activity the simulator schedules at one simulation time ([F3C](/curriculum/T1_Foundational/F3C_Delta_Cycles_and_Race_Conditions/index)).

### The design and its power intent

Every example on this page uses one small design. PD_CPU holds two 8-bit registers: **CTX** (`ctx_reg`), which is retained, and **SCRATCH** (`scratch`), which is not. PD_TOP reads and writes them over a one-cycle request bus (`req_valid`, `req_write`, `req_addr`, `req_wdata`) and receives a response one cycle later (`rsp_valid`, `rsp_rdata`). In the testbench the PMU's role is played by a UVM agent that drives six control signals.

| Power-intent element (IEEE 1801-2018 command) | In this design | Controlled by |
|---|---|---|
| Power domain (`create_power_domain`, §6.20) | PD_TOP is always on; PD_CPU holds `ctx_reg` and `scratch` | — |
| Power switch (`create_power_switch`, §6.22) | VDD_CPU, PD_CPU's switched supply | `cpu_pwr_en`; the switch answers on `cpu_pwr_ack` |
| Isolation (`set_isolation`, §6.45) | PD_CPU's outputs `rsp_valid` and `rsp_rdata`, clamped to 0 | `cpu_iso_en` (1 = clamp) |
| Retention (`set_retention`, §6.50) | `ctx_reg` only | `cpu_save`, `cpu_restore` (one-cycle pulses) |
| Not UPF: clock gating | PD_CPU's clock enable | `cpu_clk_en` |
| Not UPF: domain reset | PD_CPU's asynchronous reset | `cpu_rst_n` (active low) |

The UPF syntax itself belongs to [I-SV-8](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-8_Power_Intent_and_UPF/index); the clause numbers above are from the table of contents of IEEE 1801-2018 (published as IEC 61523-4:2023), and its clause 9 defines the simulation semantics. So that the bench on this page runs in any simulator, Make It Work writes PD_CPU as a **behavioural model** that does in plain SystemVerilog what a power-aware simulator does with the UPF: it corrupts the state to X while VDD_CPU is off, clamps the outputs while `cpu_iso_en` is 1, and copies `ctx_reg` to and from a retention element. With real UPF, the RTL stays unchanged and the simulator adds that behaviour.

The safe order, used everywhere on this page:
- **Power-down:** stop the clock (CLK_OFF), isolate (ISO_ON), save (SAVE), remove power (PWR_OFF, then wait for the acknowledge).
- **Wake-up:** restore power (PWR_ON, wait for the acknowledge), reset the domain (RST), restore the retained state (RESTORE), restart the clock (CLK_ON), and release isolation last (ISO_OFF).

Save and isolate may swap, because both happen while the domain is powered. The wake-up steps may not: each one depends on the one before it, and the next sections show the bug each swap causes.

### What the testbench sees during a power cycle

The testbench sits in, or connects to, the always-on side. What it reads from PD_CPU depends on the class of the signal and on the step of the cycle:

| What you look at | While PD_CPU is off | After power returns | After PD_CPU's reset | After the restore |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| An isolated output (`rsp_valid`, clamp 0), as PD_TOP sees it | 0, the clamp | still the clamp | still the clamp | still the clamp; after the release, PD_CPU's real value |
| An output with no isolation (a bug), 4-state | X | X | its reset value | PD_CPU's value |
| A non-retained register (`scratch`) | X | X: power returning restores nothing | its reset value, 8'h00 | still 8'h00: restore touches only retained elements |
| A retained register (`ctx_reg`) | X; the saved copy waits on the always-on supply | X | its reset value, 8'h00 | the saved value, 8'hA5 |
| A 2-state variable inside PD_CPU | usually 0, which looks valid and hides the bug (tool-dependent) | — | its reset value | — |

Three rules come out of the table:
- **Powered-down outputs do not read 0.** Only *isolated* outputs read the clamp. An unisolated 4-state output reads X, and an X is what isolation exists to stop. The clamp value is chosen so that the bus looks idle: `rsp_valid` clamped to 0 means "no response".
- **Power returning restores nothing.** Corruption gives 4-state variables the value X, which is also their default initial value (IEEE 1800-2023 Table 6-7). A 2-state variable cannot hold X, and power-aware simulators typically corrupt it to 0; how they treat 2-state types, and the partial-corruption simstates of IEEE 1801-2018 §4.8, are tool-dependent. Use 4-state types (`logic`) at power boundaries so that corruption stays visible.
- **Reset, then restore, then release.** A reset after the restore wipes the restored value. A release before the restore hands PD_TOP the wrong value (X, or the reset value). Whether a reset and a restore in the same cycle favour one or the other is tool-dependent, so never let them overlap.

**Debrief.** With "Release isolation before restore", X reaches PD_TOP at step 5, the release: power is back but `ctx` is still X, because power returning restored nothing. The restore at step 6 brings 8'hA5 back, one step too late, so the outcome is an X leak with the context recovered. Now try the other two broken presets:
- "Power off before isolation": X leaks at step 2, the moment power drops, but `ctx` still comes back, because it was saved while powered.
- "Save after power-off": the save at step 3 copies X, the restore copies X back, and X leaks at step 6 when isolation is released: both an X leak and a lost context.

The model leaves out three things real flows need: stopping the clock, resetting the non-retained logic after power-up, and waiting for the power switch's acknowledge. The bench on this page adds all three.

### Where power lives in a UVM environment

A power-aware environment is an ordinary multi-agent environment ([A-UVM-8](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-8_Multi_Agent_Topologies/index)) with one extra agent and three power-aware habits. The **PMU agent** drives the power controls; at block level it plays the PMU, and at SoC level the real PMU drives the controls and the agent programs it instead. The **virtual sequencer** holds handles to both agents' sequencers, so one virtual sequence can order traffic and power events. The PMU agent's **monitor** publishes every power event as a `pwr_item` on its analysis port, so that the scoreboard and the coverage collector learn of power events the same way they learn of traffic.

The three habits, each with its own subsection below:
- **Stimulus is ordered in one place.** The virtual sequence quiesces the traffic before it touches the power controls, and resumes it only after the release.
- **Checkers know about power.** The scoreboard pauses and flushes when isolation goes on, and expects reset or restored values after the release. Assertions check the order of the controls themselves.
- **Drivers never wait for a dead domain.** Every driver that waits for a response from PD_CPU also listens for the power event, and still completes its handshake with the sequencer.

### The power-cycle virtual sequence

The virtual sequence `power_cycle_vseq` runs the whole cycle. It starts traffic on the bus agent's sequencer and PMU actions on the PMU agent's sequencer, one after the other, so the order is explicit in one `body()`:

```systemverilog
// snippet: power_cycle_vseq::body() (complete class in Make It Work)
task body();
  run_bus(BUS_INIT);                     // CTX = 8'hA5, SCRATCH = 8'h3C
  run_bus(BUS_CHECK);                    // read both back
  // start() has returned, so every item is done: nothing is in flight (quiesced)
  run_pmu({CLK_OFF, ISO_ON, SAVE, PWR_OFF, HOLD});
  run_pmu({PWR_ON, RST, RESTORE, CLK_ON, ISO_OFF});
  run_bus(BUS_CHECK);                    // CTX must read 8'hA5, SCRATCH 8'h00
  run_bus(BUS_RANDOM);                   // and PD_CPU works normally again
endtask
```

**Quiescing is free when sequences run in series.** A child sequence's `start()` returns only when its `body()` has finished, and a bus item finishes only after the driver has seen its response and called `item_done()`. So when `run_bus()` returns, no transfer is in flight, and the PMU actions can begin. When traffic runs in parallel with the power cycle (a background sequence, an interrupt responder), quiescing needs a cooperative stop, covered under stopping stimulus below.

Each PMU action is one `pwr_item`. The PMU driver starts every action at a clock edge, so two actions never share a cycle, and it waits for the switch's acknowledge on PWR_OFF and PWR_ON. Never reset or restore before the acknowledge says VDD_CPU is back: until then the domain is not powered, and a reset or restore has nothing to act on. Randomize the time the PMU waits in a real environment (here the switch model's delay is a parameter), and keep the PMU's controls stable while it waits: with a modelled switch, an X on its control input has undefined results. In the UPF, the switch and its acknowledge are part of `create_power_switch` (IEEE 1801-2018 §6.22), which has options for an acknowledge port and its delay; how a simulator models the acknowledge is tool-dependent, so this bench models it in `cpu_pd_model`.

**Two rules the wake-up order depends on.**
- **The domain reset must work with the clock stopped,** or be given a clock. In this bench PD_CPU's reset is asynchronous (`always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge cpu_rst_n ...)`), so RST works before CLK_ON. With a synchronous reset, a reset pulsed while the clock is gated never takes effect, and the non-retained state stays X; enable the clock for the reset window instead, and write that order down.
- **Restore after the reset, never before.** The reset puts every register at its reset value, the restore then overwrites the retained ones, and the release comes last.

### Monitors and scoreboards across a power cycle

A monitor needs no change for power: it publishes what it sees at the boundary. Because `rsp_valid` is clamped to 0 while PD_CPU is isolated, the bus monitor simply sees no responses. The data lines may be X, but a protocol monitor samples data only with valid, so that is harmless. A monitor that samples data without looking at valid is the one that gets into trouble.

The **scoreboard** is where power changes the rules. A scoreboard that knows nothing about power keeps comparing through the power cycle, and that is wrong in both directions:
- Expectations for requests that were in flight when isolation went on never complete, so `check_phase` reports them as pending: a false failure, although PD_CPU behaved correctly.
- After the wake-up, a model that still holds the pre-power values for every register expects SCRATCH to read 8'h3C, while a correct PD_CPU returns 8'h00.

The fix is a third analysis imp, `pwr_export`, fed by the PMU monitor, and a few lines that encode the **specification** of a power cycle, not the order in which the PMU happened to act:

```systemverilog
// snippet: pwr_scoreboard::write_pwr() (complete class in Make It Work)
function void write_pwr(pwr_item t);
  case (t.op)
    ISO_ON: begin                        // no response can reach PD_TOP from now on
      dropped_by_power += exp_q.size();  // count them, then forget them
      exp_q.delete();
      comparing = 0;
      m_saved   = m_ctx;                 // what retention must bring back
    end
    PWR_OFF: lost_power = 1;
    ISO_OFF: begin
      if (lost_power) begin              // what a correct wake-up gives:
        m_ctx      = m_saved;            //   retained: the value at power-down
        m_scratch  = 8'h00;              //   not retained: the reset value
        lost_power = 0;
      end
      comparing = 1;
    end
    default: ;                           // the order of the steps is the checker's job
  endcase
endfunction
```

Notice the design decision in the last line. If the scoreboard's model copied every PMU event (reset sets the model to 0, restore loads the saved value), then a DUT whose PMU restores *before* resetting would be predicted correctly, CTX = 8'h00, and the bug would pass. The scoreboard checks **outcomes** against the specification; the assertions below check the **order** of the controls.

Traced through the Make It Work run (`hold_cycles` = 5):

| Time | What the monitors publish | `exp_q` after | Scoreboard action | matches | `dropped_by_power` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 55 ns | request: WRITE CTX 8'ha5 | a5 | model CTX = a5 | 0 | 0 |
| 65 ns | response a5 | (empty) | match | 1 | 0 |
| 85–155 ns | WRITE SCRATCH 3c, READ CTX, READ SCRATCH, and their responses | (empty) | three matches | 4 | 0 |
| 185 ns | ISO_ON | (empty) | flush 0, pause, `m_saved` = a5 | 4 | 0 |
| 245 ns | PWR_OFF | (empty) | `lost_power` = 1 | 4 | 0 |
| 355–425 ns | PWR_ON, RST, RESTORE, CLK_ON | (empty) | ignored: the checker owns the order | 4 | 0 |
| 435 ns | ISO_OFF | (empty) | model CTX = a5, SCRATCH = 00; resume | 4 | 0 |
| 445–485 ns | READ CTX → a5, READ SCRATCH → 00 | (empty) | two matches | 6 | 0 |
| 505–605 ns | four random items | (empty) | four matches | 10 | 0 |
| 625 ns | `check_phase`, `report_phase` | (empty) | `pending=0`; summary | 10 | 0 |

If the virtual sequence had powered down abruptly, with one read in flight at 185 ns, the 185 ns row would show `exp_q` holding one entry, flushed: `dropped_by_power` = 1, and the summary would report it. Count what you drop; a test that drops everything and checks nothing must not pass, so `check_phase` also fails a run with zero matches.

### Drivers: aborting a transfer safely

A driver that waits for PD_CPU's response waits for ever if PD_CPU loses power, or is isolated, before it answers. The bus driver therefore races the response against the power event:

```systemverilog
// snippet: bus_drv::run_phase() (complete class in Make It Work)
forever begin
  seq_item_port.get_next_item(req);
  if (cpu_offline.is_on()) begin
    req.status = BUS_DROPPED;            // PD_CPU is isolated: do not touch its pins
  end else begin
    fork begin                           // a guard: disable fork kills only these two
      fork
        drive_and_wait_rsp(req);         // blocks for ever if the response never comes
        begin
          cpu_offline.wait_on();         // level-sensitive: returns at once if already on
          req.status = BUS_ABORTED;
        end
      join_any
      disable fork;
    end join
    if (req.status == BUS_ABORTED) drive_idle();   // never leave a request on the bus
  end
  seq_item_port.item_done();             // every get_next_item() gets its item_done()
end
```

Four details make it safe:
- **The event is a level, not a pulse.** `cpu_offline` is a `uvm_event` that the PMU driver turns on with `trigger()` when it raises isolation and off with `reset()` when it releases it. `wait_on()` returns at once if the event is already on (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §10.1.1.2.2); `wait_trigger()` would miss a trigger that happened earlier in the same time slot and wait for ever (§10.1.1.2.4). [A-UVM-8](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-8_Multi_Agent_Topologies/index) owns the three waits in depth.
- **The guard fork.** `disable fork` kills every descendant of the calling process; the outer `fork begin ... end join` makes the two racing branches the only descendants (IEEE 1800-2023 §9.6.3).
- **Idle the pins after an abort.** Isolation protects PD_TOP from PD_CPU's outputs; nothing protects PD_CPU's inputs from a request the driver left high. A wake-up must not find `req_valid` already 1.
- **Always `item_done()`.** The sequence that sent the item is waiting in `finish_item()`, which returns only when the driver calls `item_done()`. The sequence then reads `req.status` and decides whether to stop or skip.

### Stopping stimulus: quiesce, flags, kill() and stop_sequences()

The driver abort handles the item that is in the driver. Something must also stop the **sequences** that would keep sending items. These are your options, from the gentlest:

| Mechanism | What it does | Use it when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiesce: let `start()` return before power-down | the virtual sequence finishes its traffic, then starts the PMU actions | planned power-down (most tests) | it needs the virtual sequence to own both the traffic and the power events |
| Cooperative flag: the sequence checks `cpu_offline.is_on()` (or `req.status`) between items and returns | the sequence ends itself at an item boundary | long or background sequences | an item already in the driver still needs the driver's abort |
| Driver abort and `item_done()` | releases the waiting sequence with a status | abrupt power loss with a transfer in flight | idle the pins; call `item_done()` on every path |
| `seq.kill()` (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §14.2.5.11) | kills one sequence, and in uvm-core 2020.3.1 also its child sequences | a background sequence with no stop flag | uvm-core does not run `post_body()`/`post_start()` (see Push Further) |
| `sqr.stop_sequences()` (§15.3.2.14) | kills every sequence on that sequencer and removes its queued requests | resetting a sequencer to idle after an abrupt power loss | the driver must not call `item_done()` for the item it holds; see below |
| A callback (`` `uvm_do_callbacks ``) | runs your code at the hook the driver calls, then returns to the driver | changing, dropping or corrupting items around a power event without editing the driver | it cannot end a sequence |

**A callback cannot abort a sequence.** `` `uvm_do_callbacks `` calls each registered callback object's method and returns (uvm-core 2020.3.1, `uvm_callback_defines.svh`); see [A-UVM-5](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-5_UVM_Callbacks/index). Whatever the callback returns, or whatever flag it sets, the sequence that sent the item stays in `finish_item()` until the driver calls `item_done()`. Only `kill()`, `stop_sequences()` or a cooperative flag the sequence itself checks can end it; a callback that calls `kill()` is using `kill()`, not the callback mechanism.

**The ordering rule for `stop_sequences()`.** While the driver holds an item, that item is still in the sequencer's request FIFO; `item_done()` removes it. `stop_sequences()` empties the FIFO (uvm-core `uvm_sequencer.svh`), so a later `item_done()` for the held item finds nothing and is fatal:

```text
UVM_FATAL ... [SQRBADITMDN] Item_done() called with no outstanding requests. Each call to item_done() must be paired with a previous call to get_next_item().
```

So choose one owner per item. Either let the driver finish (or abort) the item and call `item_done()`, then stop the sequences; or tell the driver that the sequences have been stopped, and have it skip `item_done()` for the item it holds:

```systemverilog
// snippet: an abrupt-loss variant of the driver's last line
if (stopped) stopped = 0;                // stop_sequences() already removed this item
else         seq_item_port.item_done();
```

### Objections and phases across power events

An **objection** keeps a phase alive; it does not protect the traffic ([I-UVM-1C](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-UVM-1C_UVM_Phasing/index)). Two questions decide whether a power test ends cleanly.

**Who owns the objection?** In this bench the **test** raises it around `vseq.start()` and drops it afterwards. Nothing that might be stopped holds it. If instead a sequence owns it, how it was raised matters:
- a sequence started with `set_starting_phase(phase)` and `set_automatic_phase_objection(1)` raises the objection before `pre_start()` and drops it after `post_start()` or when it is killed (§14.2.4.1–§14.2.4.4);
- uvm-core 2020.3.1's `kill()` drops that automatic objection, but `stop_sequences()` does not, and neither runs `post_body()`, so an objection dropped there by hand is never dropped (Push Further has the details).

**What happens to a hung power test?** Suppose a sequence in `main_phase` holds an objection and waits for a response that never comes. The run phase starts a global timeout counter (§9.8.1.5). The run phase cannot end while `main_phase` still has its objection, because `uvm_run_phase` and `uvm_post_shutdown_phase` share `uvm_extract_phase` as their successor, and phases that share a successor end together (§9.6). So the test does end, with a fatal error, when the timeout expires: 9200 s of simulated time by default in uvm-core 2020.3.1 (`` `UVM_DEFAULT_TIMEOUT ``). That is far too long for a power test, which is why the bench sets a timeout of 100 µs with `set_timeout` (F.7.3.3).

### Power-aware assertions

The scoreboard cannot see the order of the PMU's controls, so the order is checked with concurrent assertions on the PMU's outputs. They live in `pwr_seq_checker`, a module bound into the PD_CPU model (IEEE 1800-2023 §23.11), so the design source does not change. Three placement rules matter:
- **Clock them on the always-on clock** and disable them during the always-on reset (`default disable iff (!por_n)`, §16.15). A checker clocked by PD_CPU's gated clock stops checking exactly when the power events happen.
- **Read the controls, not the supply.** A supply is a UPF object; reading it from SystemVerilog needs the simulator's UPF package (tool-dependent). The PMU's outputs and the switch's acknowledge are ordinary signals.
- **Write `assert property`.** A `property ... endproperty` declaration on its own checks nothing (§16.12): it must be asserted.

```systemverilog
// snippet: the six assertions of pwr_seq_checker (complete module in Make It Work)
a_iso_before_off: assert property ($fell(cpu_pwr_en) |-> $past(cpu_iso_en))
  else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_iso_before_off: power switched off without isolation")
a_iso_unless_on: assert property (!(cpu_pwr_en && cpu_pwr_ack) |-> cpu_iso_en)
  else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_iso_unless_on: isolation off while not fully powered")
a_save_while_on: assert property (cpu_save |-> cpu_pwr_en && cpu_pwr_ack)
  else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_save_while_on: save while PD_CPU is not powered")
a_restore_while_on: assert property (
  cpu_restore |-> cpu_pwr_en && cpu_pwr_ack && cpu_rst_n)
  else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_restore_while_on: restore while unpowered or in reset")
a_release_after_wake: assert property (
  $fell(cpu_iso_en) |->
    cpu_pwr_ack && cpu_clk_en && cpu_rst_n && reset_seen && restored)
  else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ",
                  "a_release_after_wake: released before power, reset, restore")
a_no_x_into_top: assert property (
  !$isunknown(rsp_valid) && !(rsp_valid && $isunknown(rsp_rdata)))
  else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_no_x_into_top: X reached PD_TOP from PD_CPU")
```

What each one catches:
- `a_iso_before_off`: power removed before isolation was on, the "power off before isolation" preset. `$past` returns the value at the previous clock tick (§16.9.3), so isolation must be on at least one edge before `cpu_pwr_en` falls.
- `a_iso_unless_on`: isolation released while the domain is still off or still ramping up.
- `a_save_while_on`: a save from a dead domain, which saves X.
- `a_restore_while_on`: a restore before power is back, or during the reset, which the reset would wipe.
- `a_release_after_wake`: the release that comes too early. `reset_seen` and `restored` are two flags the checker keeps on the always-on clock: both clear when power is lost; a reset sets `reset_seen` and clears `restored` (a reset wipes an earlier restore); a restore sets `restored`. The release is legal only after power, then reset, then restore.
- `a_no_x_into_top`: any leak, whatever its cause: `rsp_valid` must never be X, and the data must be known whenever `rsp_valid` is 1. This is the catch-all; the others say *which* step was wrong.

The assertions report through `` `uvm_error ``, so a failure counts in the UVM report summary and fails the test, like any other check in the bench.

### Power-sequence coverage

Assertions say that nothing illegal happened; coverage says that the legal things you care about did happen. For power, that means **transitions**, not just states. The coverage collector samples once per power event, so idle cycles are never counted:

```systemverilog
// snippet: pwr_cov's covergroup (complete class in Make It Work)
covergroup cg_pwr with function sample(pwr_op_e op);
  cp_op: coverpoint op {
    ignore_bins hold = {HOLD};             // a wait, not a pin change: never published
  }
  cp_down: coverpoint op {
    bins down_order = (CLK_OFF => ISO_ON => SAVE => PWR_OFF);
  }
  cp_up: coverpoint op {
    bins up_order = (PWR_ON => RST => RESTORE => CLK_ON => ISO_OFF);
    illegal_bins release_unreset = (PWR_ON => ISO_OFF);   // power-on straight to release
    illegal_bins release_while_off = (PWR_OFF => ISO_OFF);
  }
endgroup
```

- `with function sample(...)` defines what one sample is (IEEE 1800-2023 §19.8.1); here, one power event.
- A **transition bin** such as `(PWR_ON => RST => ...)` is hit when consecutive samples follow that sequence (§19.5.2). `cp_op` has only an `ignore_bins` (§19.5.5), so it gets automatic bins for every other value (§19.5.3).
- An **illegal bin** reports a run-time error if it is ever hit (§19.5.6); the form of that error is tool-dependent.

Traced through the Make It Work run, the published stream is RST (the power-on reset), CLK_OFF, ISO_ON, SAVE, PWR_OFF, PWR_ON, RST, RESTORE, CLK_ON, ISO_OFF. `cp_op` hits all nine bins, `down_order` and `up_order` are each hit once, and no illegal bin is hit: 100.00%.

**Cross traffic with power only where it is legal.** A cross of "every bus operation" with "every power state" makes "a write while PD_CPU is off" a coverage goal, which is traffic to a dead domain. Exclude or forbid those cells:

```systemverilog
// snippet: traffic crossed with the power state, legal combinations only
// PD_PARKED: clock off and isolated, but still powered
typedef enum {PD_RUN, PD_PARKED, PD_OFF} pd_state_e;
covergroup cg_traffic with function sample(bit write, bit addr, pd_state_e st);
  cp_kind:  coverpoint {write, addr};      // the four operations
  cp_state: coverpoint st;
  x_kind_state: cross cp_kind, cp_state {
    ignore_bins  parked = binsof(cp_state) intersect {PD_PARKED};   // not a goal
    illegal_bins off    = binsof(cp_state) intersect {PD_OFF};      // must never happen
  }
endgroup
```

`ignore_bins` and `illegal_bins` on a cross are IEEE 1800-2023 §19.6.2 and §19.6.3. The power-aware coverage of a whole SoC, with dozens of domains, is better generated from the UPF power states than written by hand; see Push Further.

### Pitfalls and misconceptions

| Misconception or pitfall | Why it is wrong | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Powered-down outputs read 0." | Only isolated outputs read the clamp; unisolated 4-state outputs read X, and 2-state ones may read 0 and hide the bug. | Isolate every output of a switchable domain; keep boundary signals 4-state. |
| "A callback can abort the running sequence." | A callback runs where the driver calls it and returns to the driver; the sequence stays in `finish_item()`. | Abort in the driver, call `item_done()`, and stop sequences with a flag, `kill()` or `stop_sequences()`. |
| "The scoreboard can keep comparing through a power cycle." | Dropped transfers stay pending, and the model expects pre-power values from registers that were reset. | Pause and flush (with a count) at isolation; set the expected values at the release. |
| "Power coming back restores the state." | Power returning restores nothing: the state is X until a reset or a restore. | PWR_ON, then RST, then RESTORE, then release. |
| Reset after the restore | The reset overwrites the restored value. | Finish the reset, then restore. |
| A property declared but not asserted | A property declaration on its own produces no result (§16.12). | `label: assert property (...)`. |
| Assertions clocked by the gated clock | They stop exactly when power events happen. | Clock them on the always-on clock; disable them on the always-on reset. |
| A synchronous domain reset pulsed with the clock stopped | It never takes effect; the non-retained state stays X. | Use an asynchronous domain reset, or enable the clock for the reset window. |
| `wait_trigger()` on the power event | It misses a trigger earlier in the same time slot and waits for ever. | Keep the event on while the domain is off and use `wait_on()`. |
| `item_done()` after `stop_sequences()` | The request FIFO was flushed: `UVM_FATAL [SQRBADITMDN]`. | One owner per item: finish it first, or skip `item_done()` for it. |
| An objection raised in `pre_body()` and dropped in `post_body()` of a sequence that may be killed | uvm-core does not run `post_body()` on a killed sequence; the phase runs to the timeout. | Let the test own the objection, or use the automatic objection with `kill()`. |

### Recap

- During a power cycle, isolated outputs read the clamp, unisolated ones read X, and inside PD_CPU everything is X until the reset; only retained registers get their value back, at the restore.
- The safe order is CLK_OFF, ISO_ON, SAVE, PWR_OFF, then PWR_ON (wait for the acknowledge), RST, RESTORE, CLK_ON, ISO_OFF, and the virtual sequence quiesces the traffic before it starts.
- The scoreboard pauses and flushes at isolation, counts what it drops, and expects the specification's values at the release. Assertions on the always-on clock check the order. Coverage records the transitions.
- A driver that waits for PD_CPU races the response against a level-sensitive power event, idles the bus, and always calls `item_done()`. Callbacks cannot stop sequences; flags, `kill()` and `stop_sequences()` can, each with its own rules.
- The test owns the objection, and a short per-test timeout turns a hang into a quick, traceable failure.

## Make It Work

### The complete bench, file by file

The blocks below are one compile-ready set: five compile units (`cpu_pd_model.sv`, `pwr_if.sv`, `pwr_seq_checker.sv`, `pwr_pkg.sv` with its five included files, and `tb_top.sv`). The PD_CPU model makes the bench runnable without power-aware tools; with real UPF, you would replace it by the RTL and load the UPF, and the UVM side would not change. The code has been checked by reading against IEEE 1800-2023 and uvm-core 2020.3.1; no simulator run is behind this page.

**1. The PD_CPU model.** Corruption, isolation, retention and the power switch, written out:

```systemverilog
// compile: cpu_pd_model.sv (file 1 of 5)
// A behavioural stand-in for "PD_CPU's RTL plus its UPF in a power-aware simulator".
// With real UPF the simulator adds the corruption, isolation, retention and switch
// behaviour to unchanged RTL; here it is written out, so any 4-state simulator shows it.
module cpu_pd_model #(
  parameter int unsigned ACK_DELAY = 3   // switch acknowledge latency, cycles (>= 1)
) (
  input  logic       clk,                // always-on clock
  input  logic       por_n,              // always-on power-on reset, active low
  input  logic       cpu_clk_en,         // 0: PD_CPU's clock is stopped
  input  logic       cpu_iso_en,         // 1: PD_CPU's outputs are clamped to 0
  input  logic       cpu_save,           // 1 at an edge: save ctx_reg (retention)
  input  logic       cpu_restore,        // 1 at an edge: copy it back into ctx_reg
  input  logic       cpu_pwr_en,         // power-switch control: 1 = VDD_CPU on
  output logic       cpu_pwr_ack,        // the switch's acknowledge: 1 = VDD_CPU is on
  input  logic       cpu_rst_n,          // PD_CPU reset, asynchronous, active low
  input  logic       req_valid,          // a request from PD_TOP this cycle
  input  logic       req_write,          // 1: write, 0: read
  input  logic       req_addr,           // 0: CTX (retained), 1: SCRATCH (not retained)
  input  logic [7:0] req_wdata,
  output logic       rsp_valid,          // the response PD_TOP sees (after isolation)
  output logic [7:0] rsp_rdata
);
  timeunit 1ns; timeprecision 1ps;

  logic [7:0]  ctx_reg, scratch;         // PD_CPU's state
  logic [7:0]  ret_ctx;                  // ctx_reg's retention element (always-on supply)
  logic        cpu_rsp_valid;            // PD_CPU's outputs, before the isolation cells
  logic [7:0]  cpu_rsp_rdata;
  int unsigned ack_count;

  // Power switch. The acknowledge follows cpu_pwr_en ACK_DELAY cycles later, and
  // VDD_CPU counts as on exactly while the acknowledge is 1.
  always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge por_n) begin
    if (!por_n) begin
      cpu_pwr_ack <= 1'b1;               // PD_CPU is powered at power-on
      ack_count   <= 0;
    end else if (cpu_pwr_ack != cpu_pwr_en) begin
      if (ack_count == ACK_DELAY - 1) begin
        cpu_pwr_ack <= cpu_pwr_en;
        ack_count   <= 0;
      end else begin
        ack_count <= ack_count + 1;
      end
    end else begin
      ack_count <= 0;
    end
  end

  // PD_CPU's registers. The reset is asynchronous, so it works while the clock is
  // stopped. Save and restore act at an always-on clock edge (this model's convention).
  always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge cpu_rst_n or negedge cpu_pwr_ack) begin
    if (!cpu_pwr_ack) begin              // VDD_CPU off: the state is corrupted
      ctx_reg       <= 'x;
      scratch       <= 'x;
      cpu_rsp_valid <= 1'bx;
      cpu_rsp_rdata <= 'x;
    end else if (!cpu_rst_n) begin       // powered and in reset
      ctx_reg       <= '0;
      scratch       <= '0;
      cpu_rsp_valid <= 1'b0;
      cpu_rsp_rdata <= '0;
    end else if (cpu_restore) begin      // only the retained register comes back
      ctx_reg <= ret_ctx;
    end else if (cpu_clk_en) begin       // normal operation, one request per cycle
      cpu_rsp_valid <= req_valid;
      if (req_valid && req_write) begin
        if (req_addr) scratch <= req_wdata;
        else          ctx_reg <= req_wdata;
        cpu_rsp_rdata <= req_wdata;      // a write's response echoes the value written
      end else if (req_valid) begin
        cpu_rsp_rdata <= req_addr ? scratch : ctx_reg;
      end
    end
  end

  // The retention element is on the always-on supply. A save from a powered-down
  // domain copies X: the real value is already gone.
  always_ff @(posedge clk) begin
    if (cpu_save) ret_ctx <= ctx_reg;
  end

  // Isolation cells, on the always-on supply: clamp to 0 while cpu_iso_en is 1.
  assign rsp_valid = cpu_iso_en ? 1'b0  : cpu_rsp_valid;
  assign rsp_rdata = cpu_iso_en ? 8'h00 : cpu_rsp_rdata;
endmodule
```

**2. The interface.** The PMU controls start at their power-on values (powered, clocked, not isolated, PD_CPU held in reset); the PMU agent releases the reset. Declaration initializers take effect before any process starts, so there is no race at time 0.

```systemverilog
// compile: pwr_if.sv (file 2 of 5)
interface pwr_if (input logic clk);
  timeunit 1ns; timeprecision 1ps;

  logic       por_n;                     // always-on power-on reset, driven by tb_top
  logic       cpu_clk_en  = 1'b1;        // PMU controls, at their power-on values
```

```systemverilog
// Power-sequencing assertions. They watch the PMU's controls on the always-on clock,
// so they keep working while PD_CPU is off, and they report through UVM.
module pwr_seq_checker (
  input logic       clk,                 // always-on clock
  input logic       por_n,               // always-on reset
  input logic       cpu_clk_en,
  input logic       cpu_iso_en,
  input logic       cpu_save,
  input logic       cpu_restore,
  input logic       cpu_pwr_en,
  input logic       cpu_pwr_ack,
  input logic       cpu_rst_n,
  input logic       rsp_valid,           // PD_CPU's outputs as PD_TOP sees them
  input logic [7:0] rsp_rdata
);
  timeunit 1ns; timeprecision 1ps;
  import uvm_pkg::*;
  `include "uvm_macros.svh"

  // Since power last returned: has PD_CPU been reset, and restored after that reset?
  logic reset_seen, restored;
  always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge por_n) begin
    if (!por_n) begin
      reset_seen <= 1'b1;                // the power-on reset counts; nothing to restore
      restored   <= 1'b1;
    end else if (!cpu_pwr_ack) begin
      reset_seen <= 1'b0;                // power lost: both are needed again
      restored   <= 1'b0;
    end else if (!cpu_rst_n) begin
      reset_seen <= 1'b1;
      restored   <= 1'b0;                // a reset wipes a restore that came before it
    end else if (cpu_restore) begin
      restored   <= 1'b1;
    end
  end

  default clocking @(posedge clk); endclocking
  default disable iff (!por_n);

  a_iso_before_off: assert property ($fell(cpu_pwr_en) |-> $past(cpu_iso_en))
    else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_iso_before_off: power switched off without isolation")
  a_iso_unless_on: assert property (!(cpu_pwr_en && cpu_pwr_ack) |-> cpu_iso_en)
    else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_iso_unless_on: isolation off while not fully powered")
  a_save_while_on: assert property (cpu_save |-> cpu_pwr_en && cpu_pwr_ack)
    else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_save_while_on: save while PD_CPU is not powered")
  a_restore_while_on: assert property (
    cpu_restore |-> cpu_pwr_en && cpu_pwr_ack && cpu_rst_n)
    else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_restore_while_on: restore while unpowered or in reset")
  a_release_after_wake: assert property (
    $fell(cpu_iso_en) |->
      cpu_pwr_ack && cpu_clk_en && cpu_rst_n && reset_seen && restored)
    else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ",
                    "a_release_after_wake: released before power, reset, restore")
  a_no_x_into_top: assert property (
    !$isunknown(rsp_valid) && !(rsp_valid && $isunknown(rsp_rdata)))
    else `uvm_error("PWR_SEQ", "a_no_x_into_top: X reached PD_TOP from PD_CPU")
endmodule
```

```systemverilog
class bus_drv extends uvm_driver #(bus_item);
  `uvm_component_utils(bus_drv)
  virtual pwr_if vif;
  uvm_event      cpu_offline;            // on while PD_CPU is isolated (set by the env)

  function new(string name, uvm_component parent);
    super.new(name, parent);
  endfunction

  task run_phase(uvm_phase phase);
    @(vif.drv_cb iff vif.drv_cb.cpu_rst_n === 1'b1);   // PD_CPU out of its power-on reset
    forever begin
      seq_item_port.get_next_item(req);
      if (cpu_offline.is_on()) begin
        req.status = BUS_DROPPED;        // PD_CPU is isolated: do not touch its pins
      end else begin
        fork begin                       // a guard: disable fork kills only these two
          fork
            drive_and_wait_rsp(req);
            begin
              cpu_offline.wait_on();     // level-sensitive: returns at once if already on
              req.status = BUS_ABORTED;
            end
          join_any
          disable fork;
        end join
        if (req.status == BUS_ABORTED) drive_idle();
      end
      seq_item_port.item_done();         // every get_next_item() gets its item_done()
    end
  endtask

  // One request, then its response, which is the driver's acknowledge.
  task drive_and_wait_rsp(bus_item it);
    @(vif.drv_cb);
    vif.drv_cb.req_valid <= 1'b1;
    vif.drv_cb.req_write <= it.write;
    vif.drv_cb.req_addr  <= it.addr;
    vif.drv_cb.req_wdata <= it.wdata;
    @(vif.drv_cb);
    vif.drv_cb.req_valid <= 1'b0;        // a request lasts one cycle
    @(vif.drv_cb iff vif.drv_cb.rsp_valid === 1'b1);   // blocks for ever if PD_CPU is off
    it.rdata  = vif.drv_cb.rsp_rdata;
    it.status = BUS_OK;
  endtask

  task drive_idle();                     // a wake-up must not find req_valid already 1
    vif.drv_cb.req_valid <= 1'b0;
  endtask
endclass

class pmu_drv extends uvm_driver #(pwr_item);
  `uvm_component_utils(pmu_drv)
  virtual pwr_if vif;
  uvm_event      cpu_offline;            // turned on with isolation, off with the release

  function new(string name, uvm_component parent);
    super.new(name, parent);
  endfunction

  task run_phase(uvm_phase phase);
    @(vif.pmu_cb iff vif.pmu_cb.por_n === 1'b1);
    vif.pmu_cb.cpu_rst_n <= 1'b1;        // power-on: release PD_CPU's reset
    forever begin
      seq_item_port.get_next_item(req);
      `uvm_info("PMU", req.convert2string(), UVM_HIGH)
      do_op(req);
      seq_item_port.item_done();
    end
  endtask

  // Every action starts at a clock edge, so two actions never share a cycle.
  task do_op(pwr_item it);
    @(vif.pmu_cb);
    case (it.op)
      CLK_OFF: vif.pmu_cb.cpu_clk_en <= 1'b0;
      CLK_ON:  vif.pmu_cb.cpu_clk_en <= 1'b1;
      ISO_ON: begin
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_iso_en <= 1'b1;
        cpu_offline.trigger();           // from now on the bus cannot reach PD_CPU
      end
      ISO_OFF: begin
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_iso_en <= 1'b0;
        cpu_offline.reset();             // before item_done(): traffic may resume next
      end
      SAVE: begin
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_save <= 1'b1;
        @(vif.pmu_cb) vif.pmu_cb.cpu_save <= 1'b0;         // a one-cycle pulse
      end
      RESTORE: begin
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_restore <= 1'b1;
        @(vif.pmu_cb) vif.pmu_cb.cpu_restore <= 1'b0;
      end
      RST: begin
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_rst_n <= 1'b0;
        repeat (2) @(vif.pmu_cb);
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_rst_n <= 1'b1;
      end
      PWR_OFF: begin
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_pwr_en <= 1'b0;
        @(vif.pmu_cb iff vif.pmu_cb.cpu_pwr_ack === 1'b0); // the switch confirms: off
      end
      PWR_ON: begin
        vif.pmu_cb.cpu_pwr_en <= 1'b1;
        // never reset or restore before the switch confirms: on
        @(vif.pmu_cb iff vif.pmu_cb.cpu_pwr_ack === 1'b1);
      end
      HOLD: repeat (it.cycles) @(vif.pmu_cb);
    endcase
  endtask
endclass

class pmu_mon extends uvm_monitor;
  `uvm_component_utils(pmu_mon)
  virtual pwr_if vif;
  uvm_analysis_port #(pwr_item) ap;      // one new pwr_item per change of the controls

  function new(string name, uvm_component parent);
    super.new(name, parent);
    ap = new("ap", this);
  endfunction

  task run_phase(uvm_phase phase);
    logic clk_en, iso, save, restore, ack, rst_n;   // values at the previous edge
    @(vif.mon_cb);
    {clk_en, iso, save} = {vif.mon_cb.cpu_clk_en, vif.mon_cb.cpu_iso_en,
                           vif.mon_cb.cpu_save};
    {restore, ack, rst_n} = {vif.mon_cb.cpu_restore, vif.mon_cb.cpu_pwr_ack,
                             vif.mon_cb.cpu_rst_n};
    forever begin
      @(vif.mon_cb);
      if ( clk_en  && !vif.mon_cb.cpu_clk_en)  publish(CLK_OFF);
      if (!iso     &&  vif.mon_cb.cpu_iso_en)  publish(ISO_ON);
      if (!save    &&  vif.mon_cb.cpu_save)    publish(SAVE);
      if ( ack     && !vif.mon_cb.cpu_pwr_ack) publish(PWR_OFF);   // the switch says: off
      if (!ack     &&  vif.mon_cb.cpu_pwr_ack) publish(PWR_ON);
      if (!rst_n   &&  vif.mon_cb.cpu_rst_n)   publish(RST);       // the reset has ended
      if (!restore &&  vif.mon_cb.cpu_restore) publish(RESTORE);
      if (!clk_en  &&  vif.mon_cb.cpu_clk_en)  publish(CLK_ON);
      if ( iso     && !vif.mon_cb.cpu_iso_en)  publish(ISO_OFF);
      {clk_en, iso, save} = {vif.mon_cb.cpu_clk_en, vif.mon_cb.cpu_iso_en,
                             vif.mon_cb.cpu_save};
      {restore, ack, rst_n} = {vif.mon_cb.cpu_restore, vif.mon_cb.cpu_pwr_ack,
                               vif.mon_cb.cpu_rst_n};
    end
  endtask

  function void publish(pwr_op_e op);
    pwr_item t = pwr_item::type_id::create("pwr");
    t.op = op;
    ap.write(t);
  endfunction
```

```systemverilog
class pwr_scoreboard extends uvm_scoreboard;
  `uvm_component_utils(pwr_scoreboard)
  uvm_analysis_imp_req #(bus_item, pwr_scoreboard) req_export;
  uvm_analysis_imp_rsp #(bus_item, pwr_scoreboard) rsp_export;
  uvm_analysis_imp_pwr #(pwr_item, pwr_scoreboard) pwr_export;

  logic [7:0]  m_ctx, m_scratch;         // what a correct PD_CPU holds (the spec)
  logic [7:0]  m_saved;                  // CTX at isolation: what retention brings back
  logic [7:0]  exp_q[$];                 // expected response data, oldest first
  bit          comparing = 1;
  bit          lost_power;               // PD_CPU lost power since isolation went on
  int unsigned matches, mismatches, dropped_by_power;

  function new(string name, uvm_component parent);
    super.new(name, parent);
    req_export = new("req_export", this);
    rsp_export = new("rsp_export", this);
    pwr_export = new("pwr_export", this);
    m_ctx      = 8'h00;                  // reset values after the power-on reset
    m_scratch  = 8'h00;
  endfunction

  // A request: predict the response a correct PD_CPU gives, and remember it.
  function void write_req(bus_item t);
    if (!comparing) begin
      `uvm_error("SCB_OFFLINE",
                 {"request while PD_CPU is isolated: ", t.convert2string()})
      return;
    end
    if (t.write) begin
      if (t.addr) m_scratch = t.wdata;
      else        m_ctx     = t.wdata;
      exp_q.push_back(t.wdata);          // a write's response echoes the value written
    end else begin
      exp_q.push_back(t.addr ? m_scratch : m_ctx);
    end
  endfunction

  function void write_rsp(bus_item t);
    logic [7:0] want;
    if (exp_q.size() == 0) begin
      `uvm_error("SCB_UNEXPECTED", $sformatf("response 0x%02h with no request", t.rdata))
      return;
    end
    want = exp_q.pop_front();
    if ($isunknown(t.rdata) || t.rdata !== want) begin   // X never matches
      mismatches++;
      `uvm_error("SCB_MISMATCH",
                 $sformatf("expected 0x%02h observed 0x%02h", want, t.rdata))
    end else begin
      matches++;
    end
  endfunction

  // Power events: pause while the boundary is closed; say what a correct wake-up gives.
  function void write_pwr(pwr_item t);
    case (t.op)
      ISO_ON: begin
        int unsigned n;
        n = exp_q.size();
        dropped_by_power += n;           // responses that can no longer reach PD_TOP
        exp_q.delete();
        comparing = 0;
        m_saved   = m_ctx;
        `uvm_info("SCB_PWR", $sformatf(
          "isolation on: comparing paused, %0d expectation(s) dropped", n), UVM_LOW)
      end
      PWR_OFF: lost_power = 1;
      ISO_OFF: begin
        if (lost_power) begin
          m_ctx      = m_saved;          // retained: the value at power-down
          m_scratch  = 8'h00;            // not retained: the reset value
          lost_power = 0;
        end
        comparing = 1;
        `uvm_info("SCB_PWR", $sformatf(
          "isolation off: comparing resumed, expecting CTX=0x%02h SCRATCH=0x%02h",
          m_ctx, m_scratch), UVM_LOW)
      end
      default: ;                         // the order of the steps: pwr_seq_checker
    endcase
  endfunction

  function void check_phase(uvm_phase phase);
    if (exp_q.size() != 0)
      `uvm_error("SCB_PENDING", $sformatf("%0d response(s) never arrived", exp_q.size()))
    if (matches == 0)
      `uvm_error("SCB_NO_TRAFFIC", "no response was checked")
  endfunction

  function void report_phase(uvm_phase phase);
    `uvm_info("SCB_SUMMARY", $sformatf(
      "SCB_SUMMARY matches=%0d mismatches=%0d pending=%0d dropped_by_power=%0d",
      matches, mismatches, exp_q.size(), dropped_by_power), UVM_NONE)
  endfunction
endclass
```

```systemverilog
class bus_seq extends uvm_sequence #(bus_item);
  `uvm_object_utils(bus_seq)
  bus_mode_e   mode = BUS_RANDOM;
  int unsigned n = 4;                    // BUS_RANDOM only: how many items

  function new(string name = "bus_seq");
    super.new(name);
  endfunction

  task body();
    int unsigned count;
    count = (mode == BUS_RANDOM) ? n : 2;
    for (int unsigned i = 0; i < count; i++) begin
      req = bus_item::type_id::create($sformatf("req%0d", i));
      start_item(req);
      case (mode)
        BUS_INIT: begin                  // write CTX = 8'hA5, then SCRATCH = 8'h3C
          req.write = 1'b1;
          req.addr  = i[0];
          req.wdata = (i == 0) ? 8'hA5 : 8'h3C;
        end
        BUS_CHECK: begin                 // read CTX, then SCRATCH
          req.write = 1'b0;
          req.addr  = i[0];
          req.wdata = 8'h00;
        end
        default: if (!req.randomize()) `uvm_fatal("RAND", "bus_item randomization failed")
      endcase
      finish_item(req);                  // returns after the driver's item_done()
    end
  endtask
endclass

class pmu_seq extends uvm_sequence #(pwr_item);
  `uvm_object_utils(pmu_seq)
  pwr_op_e     ops[$];                   // the PMU actions, in order
  int unsigned hold_cycles = 1;          // used by HOLD

  function new(string name = "pmu_seq");
    super.new(name);
  endfunction

  task body();
    foreach (ops[i]) begin
      req = pwr_item::type_id::create($sformatf("op%0d", i));
      start_item(req);
      req.op     = ops[i];
```

### Run it

The steps are tool-neutral; switches differ between simulators.

1. **Point the compiler at uvm-core 2020.3.1**, either your simulator's built-in UVM library or `src/uvm_pkg.sv` with `src` on the include path (plus `src/dpi/uvm_dpi.cc`, or define `UVM_NO_DPI`).
2. **Use one time unit everywhere.** Every file declares `timeunit 1ns; timeprecision 1ps;`, but `uvm_pkg` declares none, so give the compile a default time unit and precision of 1 ns / 1 ps with your tool's switch. Then `set_drain_time(this, 20ns)` and `set_timeout(100us)` mean what they say ([I-UVM-3C](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-UVM-3C_First_Complete_Testbench/index) explains why).
3. **Compile in order** with the bench's directory on the include path: `cpu_pd_model.sv`, `pwr_if.sv`, `pwr_seq_checker.sv`, `pwr_pkg.sv`, `tb_top.sv`. Elaborate with `tb_top` as the top module; the checker needs assertion support enabled if your tool makes it optional.
4. **Run** `pwr_cycle_test` (it is also the `run_test()` default). Add your tool's seed switch to vary `hold_cycles`.

The bench needs no power-aware simulator, because the model does the corruption. To run the same UVM environment on the real RTL with its UPF, replace `cpu_pd_model` by the RTL, load the UPF with your simulator's power-aware option, and keep everything else; the switches are tool-dependent.

### How to build your own power-aware bench

1. **List the power domains and their controls** from the UPF and the PMU specification: switch, acknowledge, isolation, save, restore, clock enable, reset. Write down the power-down and wake-up orders.
2. **Add a PMU agent** whose driver performs one action per item, starting each at a clock edge and waiting for the switch's acknowledge, and whose monitor publishes one item per change of the controls.
3. **Give every driver that waits for a switchable domain an abort path:** a level-sensitive power event, an idle drive, and `item_done()` on every path.
4. **Write the power-cycle virtual sequence:** quiesce, power down, hold for a random time, wake up in order, check retained and reset values, and resume traffic.
5. **Make the scoreboard power-aware:** pause and flush with a count at isolation, set the specification's values at the release, and fail on pending items and on zero checks.
6. **Bind the sequencing assertions** to the PMU's outputs on the always-on clock, reporting through UVM.
7. **Cover the transitions,** with illegal bins for the orders that must never happen.
8. **Let the test own the objection** and set a short timeout.
9. **Mutate the order** (skip the reset, swap reset and restore, release early) and confirm that each mutation fails.

**Checklist before moving on:**
- [ ] Every driver pairs `get_next_item()` with `item_done()` on every path, including aborts, unless `stop_sequences()` already removed the item.
- [ ] The power event is level-sensitive (`wait_on()`), and it is reset before traffic resumes.
- [ ] The scoreboard pauses and flushes at isolation, counts what it drops, and expects reset and retained values after the release.
- [ ] `check_phase` fails on pending expectations and on a run that checked nothing.
- [ ] The six sequencing assertions are bound, clocked on the always-on clock, and report through UVM.
- [ ] Transition coverage closes, and no illegal bin is hit.
- [ ] The test owns the objection, and the timeout is set per test.
- [ ] A wake-up without reset, and a restore before reset, both make the bench fail.

## Push Further

### Expert: the global timeout and hung power tests

- **The model.** The run phase starts a global timeout counter; if it expires before the run phase ends, a fatal error results (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §9.8.1.5). `uvm_root::set_timeout()` sets it (F.7.3.3; §9.8.1.5 cites it as F.7.2.3, which is a cross-reference error in the standard). Because `uvm_run_phase` and `uvm_post_shutdown_phase` share `uvm_extract_phase` as successor (§9.6), the run phase cannot end while any run-time phase still has an objection, so a hang in `main_phase` is covered. In uvm-core 2020.3.1 the watchdog is forked only for the run phase (`uvm_phase_hopper.svh`), and `` `UVM_DEFAULT_TIMEOUT `` is 9200 s (`uvm_global_defines.svh`).
- **Who wins.** `set_timeout(timeout, overridable = 1)`: a call with `overridable = 0` locks the value. uvm-core applies `+UVM_TIMEOUT` in `uvm_root::build_phase()` (`uvm_root.svh`; uvm-core implementation, not in IEEE 1800.2 Annex G), which runs before the test's `build_phase()`. So a `set_timeout()` in the test overrides the command line, and one before `run_test()` does not.
- **Triage.** `+UVM_OBJECTION_TRACE` prints every raise and drop; `+UVM_PHASE_TRACE` makes uvm-core print the objections still outstanding when the timeout fires (uvm-core implementation). In a power test, the usual culprit is a sequence blocked in `finish_item()` behind a driver waiting for a dead domain. [E-DBG-1](/curriculum/T4_Expert/E-DBG-1_Advanced_UVM_Debug_Methodologies/index) owns hang triage in general.

### Expert: kill(), stop_sequences() and the objections they leave behind

- **The standard and the implementation differ.** IEEE 1800.2-2020 §14.2.5.11 says `kill()` sets the state to `UVM_STOPPED` and that `post_body()` and `post_start()` are executed. uvm-core 2020.3.1's own comment on `kill()` says they "will not be executed", and its code kills the sequence's process without running them (`uvm_sequence_base.svh`). Label any reliance on either behaviour.
- **Automatic objections.** §14.2.4.3 says the automatic objection is dropped after `post_start()` or when `kill()` is called. uvm-core's `kill()` does drop it. `stop_sequences()` (§15.3.2.14), which §14.2.5.12 says "effectively calls sequence.kill", reaches the sequences through the sequencer's `kill_sequence()`, which calls the internal `m_kill()` and never drops the automatic objection (`uvm_sequencer_base.svh`, `uvm_sequence_base.svh`). A top-level sequence started with `set_automatic_phase_objection(1)` and then stopped by `stop_sequences()` therefore holds its phase open until the timeout. This is uvm-core 2020.3.1 behaviour.
- **What to do.** Own the objection outside anything that may be stopped (the test, as on this page), or `kill()` a sequence whose objection is automatic. Put cleanup that must run on a kill in `do_kill()` (§14.2.5.12), which both paths call. A sequence started with `start()` has a starting phase only if you call `set_starting_phase()` (§14.2.4.1, §14.2.4.2); without one, the automatic objection raises nothing.

### Expert: in-phase power cycles or phase jumping

This page runs the whole power cycle inside one phase, and resets PD_CPU through the PMU agent. The alternative is to model a wake-up as a return to `reset_phase`: `uvm_phase::jump()` (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §9.3.1.9.1) or `uvm_domain::jump()` (§9.4.2.4) moves the run-time phases back, so every component's `reset_phase` runs again.
- **For jumping:** components already written to re-initialize in `reset_phase` (flush, reload configuration) do so for free, and per-domain UVM domains can model independently switched blocks.
- **Against:** every component in the domain must be re-entrant, objections and running sequences of the abandoned phases must be cleaned up, and jumps are hard to debug. Most teams keep power cycles in-phase and reserve jumps for full-chip reset tests.
- **Holding a phase open.** `phase_ready_to_end()` (§13.1.4.3.2) lets a component, such as a scoreboard waiting for a wake-up to finish, raise an objection again when the phase is about to end. Reset handling in general is the subject of [A-UVM-9](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-9_Reset_and_Error_Injection/index).

### Expert: who announces the power event

On this page the PMU driver turns `cpu_offline` on and off itself, in the same thread that drives isolation, and before its `item_done()`. The order is then guaranteed: the virtual sequence cannot resume traffic before the event is off. At SoC level the real PMU decides when to isolate, and the testbench can only *observe* it, through a monitor:
- A monitor sees the change at the next clock edge, a cycle late, and its `trigger()` can race with a driver that starts waiting in the same time slot. `wait_on()` (§10.1.1.2.2) closes that race for a level that stays on; `wait_ptrigger()` (§10.1.1.2.5) only for a trigger within the same time slot. [A-UVM-8](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-8_Multi_Agent_Topologies/index) owns the three waits and the `reset()` pitfalls.
- A sequence that resumes traffic after an observed release must wait for the monitor's event to go off (`wait_off()`, §10.1.1.2.3), not for its own PMU write to complete.
- An analysis port carrying `pwr_item` transactions is ordered and carries data (which domain, which state); an event carries only on or off. Large environments publish transactions and derive events from them in one subscriber.

### Expert: assertions and coverage inside switched domains

- **Assertions that live inside PD_CPU** (protocol checkers bound to its ports, for example) see X while it is off. Disable them with `disable iff` on a power-good signal, or switch them off and on with `$assertoff`, `$asserton` and `$assertcontrol` (IEEE 1800-2023 §20.11). IEEE 1801-2018 also defines `sim_assertion_control` (§6.55) for this; what simulators do with it is tool-dependent.
- **Coverage of UPF power states.** IEEE 1801-2018 defines `bind_checker` (§6.12) for attaching checkers and coverage to the power intent, and power states with legal transitions (§6.5 `add_power_state`, §6.7 `add_state_transition`). Several simulators can generate power-state and transition coverage from the UPF; the form is tool-dependent. For an SoC with dozens of domains, generated coverage beats hand-written covergroups.

### Expert: retention corner cases

Each case below is tool-dependent in its details, so check your simulator's power-aware documentation before relying on a particular value:
- **Save while off** saves X (or whatever the corrupted value is); `a_save_while_on` catches it.
- **Restore before power is back** restores into an unpowered register, which stays corrupted; `a_restore_while_on` catches it.
- **Reset and restore together:** which one wins is not something to rely on; keep them in separate cycles, as this page does.
- **The retention supply itself switched off** loses the saved value too.
- **A register not listed in the retention strategy's elements** is not retained, however the RTL names it. When a "retained" register comes back X, check the strategy first.
- **Partial corruption.** IEEE 1801-2018 §4.8 defines simulation states (simstates) between full operation and full corruption, so a domain can be "on" for some behaviour and corrupted for other behaviour. Which simstates a given power state uses comes from the UPF.

### Expert: power-aware gate-level simulation

RTL power-aware simulation inserts *behaviour*; the netlist contains the real isolation, retention and level-shifter cells, the switch network and their control wiring. A power-aware gate-level simulation applies the UPF to the netlist and catches what RTL simulation cannot: a missing or misplaced cell, a cell powered from the wrong supply, a retention flop whose save or restore pin is tied wrongly, and X-optimism in RTL `if` and `case` statements that the gates do not share. Its cost is run time and set-up effort, so teams run a small set of power-cycle tests at gate level. How the simulator maps cell models to UPF strategies is tool-dependent.

### Expert: regressions and the cost of power-aware simulation

- **Which tests run power-aware.** Power-aware simulation is slower than plain RTL simulation (by how much is tool- and design-dependent), so most regressions run most tests without UPF and a dedicated subset with it: every legal transition, random hold times, random acknowledge delays, and power events injected at transaction boundaries and mid-transfer.
- **Inject at both points.** At a transaction boundary, the quiescing path is tested; mid-transfer, the abort, flush and accounting paths are. A regression that only powers down when the bus is idle never exercises the driver's abort.
- **Make the accounting visible.** Report `dropped_by_power` in every summary and track it across the regression: a test whose drops are always zero is not testing abrupt power loss.

### Kata 1 (debug): the wake-up that skipped reset

**Objective 4.** A colleague's power test fails with these lines (abridged), and you have the virtual sequence's wake-up call: `run_pmu({PWR_ON, RESTORE, CLK_ON, ISO_OFF});`.

```text
UVM_ERROR @ 405 ns: reporter [PWR_SEQ] a_release_after_wake: released before power, reset, restore
UVM_INFO  @ 405 ns: uvm_test_top.env.scb [SCB_PWR] isolation off: comparing resumed, expecting CTX=0xa5 SCRATCH=0x00
UVM_ERROR @ 455 ns: reporter [PWR_SEQ] a_no_x_into_top: X reached PD_TOP from PD_CPU
UVM_ERROR @ 455 ns: uvm_test_top.env.scb [SCB_MISMATCH] expected 0x00 observed 0xxx
```

Name the missing step, explain why CTX still read correctly, and say which line of the log you would trust first, and why.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The domain reset is missing: the order should be `PWR_ON, RST, RESTORE, CLK_ON, ISO_OFF`. Power returning restored nothing, so `scratch` stayed X; `ctx_reg` read 8'hA5 because the restore loaded it from the retention element, which does not need a reset. Trust the first error, `a_release_after_wake` at 405 ns: it fires at the release, before any traffic, and it names the step. The two errors at 455 ns are consequences: the first read of SCRATCH returned X, which both the catch-all assertion and the scoreboard report.

</details>

### Kata 2 (write and predict): a light-sleep mode

**Objective 3.** PD_CPU gets a second, cheaper low-power mode, **light sleep**: the PMU stops the clock and isolates, but keeps the power on, so nothing is saved, restored or reset. The PMU actions are `CLK_OFF, ISO_ON, HOLD, CLK_ON, ISO_OFF`.
1. Add a transition bin for the light-sleep entry and exit to `pwr_cov`, without changing the existing bins.
2. Predict, before you trace the code: does `a_release_after_wake` pass at the release? What does the scoreboard expect CTX and SCRATCH to read afterwards?

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

```systemverilog
// snippet: new coverpoint in pwr_cov's cg_pwr
cp_light: coverpoint op {
  bins light_sleep = (CLK_OFF => ISO_ON => CLK_ON => ISO_OFF);  // HOLD: not published
}
```

1. HOLD is not a pin change, so the monitor publishes no item for it, and the transition goes straight from ISO_ON to CLK_ON. A separate coverpoint keeps the light-sleep goal apart from the full power cycle's bins; `cp_op` needs no change.
2. **`a_release_after_wake` passes.** `reset_seen` and `restored` are cleared only when power is lost, and here `cpu_pwr_ack` stays 1, so both still hold their values from the last full wake-up (or from the power-on reset). The release finds power on, the clock on, the reset deasserted and both flags set.
3. **The scoreboard expects the values from before the sleep.** `lost_power` is set only by PWR_OFF, so at ISO_OFF the model is left unchanged: CTX and SCRATCH must read what they held before. A light sleep that corrupted SCRATCH would now be a mismatch, which is the right verdict.

**Reflect:** what would the scoreboard expect if it had copied every event (reset, restore) into its model instead of following the specification? For light sleep, the same; but replay the "restore before reset" bug from quiz question 8 against that design, and see why this page's scoreboard follows the specification.

</details>

### Kata 3 (choose): a brown-out in the middle of a burst

**Objective 5.** A test injects an abrupt power loss on PD_CPU while a background `stream_seq` is sending a long burst on `bus_sqr`; one item is in the driver, waiting for its response. The test raises its own objection and must end cleanly after the wake-up. Choose the stopping mechanism, and list what each component does.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- **Driver:** the abort path on `cpu_offline.wait_on()` ends the transfer with `BUS_ABORTED`, idles the bus, and calls `item_done()`, which releases `stream_seq` from `finish_item()`.
- **Sequence:** either `stream_seq` checks `req.status` (or `cpu_offline.is_on()`) and returns, the cooperative stop; or the virtual sequence calls `stream_seq.kill()`. Do not use a callback: it cannot end the sequence.
- **If you use `stop_sequences()` instead,** call it only after the driver has returned the held item, or have the driver skip `item_done()` for it; otherwise `SQRBADITMDN`. And keep the objection out of `stream_seq`: `stop_sequences()` leaves an automatic objection raised in uvm-core 2020.3.1.
- **Scoreboard:** the in-flight expectation is flushed at ISO_ON and counted, so the summary shows `dropped_by_power=1`, and `pending=0`.

</details>
