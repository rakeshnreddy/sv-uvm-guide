## Quick Take
- **What it is:** The next level of SystemVerilog Assertions: repetition operators, conditions that must hold over a whole interval, per-attempt local variables, and properties that cross clock domains.
- **Why it matters:** Real handshakes wait an unknown number of cycles, hold signals while they wait, and move data across domains. These operators describe that directly, instead of long chains of `##1`.
- **The rule to carry with you:** every operator here still works on attempts that start at each clock tick and on sampled values, exactly as in [I-SV-4A](../I-SV-4A_SVA_Fundamentals/). Attaching assertions to a DUT without editing it (`bind`, checkers) is the topic of I-SV-4C.

## Build Your Mental Model
### Repetition operators
Repetition says how many times a Boolean (or a sequence) must match, and whether the matches must be back to back (§16.9.2):

| Operator | Meaning | Ends |
|---|---|---|
| `b[*3]` | `b` at three consecutive ticks (`b ##1 b ##1 b`) | on the third tick |
| `b[*1:3]` | one to three consecutive ticks | on any of them |
| `b[->2]` | goto: the second occurrence of `b`, with any gap before each | exactly on the tick of the second `b` |
| `b[=2]` | nonconsecutive: two occurrences, then any number of ticks without `b` | on or after the second `b`, before a third |

The standard defines the last two through the first: `b[->N]` is `(!b[*0:$] ##1 b)[*N]`, and `b[=N]` is `b[->N] ##1 !b[*0:$]` (Annex F.3.4.2.3). So `start |=> ack[->2] ##1 done` needs `done` on the tick right after the second `ack`, while `start |=> ack[=2] ##1 done` lets quiet ticks pass between the second `ack` and `done`.

### Conditions over time: throughout and within
- `busy throughout s` matches when `s` matches and `busy` is true at **every** tick of that match, including its last tick. It is shorthand for `(busy)[*0:$] intersect s` (§16.9.9).
- `s1 within s2` matches when `s2` matches and `s1` matches somewhere inside it. It is shorthand for `(1[*0:$] ##1 s1 ##1 1[*0:$]) intersect s2` (§16.9.10).
- `s1 intersect s2` needs both to match with the **same** start and end; `s1 and s2` needs both to match from the same start and ends when the later one ends; `s1 or s2` needs either (§16.9.5–§16.9.7).

A typical handshake rule combines the two families: `req |=> busy throughout done[->1]` says "after a request, busy stays high on every tick until the first done, including the tick where done arrives."

## Make It Work
1. **Exact count, back to back?** Use `[*N]` (or `[*m:n]`).
2. **Wait for the Nth occurrence, then act on the next tick?** Use goto `[->N]`.
3. **N occurrences, then anything quiet before the next step?** Use nonconsecutive `[=N]`.
4. **A level that must hold while something else happens?** Use `throughout`, and remember it also covers the final tick.
5. **Something that must happen inside a window?** Use `within`.
6. **Data that must match later?** Store it in a [local variable](./local-variables) in the antecedent and compare it in the consequent.
7. **Two clocks?** Hop clocks with `@(...)` inside the property and pick `##0` or `##1` deliberately ([multi-clocking](./multi-clocking)).

## Push Further
- **Pending at the end of simulation:** `##[1:$]` and `[->N]` can wait forever. In `assert property` sequences are weak, so an attempt that never finishes is not reported as a failure (§16.12.2). If "must eventually happen" matters, bound the wait (`##[1:16]`) or check for unfinished attempts at the end of the test.
- **Liveness:** `s_eventually` and `strong(...)` make an unfinished attempt a failure. They suit formal tools better than finite simulations.
- **Overlapping transactions:** Local variables give each attempt private storage, which is how one property checks pipelined, overlapping requests.
