## Quick Take

- **What it is:** AXI4 carries every read and write over **five one-way channels**: AW (write address), W (write data) and B (write response) for writes, AR (read address) and R (read data) for reads. Each channel has its own **VALID/READY handshake**: the **source** (the side that sends on that channel) raises VALID when its information is real, the **destination** (the side that receives) raises READY when it can take it, and one item moves at each rising clock edge where both are high (Arm IHI0022E §A3.2.1).
- **Why it matters:** Every AXI driver, responder, monitor and protocol checker you will build sits on these rules, and the classic AXI testbench bugs break one of them: a monitor that assumes the address always comes before the data, an AXI3-style slave that answers before it has accepted the address, a driver that waits for READY before raising VALID and hangs.
- **The Analogy:** In [B-AMBA-2](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-2_Protocol_Intuition_and_Memory_Hooks/index) a handshake was a courier holding a parcel out at a door. AXI has five doors, one per channel, each with its own courier. The courier (VALID) may not pull the parcel back or swap it; the door (READY) opens when it likes; the parcel passes at a clock edge where both are true. *Where it breaks:* a door may open and close again before any courier arrives; one door can pass a new parcel at every edge; three rules link the doors (R after AR, B after the last W, and in AXI4 also B after AW); and a real courier might wait to see an open door, while an AXI courier never may.

**You will be able to:**
1. Predict the edge at which each transfer happens from VALID and READY waveforms (VALID first, READY first, same edge, READY withdrawn, back-to-back), and spot the two handshake violations: VALID dropped before the handshake, and the information changed while VALID is high and READY low (IHI0022E §A3.2.1).
2. Decide whether a cross-channel order is legal: RVALID only after the AR handshake; in AXI4, BVALID only after both the AW handshake and the WLAST handshake; W before, with or after its AW (§A3.3, §A3.3.1).
3. Explain why a source must never wait for READY (deadlock, §A3.3.1), and why master and slave interfaces may have no combinational path from an input to an output (§A3.1.1, §A3.2.1).
4. Trace a complete write and read with backpressure, and the exit from reset, naming the edge of every handshake (§A3.1.2).
5. Write and debug SystemVerilog assertions for handshake stability, reset and the R and B dependencies, and run them on legal traffic in which write data arrives before its address.

The assertions on this page use implication (`|->`, `|=>`), `$stable`, `$rose` and `disable iff`; if any of those is rusty, refresh [I-SV-4A SVA Fundamentals](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-4A_SVA_Fundamentals/index) first.

## Build Your Mental Model

**How to read this lesson.** The core path is the first nine subsections: the words, the five channels, the handshake, the hold rule, why a source never waits, the cross-channel dependencies, independence and backpressure, the two complete flows, and reset. Each concept after the glossary has a picture. Practitioner depth follows: AXI3 versus AXI4, how the rules live in a real testbench, and how to debug them. The expert layer is in Push Further.

### Words you need first

These terms come from the specification this lesson cites, Arm **IHI0022E** (AMBA AXI and ACE Protocol Specification, issue E):

- **Master** and **slave.** The master starts each operation; the slave answers. Later Arm issues call them *manager* and *subordinate*; this track uses the terms of the issue it cites. An **interconnect** is the block that routes masters to slaves; to each master it looks like a slave, and to each slave like a master (§A1.3.2).
- **Interface.** The set of AXI wires between two components. The same interface definition applies master–slave, master–interconnect and interconnect–slave (§A1.3.2), so every rule on this page holds at every hop, not just end to end.
- **Transaction, burst, beat.** The complete operation is the **transaction**; its payload data moves as a **burst**; a burst is made of **transfers**, also called **beats** (§A1.4.2). Burst length and addresses are the subject of [B-AXI-2](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-2_AXI_Burst_Math/index).
- **Channel.** A set of **information signals** (address, data, control, response) plus one VALID and one READY signal (§A1.3.1).
- **Source** and **destination.** On each channel the **source** drives VALID and the information; the **destination** drives READY (§A3.2.1). On AW, W and AR the master is the source; on B and R the slave is.
- **Handshake** (or transfer). One item moving across one channel: it happens at a rising edge where VALID and READY are both high.
- **Clock and sampling.** Every component uses one clock, **ACLK**. "All input signals are sampled on the rising edge of ACLK", and all output changes happen after that edge (§A3.1.1). So a waveform value "at edge k" is the value that settled before rising edge k. **ARESETn** is the single, active-LOW reset (§A3.1.2).
- **Edge numbering.** On this page, edges are numbered from 0, and "edge k" is the k-th rising edge of ACLK; every figure and the visualizer number edges the same way. How to find edge k in a picture:
  - In the timing diagrams and the explorers, edge k is the numbered dashed line in column k.
  - The three waveforms with no numbered edge axis ("one transfer", and the complete write and read) work differently. There, column k holds the value sampled at edge k, and edge k is the rising clock edge at the right-hand end of column k. The rising edge drawn at the far left comes before edge 0, so do not count it.

**Why five channels, not four or six.** A write needs a completion signal of its own: the slave reports the outcome of the whole burst once, on B (§A1.3.1, §A3.4.4). A read needs none: the response rides on R with every beat, so a slave can report `OKAY` for some beats and an error for another (§A3.4.4). And because each channel moves information in one direction only, with no fixed timing relationship to the others, a pipeline register (a **register slice**) can be inserted on any channel at the cost of one cycle of latency (§A1.3.3); Push Further returns to that.

**What the attributes are for.** The ID signals (`AxID`, where "Ax" means "AW or AR") let a master keep several transactions **outstanding** (issued but not yet complete) and let their responses come back out of order; that is [B-AXI-3](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-3_AXI_Ordering_and_IDs/index). `AxCACHE`, `AxPROT`, `AxQOS`, `AxLOCK` and `AxREGION` describe the transaction and are [B-AXI-4](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-4_AXI_Expert_Features_Cache_Atomics/index)'s subject. The `xUSER` signals (x is the channel letter: `AWUSER`, `WUSER`, `BUSER`, `ARUSER`, `RUSER`) are optional, user-defined, and AXI4 only. For this lesson they matter in one way: they are information signals, so the hold rule below covers them too.

**AXI4-Lite** (the register-access subset) keeps all five channels and all five handshake pairs, with every burst one beat long (§B1.1, Table B1-1). Every rule on this page applies to it. Its interface is smaller, though: it has no `WLAST` or `RLAST` (every transfer counts as the last, "equivalent to a WLAST or RLAST value of 1"), no IDs (§B1.1.4), and no attributes beyond `AxPROT`. To reuse the Make It Work checker on an AXI4-Lite interface, tie its `WLAST` and `RLAST` inputs high and the absent information inputs to constants. [B-AMBA-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-1_Protocol_Families_and_Tradeoffs/index) covers what else it drops.

**Two mistakes with the channel map.**
- **A master model that never drives BREADY or RREADY.** On B and R the master is the destination, so it must drive READY. If BREADY stays low, the slave must keep BVALID high (§A3.2.1), so BVALID sits high forever and the write never completes.
- **Expecting a write response per beat.** B carries one response for the whole burst; only R carries a response with every beat (§A3.4.4).

### The VALID/READY handshake

All five channels use one handshake process (IHI0022E §A3.2.1). In the specification's words and order:

1. **A transfer happens at a rising edge where VALID and READY are both high.** "Transfer occurs only when both the VALID and READY signals are HIGH." Every such edge is one transfer, so a source that keeps VALID high and changes the information right after each transfer edge moves one item at every edge where READY is also high (**back-to-back** transfers).
2. **A source never waits for READY.** "A source is not permitted to wait until READY is asserted before asserting VALID."
3. **VALID holds.** "Once VALID is asserted it must remain asserted until the handshake occurs", and the source "must keep its information stable until the transfer occurs".
4. **READY is free.** "A destination is permitted to wait for VALID to be asserted before asserting the corresponding READY", may assert READY before VALID, and "If READY is asserted, it is permitted to deassert READY before VALID is asserted."

There are three basic timings (Figures A3-2 to A3-4): VALID before READY, READY before VALID, and both on the same edge. A fourth case follows from rule 4: READY rises and falls again with no VALID, and nothing transfers.

**Two mistakes everyone makes here.**
- **Counting one transfer per VALID pulse.** In the back-to-back scenario VALID is one long pulse that carries three items. A monitor must record a transfer at *every* edge where VALID and READY are both high, and nothing else.
- **Treating READY alone as "data moved".** A READY with no VALID (the withdrawn case) transfers nothing; a monitor that samples on READY records phantom items.

### Holding VALID and the information

Rule 3 is the one checkers enforce most. Once the source raises VALID, the transfer happens at whichever edge the destination chooses to raise READY, so neither VALID nor any information signal may change until that edge (§A3.2.1). After the handshake edge the source may drop VALID or present the next item.

The check has to fire on **every stalled edge**, not just when VALID rises. A **stalled edge** is one where VALID is high and READY low. The property below says: at every stalled edge, VALID is still high at the next edge, and the information there is unchanged.

```systemverilog
// snippet: the hold rule (IHI0022E A3.2.1) for one channel
// valid && !ready: the item is waiting. |=> checks the next edge.
property p_hold(valid, ready);
  @(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    valid && !ready |=> valid;              // VALID not withdrawn
endproperty
property p_stable(valid, ready, info);
  @(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    valid && !ready |=> $stable(info);      // information not changed
endproperty
```

`|=>` (non-overlapping implication) checks its right side one edge after the left side matched, and `$stable(x)` is true when the sampled value of `x` equals the sampled value at the previous edge (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.12.7, §16.9.3). `disable iff (!ARESETn)` switches the check off while reset is asserted. Make It Work applies these two properties to all five channels with every information signal.

**A classic weak check.** `$rose(valid) |=> $stable(payload)` looks similar but checks only the edge after VALID rises. It misses a stall that begins after a back-to-back transfer, when VALID never fell, and it misses a change on the second or third stalled edge. It also fails *falsely* on legal traffic: when an item transfers at the very edge where VALID rises and the next item follows back-to-back, the information changes at the next edge, exactly as it should (Make It Work, W beats at edges 3 and 4).

### Why a source never waits for READY

This is the rule behind objective 3, and two different rules are often blurred into one. Keep them apart.

**Rule A: no waiting for READY, because of deadlock.** The specification states the dependency rules exist "To prevent a deadlock situation" (§A3.3.1). A destination is allowed to wait for VALID before it raises READY. If a source were also allowed to wait for READY before it raised VALID, connecting those two legal-looking components would leave both waiting forever: a **deadlock**. Nothing oscillates and nothing glitches; the bus simply never moves. So the protocol gives the right to wait to exactly one side of every channel, the destination.

The specification's own example crosses two channels (§A3.3.1, Caution): a master must not wait for AWREADY before driving WVALID, because a deadlock occurs if the slave is waiting for WVALID before it asserts AWREADY. The slave's wait is legal; the master's is not.

**Rule B: no combinational paths, because of timing loops.** Separately, "On master and slave interfaces there must be no combinatorial paths between input and output signals" (§A3.1.1, repeated in §A3.2.1). The specification states no reason; the usual engineering one is that if both sides of a channel had such paths, connecting them could close a combinational loop, and even one such path makes one component's timing depend on another's. This rule is symmetric: it forbids a slave from computing AWREADY combinationally from AWVALID just as much as it forbids a master from computing VALID combinationally from READY. So "a destination may wait for VALID" means *across clock edges*: the slave sees VALID at one edge and raises READY for a later edge (or raises READY in advance, before any VALID).

| Statement | True or false | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A source may hold VALID low until it sees READY | **False**: deadlock risk | §A3.2.1, §A3.3.1 |
| A destination may hold READY low until it sees VALID | **True** | §A3.2.1, §A3.3.1 |
| A destination may raise READY before any VALID, and drop it again | **True** | §A3.2.1 |
| At a slave interface, READY may be a combinational function of VALID | **False** | §A3.1.1, §A3.2.1 |
| The asymmetry exists to prevent combinational loops | **False**: it prevents deadlock; the combinational-path ban is a separate, symmetric rule | §A3.3.1 vs §A3.1.1 |

A deadlock at system scale, through an interconnect with many masters, is [B-AXI-5](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-5_AXI_Pitfalls_Interconnect_Deadlocks/index)'s subject, with its deadlock simulator.

### Cross-channel dependencies

Apart from the handshake on each channel, AXI defines very few relationships between channels. §A3.3 lists them: a write response follows the last write transfer of its transaction, read data follows its address, and handshakes obey the dependency rules of §A3.3.1. "Otherwise, the protocol does not define any relationship between the channels." In any transaction:

1. **A source never waits for READY.** The master must not wait for AWREADY or WREADY before asserting AWVALID or WVALID, nor for ARREADY before ARVALID. The slave must not wait for BREADY or RREADY before asserting BVALID or RVALID.
2. **A destination may wait for VALID, or not.** The slave may wait for AWVALID or WVALID, or both, before asserting AWREADY, and likewise before asserting WREADY; it may also assert either READY first.
3. **Write data may come first.** "The write data can appear at an interface before the write address for the transaction", for example when the address path has more register stages; it may also appear on the same edge (§A3.3).
4. **Read data follows the read address.** The slave "must wait for both ARVALID and ARREADY to be asserted before it asserts RVALID" (§A3.3.1).
5. **In AXI4, the write response follows both the address and the last data beat.** The slave "must wait for AWVALID, AWREADY, WVALID, and WREADY to be asserted before asserting BVALID", and also for WLAST, because BRESP may be signalled only after the last data transfer (§A3.3.1, AXI4 write response dependency).

**How to read the specification's dependency figures.** In Figures A3-5 to A3-7, "single-headed arrows point to signals that can be asserted before or after the signal at the start of the arrow", and "double-headed arrows point to signals that must be asserted only after assertion of the signal at the start of the arrow" (§A3.3.1). In prose this page always says "X waits for Y" instead of using arrows.

These are rules *within* one transaction. The order *between* transactions is set by IDs: AXI4 write data always follows the order of the write addresses, with no interleaving of beats from different writes (§A5.4, §A5.4.1), while read data of different IDs may interleave (§A5.3.1). [B-AXI-3](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-3_AXI_Ordering_and_IDs/index) owns that.

### Channel independence and backpressure

**Backpressure** is a destination holding READY low because it cannot take more; the source **stalls** with VALID high. Because each channel has its own handshake, a stall on one channel does not stop the *handshakes* on the others. A slave whose write buffer is full holds WREADY low, and the same master can keep issuing read addresses on AR and receiving read data on R.

Independence is not total, though. A stall reaches exactly the channels that depend on the stalled one through rules 4 and 5: backpressure on W delays that write's B (B waits for the WLAST handshake), backpressure on AW delays that write's B, and backpressure on AR delays that read's R. Nothing else waits.

**Predict first.** In the dependency explorer above, choose "Backpressure on W only". WREADY stays low for several edges while a read is in flight. At which edge does RLAST transfer, and at which edges do the W beats and B move?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

RLAST transfers at edge 3. The W beats do not move until edges 4 and 5, and B follows at edge 6, the first edge after the WLAST handshake. The read never waited for W.

</details>

Two clarifications that interviews like to probe:
- **Protocol versus design.** A slave may still couple its channels internally, for example by refusing reads while a write is pending. That is legal: READY is the slave's free choice. A master may likewise hold back a read until an earlier write has completed, by its own design choice. Independence is a property of the protocol, not a promise about every component.
- **The AHB contrast.** On an AHB bus ([B-AHB-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-1_AHB_Design_Timing_Mechanics/index)) one HREADY signal stalls the shared address and data pipeline together. AXI replaces it with one READY per channel.

- **Reads of different IDs may interleave** their beats on R (§A5.3.1), so a monitor groups R beats by RID.

### Reset

`ARESETn` is active LOW. IHI0022E §A3.1.2 gives three rules:

1. Reset "can be asserted asynchronously" (it may go LOW at any time, not only at a clock edge), "but deassertion must be synchronous with a rising edge of ACLK".
2. During reset, a master drives ARVALID, AWVALID and WVALID LOW, and a slave drives RVALID and BVALID LOW. All other signals may take any value.
3. "The earliest point after reset that a master is permitted to begin driving ARVALID, AWVALID, or WVALID HIGH is at a rising ACLK edge after ARESETn is HIGH."

Rule 3 is the one people get wrong. The master must first *see* ARESETn high at an edge, and only then may it start driving VALID; so VALID is low at that first edge and can be high, at the earliest, at the next one.

The assertion must therefore check one edge more than the reset itself:

```systemverilog
// snippet: rules 2 and 3 of IHI0022E A3.1.2, checked at every ACLK edge
// $rose(ARESETn) is true at the first edge where ARESETn is sampled HIGH.
property p_reset(valid);
  @(posedge ACLK) (!ARESETn || $rose(ARESETn)) |-> !valid;
endproperty
```

A check of `!ARESETn |-> !valid` alone covers rule 2 but misses rule 3. For AWVALID, WVALID and ARVALID the extra term is rule 3 itself. For BVALID and RVALID it adds nothing new but costs nothing: at the first edge out of reset no AW, W or AR handshake can have happened yet, so §A3.3.1 already forbids them there.

### AXI3 and AXI4 at the channel level

You will meet AXI3 components inside AXI4 systems. At the level of channels and handshakes, the differences are few:

| Item | AXI3 | AXI4 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WID` on the W channel | present (supports write interleaving) | removed: write data follows AW order | §A2.3, §A5.4, §A5.4.1 |
| `AxQOS`, `AxREGION`, `xUSER` | absent | present (USER optional) | §A2.2–§A2.6 |
| `AxLOCK` | 2 bits (normal, exclusive, locked) | 1 bit (normal, exclusive) | §A7.4 |
| B after AW | not required (strongly recommended for new slaves) | required | §A3.3.1, §A3.3.2 |
| Handshake, hold rule, R after AR, B after WLAST | same | same | §A3.2.1, §A3.3.1 |

"Any AXI3 master complies with the AXI4 write response requirements" (§A3.3.2): the extra rule constrains slaves only.

### In a real testbench

Every rule on this page becomes code in a protocol **agent** (the testbench block that drives and watches one interface; [A-UVM-7](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-7_VIP_Construction/index) builds one):

- **Master driver** (turns items into pin activity on the source side). Raise VALID with the information as soon as the item is ready, without looking at READY, and hold both until the handshake edge. Drive through a clocking block ([F4C](/curriculum/T1_Foundational/F4C_Clocking_Blocks/index)) so READY is sampled at the edge, not in a race:

  ```systemverilog
  // snippet: drive one AW item from a master driver
  // Call it right after @(vif.cb), as a driver loop does. A clocking drive issued
  // between edges takes effect at the next clocking event (IEEE 1800-2023 14.16).
  task automatic drive_aw(axi_aw_item item);
    vif.cb.AWVALID <= 1'b1;                 // raised without looking at AWREADY
    vif.cb.AWADDR  <= item.addr;            // ...and the rest of the AW information
    do @(vif.cb); while (!vif.cb.AWREADY);  // wait for the handshake edge
    vif.cb.AWVALID <= 1'b0;                 // or present the next item back-to-back
  endtask
  ```

  The `do … while` loop (IEEE 1800-2023 §12.7.5) waits for clocking events until AWREADY is sampled high at one; that edge is the handshake, because AWVALID has been high since the clocking event at which the task started. That is why the task must start right after `@(vif.cb)`, as a driver loop does (or begin with `@(vif.cb);` itself). A clocking drive issued between edges acts "as if [it] had executed at the time of the next clocking event" (§14.16). Called mid-cycle, the task would raise AWVALID only at the very edge its first `@(vif.cb)` wakes on; if AWREADY is sampled high there, the loop exits at once, the `AWVALID <= 0` drive lands in the same time step and wins (§14.16.2), and the item is lost without any transfer. Never write the AW and W drives in one sequential task that waits for AWREADY before starting W: that is exactly the illegal wait of the §A3.3.1 Caution.
- **Slave responder** (a testbench model of a slave). Any READY pattern is legal, so randomize it: always high, delayed, early-then-withdrawn. Random backpressure is how you find VALID-hold bugs in the design. Raise BVALID and RVALID without waiting for BREADY or RREADY, and only after the dependencies are met.
- **Monitor** (rebuilds transactions from the pins). Run one thread per channel. Record a transfer only at an edge where VALID and READY are both high. Queue W bursts and pair them with AW items in AW order, accepting W first (§A3.3, §A5.4.1). Group R beats by RID (§A5.3.1).
- **Protocol checker.** The assertions of Make It Work, in a module bound to every AXI interface with `bind` ([I-SV-4C](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-4C_Checkers/index), IEEE 1800-2023 §23.11). Because the rules hold at every hop (§A1.3.2), bind one at each master port and each slave port of an interconnect. In UVM verification IP (VIP: a reusable, packaged set of these components for one protocol), route failures into UVM reporting so they count in the test result (A-UVM-7).

**When not to rely on this checker alone.** Counter-based dependency checks see totals, not IDs; per-ID pairing of B with AW and R with AR belongs in the monitor and scoreboard ([B-AXI-6](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-6_AXI_Verification_Performance/index)).

### Recap

| Concept | AHB ([B-AHB-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-1_AHB_Design_Timing_Mechanics/index)) | AXI4 |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | One address/control path plus separate write-data and read-data buses, all under one HREADY | Five one-way channels: AW, W, B, AR, R |
| Flow control | One HREADY for the whole pipeline | One VALID/READY pair per channel |
| Who may wait | The slave, by holding HREADY low | Only the destination of each channel; a source never waits for READY |
| Write completion | The data phase ends the transfer | One BRESP per burst, after AW and WLAST (AXI4) |
| Read response | HRESP per transfer | RRESP per beat on R |
| Ordering between address and data | Fixed: address phase, then data phase | W may come before, with or after AW |

## Make It Work

### A checker you can run

The example has two files. `axi4_handshake_checker` watches one AXI4 interface and checks every rule this lesson teaches: hold and stability on all five channels, reset, and the R and B dependencies. `tb_top` plays a 10-edge legal trace with stalls on AW, W, B and R, in which the write data arrives before its address, and four **mutations** (deliberate single bugs in the trace) selected with **plusargs** (simulator command-line options such as `+BROKEN_B`, read with `$test$plusargs`, IEEE 1800-2023 §21.6).

```systemverilog
// compile
// axi4_handshake_checker.sv: pin-level AXI4 handshake checks for one interface.
// Each assertion names the Arm IHI0022E section it checks.
module axi4_handshake_checker #(
  parameter int ID_W = 4, ADDR_W = 32, DATA_W = 32
) (
  input logic                ACLK, ARESETn,
  // AW: write address channel, master -> slave
  input logic [ID_W-1:0]     AWID,
  input logic [ADDR_W-1:0]   AWADDR,
  input logic [7:0]          AWLEN,
  input logic [2:0]          AWSIZE, AWPROT,
  input logic [1:0]          AWBURST,
  input logic                AWLOCK,           // one bit in AXI4 (A7.4)
  input logic [3:0]          AWCACHE, AWQOS, AWREGION,
  input logic                AWVALID, AWREADY,
  // W: write data channel, master -> slave
  input logic [DATA_W-1:0]   WDATA,
  input logic [DATA_W/8-1:0] WSTRB,
  input logic                WLAST, WVALID, WREADY,
  // B: write response channel, slave -> master
  input logic [ID_W-1:0]     BID,
  input logic [1:0]          BRESP,
  input logic                BVALID, BREADY,
  // AR: read address channel, master -> slave
  input logic [ID_W-1:0]     ARID,
  input logic [ADDR_W-1:0]   ARADDR,
  input logic [7:0]          ARLEN,
  input logic [2:0]          ARSIZE, ARPROT,
  input logic [1:0]          ARBURST,
  input logic                ARLOCK,
  input logic [3:0]          ARCACHE, ARQOS, ARREGION,
  input logic                ARVALID, ARREADY,
  // R: read data channel, slave -> master
  input logic [ID_W-1:0]     RID,
  input logic [DATA_W-1:0]   RDATA,
  input logic [1:0]          RRESP,
  input logic                RLAST, RVALID, RREADY
);
  timeunit 1ns;
  timeprecision 1ps;

  // The information each channel must hold stable during a stall (A3.2.1).
  // xUSER is optional and not connected here; add it if your interface has it.
  // A let names an expression that properties can reuse (IEEE 1800-2023 11.12).
  let aw_info = {AWID, AWADDR, AWLEN, AWSIZE, AWBURST, AWLOCK, AWCACHE,
                 AWPROT, AWQOS, AWREGION};
  let w_info  = {WDATA, WSTRB, WLAST};
  let b_info  = {BID, BRESP};
  let ar_info = {ARID, ARADDR, ARLEN, ARSIZE, ARBURST, ARLOCK, ARCACHE,
                 ARPROT, ARQOS, ARREGION};
  let r_info  = {RID, RDATA, RRESP, RLAST};

  // Failure accounting, read by tb_top for its summary line.
  int unsigned fails;
  task automatic fail(input string rule);
    fails++;
    $error("%s at %0d ns", rule, $time);
  endtask

  // Handshake counters. Assertions sample them in the Preponed region
  // (IEEE 1800-2023 16.5.1), so at edge k they hold the handshakes of
  // edges 0..k-1: "an earlier handshake" is what A3.3.1 requires.
  int unsigned aw_done, wlast_done, b_done, ar_done, rlast_done;
  always_ff @(posedge ACLK or negedge ARESETn)
    if (!ARESETn) begin
      aw_done <= 0; wlast_done <= 0; b_done <= 0; ar_done <= 0; rlast_done <= 0;
    end else begin
      if (AWVALID && AWREADY)        aw_done    <= aw_done + 1;
      if (WVALID && WREADY && WLAST) wlast_done <= wlast_done + 1;
      if (BVALID && BREADY)          b_done     <= b_done + 1;
      if (ARVALID && ARREADY)        ar_done    <= ar_done + 1;
      if (RVALID && RREADY && RLAST) rlast_done <= rlast_done + 1;
    end

  // A3.2.1: once VALID is HIGH it stays HIGH until the handshake edge.
  property p_hold(valid, ready);
    @(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn) valid && !ready |=> valid;
  endproperty
  // A3.2.1: the source keeps its information stable until the transfer.
  property p_stable(valid, ready, info);
    @(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn) valid && !ready |=> $stable(info);
  endproperty
  // A3.1.2: VALID LOW during reset and at the first edge with ARESETn HIGH.
  property p_reset(valid);
    @(posedge ACLK) (!ARESETn || $rose(ARESETn)) |-> !valid;
  endproperty

  a_aw_hold:   assert property (p_hold(AWVALID, AWREADY)) else fail("a_aw_hold (A3.2.1)");
  a_w_hold:    assert property (p_hold(WVALID, WREADY))   else fail("a_w_hold (A3.2.1)");
  a_b_hold:    assert property (p_hold(BVALID, BREADY))   else fail("a_b_hold (A3.2.1)");
  a_ar_hold:   assert property (p_hold(ARVALID, ARREADY)) else fail("a_ar_hold (A3.2.1)");
  a_r_hold:    assert property (p_hold(RVALID, RREADY))   else fail("a_r_hold (A3.2.1)");

  a_aw_stable: assert property (p_stable(AWVALID, AWREADY, aw_info))
    else fail("a_aw_stable (A3.2.1)");
  a_w_stable:  assert property (p_stable(WVALID, WREADY, w_info))
    else fail("a_w_stable (A3.2.1)");
  a_b_stable:  assert property (p_stable(BVALID, BREADY, b_info))
    else fail("a_b_stable (A3.2.1)");
  a_ar_stable: assert property (p_stable(ARVALID, ARREADY, ar_info))
    else fail("a_ar_stable (A3.2.1)");
  a_r_stable:  assert property (p_stable(RVALID, RREADY, r_info))
    else fail("a_r_stable (A3.2.1)");

  // Master VALIDs: A3.1.2 rules 2 and 3. Slave VALIDs: rule 2; at the first
  // edge out of reset A3.3.1 already forbids them, so the extra term is safe.
  a_aw_reset: assert property (p_reset(AWVALID)) else fail("a_aw_reset (A3.1.2)");
  a_w_reset:  assert property (p_reset(WVALID))  else fail("a_w_reset (A3.1.2)");
  a_ar_reset: assert property (p_reset(ARVALID)) else fail("a_ar_reset (A3.1.2)");
  a_b_reset:  assert property (p_reset(BVALID))  else fail("a_b_reset (A3.1.2)");
  a_r_reset:  assert property (p_reset(RVALID))  else fail("a_r_reset (A3.1.2)");

  // A3.3.1, AXI4 write response dependency: BVALID only after an earlier
  // AW handshake and an earlier WLAST handshake that no B has used yet.
  a_b_dep: assert property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    BVALID |-> (aw_done > b_done) && (wlast_done > b_done))
    else fail("a_b_dep (A3.3.1)");
  // A3.3.1, read: RVALID only while a read address handshake is outstanding.
  a_r_dep: assert property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    RVALID |-> ar_done > rlast_done)
    else fail("a_r_dep (A3.3.1)");
endmodule
```

```systemverilog
// compile
// tb_top.sv: a 10-edge legal trace and four plusarg mutations for the checker.
// Edge k is the rising ACLK edge at 5 + 10*k ns. Element [k] of each *_V
// array is the value sampled at edge k.
module tb_top;
  timeunit 1ns;
  timeprecision 1ps;

  localparam int N = 10;

  logic ACLK = 1'b0;
  always #5 ACLK = ~ACLK;
```

### How to build a handshake checker

1. **Make it a module with only inputs**, one port per AXI signal, and attach it with `bind` (or instantiate it next to the interface).
2. **Add the five handshake counters**, reset asynchronously with ARESETn.
3. **Write one hold property and one stability property per channel**, with the complete information of that channel, including any `xUSER` signals your interface has.
4. **Add the reset property** on all five VALIDs, with the `$rose(ARESETn)` term.
5. **Add the R and B dependency properties** using the counters.
6. **Run legal traffic first** and expect zero failures, including traffic with W before AW, READY before VALID, and back-to-back transfers. A checker that fails on legal traffic is worse than none.
7. **Run each mutation** and confirm the failing assertion and edge you predicted.
8. **Add `cover property` statements** for the timings you must see: READY first, back-to-back, W before AW, and long stalls (Push Further shows them).

## Push Further

### Expert: combinational paths and register slices

"On master and slave interfaces there must be no combinatorial paths between input and output signals" (IHI0022E §A3.1.1, §A3.2.1). This forbids READY computed combinationally from VALID as much as VALID from READY, so no handshake output depends combinationally on an interface input, and timing closes per component, not across a chain of them. Because each channel is one-way with no fixed relationship to the others, "a register slice can be inserted at almost any point in any channel, at the cost of an additional cycle of latency" (§A1.3.3). A naive slice that registers READY halves throughput; a full-throughput slice (a two-entry "skid buffer") keeps one transfer per edge. That design is common practice, not a specification requirement.

A combinational path is a structural property. A simulation assertion sees only values at edges and cannot detect one; lint rules, static timing analysis or structural formal checks can (tool-dependent). A related simulation subtlety: a clocking-block drive issued right after `@(vif.cb)` takes effect at that same clocking event (IEEE 1800-2023 §14.16), which is why the `drive_aw` snippet does not hold AWVALID one cycle too long, and why it must be called right after a clocking event.

### Expert: default READY values and their obligations

§A3.2.2 states each channel's rule. AWREADY and ARREADY may default HIGH or LOW, and the specification recommends HIGH; but "When AWREADY is HIGH the slave must be able to accept any valid address that is presented to it." A default of LOW "forces the transfer to take at least two cycles, one to assert AWVALID and another to assert AWREADY". The other three are worded per channel. WREADY may default HIGH "only if the slave can always accept write data in a single cycle", and BREADY "only if the master can always accept a write response in a single cycle". RREADY may default HIGH "only if the master is able to accept read data immediately, whenever it starts a read transaction". And on R: "Even if a slave has only one source of read data, it must assert the RVALID signal only in response to a request for data." §A3.2.2 also phrases the hold rule per channel: for example, "AWVALID must remain asserted until the rising clock edge after the slave asserts AWREADY."

In a testbench, a slave model that ties READY high must really be able to take every item; a responder that defaults READY low tests the master's hold logic on every transfer.

### Expert: why AXI4 added the B-after-AW dependency

The AXI4 note gives two reasons (§A3.3.1): it "reflects the expected use in AXI3", since no sensible component accepts all write data and responds before accepting the address; and "by issuing a write response, the slave takes responsibility for hazard checking the write transaction against all subsequent transactions". A slave cannot check a write it has not seen the address of. §A3.3.2 draws the consequence: an AXI3 slave that responds before accepting the address is not AXI4-compliant and needs a wrapper that holds the response "until the appropriate address has been accepted"; "Any AXI3 master complies with the AXI4 write response requirements." A mixed AXI3/AXI4 environment therefore needs the `a_b_dep` check configurable per interface, enabled on every AXI4 slave port.

### Expert: reset corner cases

- **Asynchronous assertion.** ARESETn may fall between edges (§A3.1.2). An `@(posedge ACLK)` assertion sees only edge values, so a reset pulse that starts and ends between two edges is invisible to it; and `disable iff (!ARESETn)` uses current values, not sampled ones (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.12), so it disables attempts in flight as soon as reset falls. If your design must handle glitch-length resets, check them with the design's own reset synchronizer in mind (tool-dependent and design-dependent).
- **The first edge out of reset.** Covered by `$rose(ARESETn)` in `p_reset`. At the first ACLK edge there is no previous tick, and `$rose` compares with the default sampled value (§16.5.1, §16.9.3); since ARESETn starts LOW in the bench, x to 0 is not a rise.
- **X on VALID.** The specification says nothing about X; it is a simulation artifact. A checker that adds `!$isunknown(AWVALID)` outside reset (IEEE 1800-2023 §20.9) catches un-reset flops early. That is checker practice, not a protocol rule.
- **Transactions abandoned by a mid-test reset.** The specification defines interface behaviour during reset, not what happens to transactions in flight. Whether the scoreboard flushes them is testbench policy; [A-UVM-9](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-9_Reset_and_Error_Injection/index) covers reset-aware components.

### Expert: responses, errors and burst completion

"For a write transaction, a single response is signaled for the entire burst"; on reads, "the slave can signal different responses for different transfers in a burst" (§A3.4.4). "The required number of data transfers must be performed, even if an error is reported": a read of 8 beats that hits an error still returns 8 beats, each with an error response, and "No component can terminate a burst early" (§A3.4.1). For a write, the master may reduce the effect of the remaining beats by deasserting all write strobes, but it must still complete them (§A3.4.1). Consequences for a monitor: count beats to RLAST and WLAST regardless of response; never close a transaction on the first error beat. The four response values (OKAY, EXOKAY, SLVERR, DECERR) are defined in §A3.4.4; [B-AXI-4](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-4_AXI_Expert_Features_Cache_Atomics/index) covers EXOKAY with exclusive accesses.

### Expert: checker architecture

- **Counters give necessary conditions only.** `a_b_dep` and `a_r_dep` can never fire on legal traffic (a legal BVALID always has more AW and WLAST handshakes than B handshakes behind it), so they produce no false failures. But they can miss bugs: a BID that matches no outstanding AWID, or an R beat for an ARID that is not outstanding while another read is. Per-ID pairing (§A5.3.1) belongs in the monitor and scoreboard of [B-AXI-6](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-6_AXI_Verification_Performance/index).
- **The same properties as assumptions.** When you formally verify a slave, the master-side rules become constraints on its inputs: `assume property (p_hold(AWVALID, AWREADY));` and the stability and reset properties for AW, W and AR, plus "BREADY and RREADY are free". The slave-side rules stay assertions. An assumed property constrains the environment and is not itself proved (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.14.2). Swap roles when you verify a master.
- **Attach without editing the design.** `bind axi_slave axi4_handshake_checker chk (.*);` instantiates the checker inside every `axi_slave` instance, provided its port names match (§23.11).
- **Measure what the traffic exercised.** Coverage of handshake timings, with `cover property` (§16.14.3):

  ```systemverilog
  // snippet: inside axi4_handshake_checker
  c_ready_first: cover property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    !AWVALID && AWREADY ##1 AWVALID && AWREADY);
  c_back2back:   cover property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    WVALID && WREADY ##1 WVALID && WREADY);
  c_w_before_aw: cover property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    WVALID && WREADY && WLAST && !(AWVALID && AWREADY)
    && (aw_done <= wlast_done));
  c_long_stall:  cover property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
    (AWVALID && !AWREADY) [*4]);
  ```

  `c_w_before_aw` matches a WLAST handshake whose burst's address has not been accepted, neither at an earlier edge nor at this one. In AXI4 write data follows AW order (§A5.4.1), so this is burst number `wlast_done + 1`, and its AW is still missing whenever `aw_done <= wlast_done`.

### Expert: throughput and latency

One transfer per edge per channel is the ceiling, and only back-to-back VALID and READY reach it. Default-LOW AWREADY or ARREADY costs at least one extra cycle per transfer (§A3.2.2, Note). Each register slice adds one cycle of latency on its channel (§A1.3.3). Because reads and writes use separate channels, a slave can sustain read and write data at the same time. Measuring achieved throughput and latency from handshake counts and timestamps is [B-AXI-6](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-6_AXI_Verification_Performance/index)'s subject.

### Expert: interconnect realignment and checking every hop

W carries no address, so an interconnect that has to decide which slave receives the data "must realign the address and write data", to assure that write data is signalled as valid only to the slave it is destined for (§A3.3, Note). W-before-AW traffic is therefore exactly what exercises an interconnect's W buffering. And because one interface definition applies at every hop (§A1.3.2), a protocol violation can be introduced *inside* the interconnect: bind a checker at the slave side of the interconnect as well as at the master side, or you will only see the symptom far downstream.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

```systemverilog
// snippet: kata solution
a_w_hold:   assert property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
              WVALID && !WREADY |=> WVALID);
a_w_stable: assert property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
              WVALID && !WREADY |=> $stable({WDATA, WSTRB, WLAST}));
a_aw_reset: assert property (@(posedge ACLK)
              (!ARESETn || $rose(ARESETn)) |-> !AWVALID);
a_b_dep:    assert property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
              BVALID |-> (aw_done > b_done) && (wlast_done > b_done));
```

(a) **Zero.** The only W stall is at edge 4, and at edge 5 WVALID is still high with D1 and WLAST unchanged. AWVALID is low at edges 0 to 3. BVALID is high at edges 7 and 8, when `aw_done` and `wlast_done` are both 1 and `b_done` is 0.

(b) Each new mutation fails exactly one of your assertions.
- `+KATA_WDATA`: `a_w_stable` at **edge 5** (55 ns). The WLAST beat stalls at edge 4 with D1, and at edge 5 WDATA is D2, although no handshake happened at edge 4 (IHI0022E §A3.2.1). WVALID stayed high, so `a_w_hold` passes. The slave accepts D2 at edge 5, and D1 is lost.
- `+KATA_LATE_AW`: `a_b_dep` at **edge 7** (75 ns). The AW handshake moves to edge 7, the same edge at which BVALID is first high, so `aw_done` is still 0 there (§A3.3.1). At edge 8 the handshake is counted and B is legal. AWVALID is held through the longer stall, so no hold check fails.

(c)
- `+BROKEN_W`: `a_w_hold` at **edge 5** (VALID dropped after the stall at edge 4; §A3.2.1). `a_w_stable` passes, because the information did not change.
- `+BROKEN_RST`: `a_aw_reset` at **edge 2**, the first edge with ARESETn high (§A3.1.2 rule 3).
- `+BROKEN_B`: `a_b_dep` at **edge 6**: the AW handshake happens at edge 6 itself, so it is not yet counted (§A3.3.1).

**Two wrong answers that these traces expose.**
- **A `$rose` trigger for the hold rule**, for example `$rose(WVALID) |=> WVALID`. The stall at edge 4 follows the back-to-back D0 transfer at edge 3, so VALID did not rise there. Under `+BROKEN_W` a `$rose` trigger therefore never checks edge 5 and misses the bug. It then fails falsely at edge 7, after the legal handshake at edge 6. With `$stable` on the right-hand side it also fails falsely on the legal trace at edge 4, where D0 legally changes to D1, and under `+KATA_WDATA` it misses the change at edge 5 (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.9.3, §16.12.7).
- **A reset property without `$rose(ARESETn)`.** Then `+BROKEN_RST` passes silently.

### Debug challenge: the driver that waits

A colleague's master driver sends one write like this, and the simulation hangs against a slave model that raises AWREADY only after it has seen WVALID:

```systemverilog
// snippet: buggy driver task (inside a driver class)
// Called right after @(vif.cb), like drive_aw above.
task automatic send_write(axi_wr_item item);
  vif.cb.AWVALID <= 1'b1;
  vif.cb.AWADDR  <= item.addr;
  do @(vif.cb); while (!vif.cb.AWREADY);  // wait for the address handshake
  vif.cb.AWVALID <= 1'b0;
  vif.cb.WVALID  <= 1'b1;                  // only now offer the data
  vif.cb.WDATA   <= item.data;
  vif.cb.WLAST   <= 1'b1;
  do @(vif.cb); while (!vif.cb.WREADY);
  vif.cb.WVALID  <= 1'b0;
endtask
```

Name the rule it breaks, say which component is at fault, and sketch the fix before you open the answer.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The driver holds WVALID low until it has seen AWREADY. The slave holds AWREADY low until it sees WVALID, which is legal. Each waits for the other: the deadlock of the IHI0022E §A3.3.1 Caution ("a master must not wait for AWREADY to be asserted before driving WVALID"). The driver is at fault. The handshake counters stay frozen and the test ends on a timeout.

Fix: drive AW and W independently, for example from two parallel threads, each with its own handshake loop:

```systemverilog
// snippet: fixed driver task; still called right after @(vif.cb), like drive_aw
task automatic send_write(axi_wr_item item);
  fork
    begin                                  // AW: does not look at W
      vif.cb.AWVALID <= 1'b1;
      vif.cb.AWADDR  <= item.addr;
      do @(vif.cb); while (!vif.cb.AWREADY);
      vif.cb.AWVALID <= 1'b0;
    end
    begin                                  // W: does not look at AW
      vif.cb.WVALID <= 1'b1;
      vif.cb.WDATA  <= item.data;
      vif.cb.WLAST  <= 1'b1;
      do @(vif.cb); while (!vif.cb.WREADY);
      vif.cb.WVALID <= 1'b0;
    end
  join
endtask
```

The bug hides against slaves with AWREADY tied high, which is why a responder in your testbench should randomize its READY policy, including "wait for WVALID before AWREADY". For the system-level view of such cycles, try the deadlock simulator in [B-AXI-5](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-5_AXI_Pitfalls_Interconnect_Deadlocks/index).

</details>
