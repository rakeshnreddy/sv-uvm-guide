## Quick Take

- **What it is:** a clinic that applies what you learned about AXI, AHB, bridges and coherency to problems you have not seen before: whiteboard designs, waveforms to triage, verification plans and trick questions. Every answer is traced to a clause of the Arm specification. The clinic adds no new protocol rules; it teaches you to *use* the rules from [B-AXI-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-1_AXI_Channel_Architecture/index) to [B-AXI-6](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-6_AXI_Verification_Performance/index), [B-AHB-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-1_AHB_Design_Timing_Mechanics/index) to [B-AHB-3](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-3_AHB_Verification/index), [B-AMBA-F1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-F1_Bridges_and_System_Integration/index) and [B-AMBA-F2](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-F2_Future_Protocols_ACE_CHI/index).
- **Why it matters:** interviews and real debug sessions reward the same thing. They do not reward reciting signal names. They reward naming the **governing rule**, the **risk** it guards against, and the **smallest proof** (a checker or a test) that the rule holds. A verification engineer who answers that way finds bugs faster and writes plans that close.
- **The Analogy:** a code review in which every comment must cite a line of the specification. "This looks wrong" is not accepted; "§A3.3.1 forbids this dependency, here is the assertion that catches it" is. *Where it breaks:* a good interview answer also names risks you cannot prove on the spot, such as performance and buffer sizing, and some answers are design policy, not protocol (a posted bridge, a stall-or-reorder choice). Say which kind of answer you are giving.

**You will be able to:**
1. **Triage** a protocol failure from a waveform by checking handshakes first, then phases, then cross-channel dependencies, and **decide** whose bug it is: master, slave, interconnect or checker.
2. **Produce** a verification plan (stimulus, checks tied to a clause, and coverage) for an AXI interconnect, an AHB-to-AXI bridge or a coherent subsystem.
3. **Answer** AMBA trick questions with the governing rule and its clause.
4. **Calculate** the outstanding transactions an interface needs from bandwidth and latency, and **identify** interconnect deadlock risks.

### Words you need first

All of these were taught in the prerequisite lessons; this is a one-line reminder of each.

- **Master and slave:** the master starts transactions; the slave answers them. This lesson uses the words of IHI0022E (AXI) and IHI0033B.b (AHB). Later Arm issues say **manager** and **subordinate** for the same two roles.
- **Channel, handshake:** AXI has five **channels**: write address (AW), write data (W), write response (B), read address (AR) and read data (R). Each moves information with a **handshake**: the source drives `VALID`, the destination drives `READY`, and a transfer happens at a rising clock edge where both are high (IHI0022E §A3.2.1).
- **Beat, burst, AxLEN:** a **beat** is one data transfer; a **burst** is the beats of one transaction. `AxLEN` means `AWLEN` or `ARLEN`; a burst has `AxLEN + 1` beats (§A3.4.1).
- **Outstanding transaction:** one whose address has been accepted but whose response has not yet returned. AXI lets a master have many outstanding at once; its **ID** (`AWID`, `ARID`) says which ones must stay in order.
- **Interconnect:** the fabric that connects several masters to several slaves (decode, arbitration, routing). A **bridge** connects two different protocols, such as AHB and AXI.
- **Waited transfer (AHB):** a transfer whose phase is stretched because `HREADY` is low. A slave drives `HREADYOUT`; the bus multiplexer turns the selected slave's `HREADYOUT` into the `HREADY` that every master and slave sees.
- **Checker and scoreboard:** a **checker** (usually SystemVerilog Assertions) checks protocol rules cycle by cycle; a **scoreboard** checks that data and responses are correct ([A-UVM-6](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-6_Scoreboards_and_Reference_Models/index)). Either can be the thing that is wrong.
- **Verification plan (vplan):** the list of features to verify, each with its stimulus, its checks and the coverage that shows it was exercised.
- **Deadlock:** two or more parts each wait for something only the other can provide, so nothing ever moves.
- **Snoop (coherent systems only):** a request from the interconnect to a cache, asking for its copy of a line or telling it to drop that copy. [B-AMBA-F2](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-F2_Future_Protocols_ACE_CHI/index) teaches it; this lesson uses it only in the coherent-subsystem plan.

### How a strong answer is built

Most strong AMBA answers have the same four-part shape:

1. **Rule and clause.** State the protocol guarantee and where it lives ("§A3.3.1: the master must not wait for `AWREADY` before asserting `WVALID`").
2. **Risk.** Say what goes wrong if the rule is broken (a deadlock against a legal slave; a response routed to the wrong master).
3. **Smallest proof.** Propose the checker, test or architecture element that proves the rule holds, and say where it sits.
4. **Edge cases, volunteered.** Name the corner cases before the interviewer has to ask (W before AW, a burst ending exactly at 4KB, reset mid-burst).

**Score your own answers with this rubric.** Give each criterion 0 to 3; 10 or more out of 12 is a strong answer.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rule and clause | wrong or missing | right verdict, wrong or vague reason | right rule, no clause | right rule with its clause |
| Risk named | none | "it might break" | the failure named | the failure and who sees it (which component hangs or misroutes) |
| Smallest proof | none | "add assertions" | a named check | the check written or sketched, with where it lives |
| Edge cases | none | one, when prompted | one, volunteered | two or more, volunteered, including a legal case the naive check would flag |

**A graded example (a traced answer).** *Prompt:* "Can an AXI4 slave assert `BVALID` as soon as it has seen `WLAST`, before it has accepted the address?"

> *Candidate:* "No, because a write needs an address first. I would add an assertion."

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rule and clause | 1 | The verdict is right, but the reason is not. The rule is an explicit AXI4 dependency: the slave must wait for `AWVALID`, `AWREADY`, `WVALID`, `WREADY` and the `WLAST` beat before asserting `BVALID` (§A3.3.1). No clause given. |
| Risk named | 0 | Not mentioned. By issuing a write response, the slave takes responsibility for hazard checking the write against all later transactions (§A3.3.1, Note), which it cannot do before it knows the address. |
| Smallest proof | 1 | "An assertion" names no property. |
| Edge cases | 0 | Missed that W may legally arrive before AW (§A3.3), so all the data can be done while the address is still waiting, and that AXI3 does not state this dependency. |

**Total 2 of 12.** A 12-of-12 answer: "No. §A3.3.1 adds an AXI4 write-response dependency: `BVALID` only after the AW handshake and the handshake of the `WLAST` beat. The reason is that a response makes the slave responsible for ordering the write against later transactions. W may arrive before AW (§A3.3), so I would test W-first traffic and check `BVALID` with a counter of writes whose AW and `WLAST` handshakes have both happened; that checker is in Make It Work. In AXI3 this dependency was expected use, not a rule."

**Weak and strong answers side by side** (the pitfalls of answering):

| Weak answer | Strong answer |
|---|---|
| "I would use a scoreboard." | "I would reconstruct transactions per channel, keep expected queues per ID, and compare only after the `B` response or the `RLAST` beat (§A5.3.1)." |
| "The bridge splits bursts." | "A legal AHB incrementing burst never crosses 1KB (IHI0033B.b §3.5), so an AHB-to-AXI bridge never splits at 4KB. It splits an undefined-length INCR longer than 256 AXI beats (§A3.4.1) and reshapes for width conversion. The AXI-to-AHB direction must split at 1KB." |
| "I would add assertions." | "VALID stable until the handshake (§A3.2.1), no 4KB crossing (§A3.4.1), `WLAST` on beat `AWLEN + 1` (§A3.2.2), `BVALID` after both handshakes (§A3.3.1), plus a bounded-response liveness check whose bound is my test policy, not a protocol rule." |
| "AXI is faster than AHB." | "AXI decouples address, data and response channels and allows many outstanding transactions per ID set; AHB has one address/data pipeline in which a waited transfer stalls both phases." |

### The triage order for a waveform

When a simulation hangs or a checker fires, do not start with random internal DUT signals. Walk the interface in this order; each step rules out a class of bug before you look deeper.

1. **Handshakes.** Is every `VALID` held until its handshake, with its payload stable? Does any `VALID` wait for `READY`? (§A3.2.1)
2. **Phases.** For AHB, line up each address phase with its data phase; a waited transfer stretches both (IHI0033B.b §3.1, §3.6).
3. **Cross-channel dependencies.** Did `RVALID` follow its AR handshake, and `BVALID` the AW and `WLAST` handshakes (§A3.3.1)?
4. **Payload and LAST.** Is `WLAST` on beat `AWLEN + 1` and no other (§A3.2.2)? Are burst addresses legal (§A3.4.1)?
5. **Ordering.** Do same-ID responses come back in order, and are different-ID responses allowed to be out of order (§A5.3.1)?
6. **Classify the bug owner** with the table below.

| Symptom on the waveform | Usually whose bug | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `VALID` drops before `READY`, or the payload changes while `VALID` waits | the source of that channel | §A3.2.1 |
| `VALID` stays low until `READY` rises | the source (a forbidden dependency) | §A3.2.1, §A3.3.1 |
| `BVALID` before the `WLAST` handshake (AXI4: or before the AW handshake) | slave | §A3.3.1 |
| `RVALID` before the AR handshake | slave | §A3.3.1 |
| `B` or `R` delivered to the wrong master; same-ID reads from two slaves returned out of order | interconnect | §A5.3.5, §A5.3.1 |
| Different-ID responses out of order, flagged as an error | checker (scoreboard) | §A5.3.1 |
| AHB address or control changes while a NONSEQ or SEQ is waited | master | IHI0033B.b §3.6.1, §3.6.2 |
| AHB ERROR that lasts one cycle | slave | IHI0033B.b §5.1.3 |
| An assertion fires on a waited IDLE that becomes NONSEQ | checker | IHI0033B.b §3.6.1 |

**Debrief.** The first illegal edge is **edge 3**, and it is a **master bug**. At edge 2 the SEQ to `4000_0004` is waited (`HREADY` 0, `HRESP` OKAY), so its address and control must stay unchanged until `HREADY` is high (IHI0033B.b §3.6.1, §3.6.2). The slave is not at fault: it may insert wait states on any transfer, and at edge 4 it accepts what the master put on the bus. No `HRESP` value rescues this trace: an ERROR needs two cycles, the first with `HREADY` low and the second with `HREADY` high (§5.1.3), and a master that cancels after the first ERROR cycle must drive IDLE, not another SEQ (§3.5.2, §3.6.2).

The property that tells these two traces apart is B-AHB-3's `p_hold_in_wait`. A plain "nothing changes while `HREADY` is low" check would fire on the legal trace, and on a waited IDLE that legally becomes NONSEQ (§3.6.1):

```systemverilog
// snippet: from B-AHB-3's AHB checker (full module there)
// [IHI0033B.b §3.6.1, §3.6.2, §3.5.2] a waited NONSEQ/SEQ holds its address and
// control, unless the master cancels after the first ERROR cycle by driving IDLE.
property p_hold_in_wait;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (!HREADY && HTRANS inside {NONSEQ, SEQ}) |=>
        ($stable(HTRANS) && $stable(HADDR) && $stable({HWRITE, HSIZE, HBURST}))
     || ($past(HRESP) && HTRANS == IDLE);
endproperty
```

On Drill 1 its attempt that starts at edge 2 fails at edge 3, because `HADDR` changed and `$past(HRESP)` is 0. On the legal trace the attempt that starts at edge 2 passes at edge 3 through its cancel branch. (`$stable` and `$past` compare with the value sampled at the previous clock edge, IEEE 1800-2023 §16.9.3.)

**Debrief.** **Edge 6, master bug.** `AWLEN` 3 means four beats (§A3.4.1), and the master must drive `WLAST` high on the final write transfer of the burst (§A3.2.2). The fourth beat, at edge 6, has `WLAST` low. The slave is right to stay silent: it *must* wait for the handshake of the `WLAST` beat before asserting `BVALID` (§A3.3.1), so the transaction hangs.

**The checker trap.** The obvious checker, "count W beats after the AW handshake and require `WLAST` on beat `AWLEN + 1`", is wrong. Write data may legally arrive *before* its address (§A3.3), so that checker misses early beats and false-fails or hangs on legal traffic. Count W bursts on their own and pair the n-th W burst with the n-th AW, which works because AXI4 has no write interleaving: write data follows the order of its addresses (§A5.4.1). Make It Work builds this checker and proves it on both orders.

#### Drill 3: the checker is the bug

The third common verdict is "checker bug". A scoreboard sees `ARID` 1 issued before `ARID` 2, the slave returns all of `RID` 2's data first, and the scoreboard reports "expected transaction 1, got transaction 2". AXI lets transactions with different IDs complete in any order and even interleave their read data (§A5.3.1), so a scoreboard that compares in one global issue order is wrong. It must keep one ordered queue per ID.

The per-ID queue fix passes the correct DUT *and* still catches the buggy ones, which is the test of a good fix: the "search any match" policy also silences the false errors, but it stops catching a DUT that reorders the same ID. ### The rules behind the trick questions

Trick questions usually hide one of three things: an off-by-one encoding, a phase misalignment, or a dependency the specification forbids. Each row below is a rule you should be able to state with its clause. The last column is the lesson that teaches it in depth.

| # | Rule | Clause | The usual trick | Taught in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A master must not wait for `AWREADY` or `WREADY` before asserting `AWVALID` or `WVALID`. A slave may wait for either `VALID` before its `READY`. | IHI0022E §A3.3.1 | "May a master hold `WVALID` until `AWREADY`?" No. | [B-AXI-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-1_AXI_Channel_Architecture/index) |
| 2 | Burst length is `AxLEN + 1`. | §A3.4.1 | "`AWLEN = 0`?" One beat. | [B-AXI-2](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-2_AXI_Burst_Math/index) |

| 4 | Write data may arrive before, with or after its address. | §A3.3 | "Is W before AW a bug?" No. | [B-AXI-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-1_AXI_Channel_Architecture/index) |

### Whiteboard: an AXI interconnect for 4 masters and 2 slaves

**Prompt.** "Four AXI masters, two AXI slaves, every master can reach both slaves. Design the interconnect at block level."

**Strong answer outline:**

1. **Decode** each AW and AR address to a slave. An address that maps to no slave goes to a **default slave** that answers DECERR; the specification requires DECERR for an undecodable access and recommends the default slave (§A3.4.4).
2. **Arbitrate** per slave port, separately for AW and AR.
3. **Extend IDs.** Append bits that name the master port to `AWID` and `ARID`, so IDs from different masters never collide at a slave; route `B` and `R` back by those bits and strip them (§A5.3.5). With 4 masters that is 2 extra bits, so a slave sees wider IDs than any master drives.
4. **Route write data.** W carries no ID in AXI4, so keep, per slave, a queue of the AWs granted to it and forward W bursts in that order (§A5.3.2), and, per master, a queue of where its AWs went so its W bursts follow them (§A5.4). Write data can arrive before its address (§A3.3), so hold `WREADY` low or buffer until the route is known; the specification notes that the interconnect "must realign the address and write data" (§A3.3, Note).
5. **Keep same-ID order across slaves.** Two reads with the same `ARID` sent to S0 then S1 must return to the master in issue order (§A5.3.1). Either stall the second until the first completes, or reorder responses in a buffer.
6. **Size the outstanding tables** (see "Sizing outstanding transactions" below).
7. **Avoid deadlock:** no `VALID` ever waits for `READY` (§A3.2.1); no buffer waits for another that waits for it.
8. **Plan verification:** protocol checkers on every port, a per-ID scoreboard per master, and the plan in Make It Work.

**Follow-up traps an interviewer uses:**

| Interviewer asks | A strong answer |
|---|---|
| "Can W data arrive before AW?" | Yes (§A3.3). The interconnect must hold or buffer it until the AW route is known. |
| "What if two masters use the same ID?" | No problem: the interconnect appends master-port bits, so the IDs differ at the slave (§A5.3.5). |
| "Can responses return out of order?" | Different IDs may; same-ID responses must return in issue order, even from different slaves (§A5.3.1). |
| "How do you avoid deadlock?" | `VALID` never waits for `READY` (§A3.2.1, §A3.3.1); `READY` *may* wait for `VALID`, and may depend on buffer space. A deadlock needs a cycle of waits: a master that waits for `AWREADY` before `WVALID` facing a slave that waits for `WVALID`; write data forwarded out of AW order (§A5.3.2); same-ID tracking that stalls behind a full buffer. |
| "How wide are the IDs at the slave?" | Master ID width plus the master-port bits; IHI0022E recommends up to 4 master bits, up to 4 port bits and 8 bits of slave support (§A5.3.6). |

### Whiteboard: an AHB-to-AXI bridge

**Prompt.** "Design an AHB-to-AXI bridge that lets an AHB DMA engine write to AXI memory."

**Strong answer outline:**

1. The AHB side is a **slave** port: it accepts `HADDR`, `HTRANS`, `HWRITE`, `HSIZE`, `HBURST` and `HWDATA`, and drives `HREADYOUT` and `HRESP`.
2. The AXI side is a **master** port: it drives AW and W and accepts B.
3. A **phase aligner** pairs each accepted AHB address phase with the data phase that follows it (IHI0033B.b §3.1), and a **write-data buffer** decouples AHB's fixed pipeline from AXI's independent AW and W channels.
4. **Translate** `HBURST` and `HSIZE` into `AWLEN`, `AWSIZE` and `AWBURST` (the mapping is in [B-AMBA-F1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-F1_Bridges_and_System_Integration/index)).
5. **Split only where AXI needs it:** an undefined-length INCR longer than 256 beats (the AXI4 INCR limit, §A3.4.1), and width conversion. Because a legal AHB incrementing burst never crosses 1KB (§3.5), an INCR of more than 256 beats is only possible with byte or halfword transfers. No 4KB split is ever needed: every 4KB boundary is also a 1KB boundary, and a wrapping burst stays inside its aligned wrap window.
6. **Map responses:** `BRESP` SLVERR or DECERR becomes a two-cycle AHB ERROR (§5.1.3). Decide whether writes are **posted** (the AHB transfer completes before the AXI response) or **non-posted**.
7. **Stall the AHB side** by driving `HREADYOUT` low while buffers are full, and, for non-posted writes, until `BRESP` returns.

**Edge cases to volunteer:**

- An AHB burst that ends exactly at a 1KB boundary is legal and maps to one AXI burst.
- An AHB incrementing burst that crosses 1KB is illegal stimulus. An AHB-side checker should flag it (§3.5); the bridge is not designed to split it.
- Every transfer in an AHB burst is aligned to its size (§3.5), so "unaligned starts" are not AHB stimulus; test **narrow** transfers (smaller than the bus) at every byte-lane offset instead.
- `AWLEN` is beats minus one, and each split burst needs its own `WLAST` (§A3.2.2).
- If the second split burst returns SLVERR, a non-posted bridge must give the AHB master an ERROR even though the first split was OKAY. A posted bridge has already completed the AHB write and must report the error another way (an interrupt or a status register; a design choice, not a protocol rule).

### How to build a verification plan

A verification plan answers four questions per feature. Build it in this order and you will not end up with a list of test names that proves nothing:

1. **Feature:** what the design promises, in one line, with the clause it comes from.
2. **Stimulus:** the legal traffic that exercises it, including the corner that a naive design gets wrong, and the illegal traffic it must reject or flag.
3. **Check:** the assertion or scoreboard rule that fails if the promise is broken, with its clause.
4. **Coverage:** the coverpoint or cross that proves the stimulus actually happened.

**Prompt: verify an AHB-Lite slave.** Start with protocol (`HTRANS` legality, holds during waits, `HREADYOUT`, two-cycle ERROR), then transfer reconstruction (address phase aligned with the next data phase, §3.1), then functional behaviour (register reads and writes, byte lanes from `HSIZE` and address), negative tests (unmapped address, unsupported size, reset mid-transfer), and coverage of sizes, burst types, responses, regions and wait-state depths. [B-AHB-3](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-3_AHB_Verification/index) builds that environment.

**Prompt: verify the AHB-to-AXI bridge above.**

### Sizing outstanding transactions and spotting deadlock

**Why sizing matters.** A master that may have only a few transactions outstanding stops issuing while it waits for responses, so a long-latency path throttles its bandwidth. Interviewers ask "how many outstanding transactions do you need?" to see whether you can connect bandwidth, latency and buffer sizes.

**The method (a queueing result, not an AMBA rule).** **Little's law** says that, in steady state, the average number of items in a system equals their arrival rate times the average time each spends in it (Little, 1961). For a read port: *outstanding reads = issue rate × round-trip latency*.

**Worked example.** A master must sustain one 64-byte read burst every 4 cycles (on a 128-bit data bus, 4 beats per burst keep R fully busy). The latency from the AR handshake to the `RLAST` beat is 120 cycles. Then it needs 1/4 × 120 = **30** reads in flight. With only 16 it would issue 16 reads, then wait, and deliver 16 / 30, about 53%, of the target bandwidth.

The waveform shows the same law at a small scale: one read issued every 2 cycles, each completing 6 cycles after its AR handshake.

**What the number sizes.**
- The master's tracking: 30 entries, and enough IDs if it wants them to complete out of order (a 4-bit ID gives 16 values; reads that share an ID stay in order, §A5.3.1).
- The interconnect's tables: outstanding-transaction counters per master port, and the reorder buffer or stall logic for same-ID order across slaves (§A5.3.1).
- The slave's queue and its **read data reordering depth**, which is a static value its designer documents; a master cannot discover it (§A5.3.1).
- The ID width at the slave: master ID plus the master-port bits (§A5.3.5, §A5.3.6).

[B-AXI-3](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-3_AXI_Ordering_and_IDs/index) gives the same estimate when latency is measured to the first beat instead of the last.

**Deadlock checklist.** A deadlock needs a cycle of waits. Look for each of these:
1. A `VALID` that waits for `READY` (forbidden, §A3.2.1, §A3.3.1). The experiment above showed the classic case.
2. Write data forwarded to a slave in a different order from its AWs, so the slave waits for data stuck behind another slave's data (§A5.3.2). [B-AXI-5](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-5_AXI_Pitfalls_Interconnect_Deadlocks/index) analyses this case.
3. Same-ID ordering across slaves implemented with a buffer that can fill while the response it waits for is blocked behind it (§A5.3.1).
4. In coherent systems, a snoop that waits for the snooped master's own outstanding read (forbidden, §C5.2.5).

### Pitfalls the clinic is built around

- **"A master may wait for `AWREADY` before asserting `WVALID`."** Wrong (§A3.3.1). A slave may wait for `WVALID` before `AWREADY`, so such a master deadlocks a legal slave.
- **"WRAP and FIXED bursts fall under the INCR 4KB arithmetic."** Wrong (§A3.4.1). A WRAP stays inside its aligned window and FIXED repeats one address; only INCR needs the start-plus-length check.
- **"Out-of-order read data across IDs is a scoreboard failure."** Wrong (§A5.3.1). Different IDs may complete in any order and interleave; only same-ID reordering is a DUT bug.

### Recap

- A strong answer has four parts: rule and clause, risk, smallest proof, edge cases. Score it with the 0 to 3 rubric.
- Triage in order: handshakes, phases, dependencies, payload and LAST, ordering; then name the bug owner.
- Fourteen rules cover almost every trick question; know each clause.
- An interconnect needs decode, per-slave arbitration, ID extension, W routing in AW order, same-ID order across slaves, sized tables and a deadlock argument.
- An AHB-to-AXI bridge never splits at 4KB; it splits for the 256-beat limit and width conversion.
- Outstanding = rate × latency (Little's law); a deadlock needs a cycle of waits.

### A worked clinic answer: the plan for the 4×2 interconnect

### The smallest proof: a write-burst checker that tolerates W before AW

This is the corrected Drill 2 checker. It pairs the n-th W burst with the n-th AW, which is legal because AXI4 write data follows address order (§A5.4.1). It checks `WLAST` as soon as the burst's AW is known, and otherwise when the AW arrives. It also checks the AXI4 `BVALID` dependency. The bench then proves the checker: two legal orders must give no error, and three injected bugs must give exactly one error each.

```systemverilog
// compile
// A write-burst checker that accepts legal W-before-AW traffic (IHI0022E A3.3),
// and a bench that proves it: two legal orders, three injected bugs.
`timescale 1ns/1ps

module axi_wburst_checker (
  input logic       aclk,
  input logic       aresetn,
  input logic       awvalid,
  input logic       awready,
  input logic [7:0] awlen,
  input logic       wvalid,
  input logic       wready,
  input logic       wlast,
  input logic       bvalid,
  input logic       bready
);
  int unsigned aw_lens[$]; // AWLEN+1 of each address whose W burst has not ended
  int unsigned w_lens[$];  // beat counts of W bursts that ended before their AW
  int unsigned beat;       // W beats so far in the current burst
  int unsigned resp_owed;  // writes with AW and WLAST handshakes done, no B yet
  int unsigned len;
  int unsigned errors;

  function automatic void report(string msg);
    errors++;
    $error("AXI_WBURST at %0d ns: %s", $time, msg);
  endfunction

  // Testbench code, not RTL: it samples the handshakes at each rising edge.
  always @(posedge aclk) begin
    if (!aresetn) begin
      aw_lens.delete();
      w_lens.delete();
      beat = 0;
      resp_owed = 0;
    end else begin
      // 1. B first, judged only by handshakes from earlier edges (A3.3.1).
      if (bvalid && resp_owed == 0)
        report("BVALID before both the AW and the WLAST handshakes (A3.3.1)");
      if (bvalid && bready && resp_owed > 0)
        resp_owed--;

      // 2. AW. The n-th W burst belongs to the n-th AW (A5.4.1).
      if (awvalid && awready) begin
        len = awlen + 1;  // burst length = AxLEN + 1 (A3.4.1)
        if (w_lens.size() > 0) begin
          // Its data already arrived, which is legal (A3.3): compare now.
          if (w_lens[0] != len)
            report($sformatf("W burst had %0d beats, its AWLEN+1 = %0d (A3.2.2)",
                             w_lens[0], len));
          void'(w_lens.pop_front());
          resp_owed++;
        end else begin
          aw_lens.push_back(len);
          if (aw_lens.size() == 1 && beat >= len) begin
            // This AW names the burst in progress, which is already too long.
            report($sformatf("%0d W beats without WLAST, AWLEN+1 = %0d (A3.2.2)",
                             beat, len));
            void'(aw_lens.pop_front());
            beat = 0;
          end
        end
      end

      // 3. W. Once the AW is known, check WLAST on every beat (A3.2.2).
      if (wvalid && wready) begin
        beat++;
        if (aw_lens.size() > 0) begin
          if (wlast && beat < aw_lens[0])
            report($sformatf("WLAST on beat %0d, AWLEN+1 = %0d (A3.2.2)",
                             beat, aw_lens[0]));
          else if (!wlast && beat == aw_lens[0])
            report($sformatf("beat %0d = AWLEN+1 without WLAST (A3.2.2)", beat));
          if (wlast || beat == aw_lens[0]) begin
            if (wlast) resp_owed++;  // only a WLAST handshake allows a response
            void'(aw_lens.pop_front());
            beat = 0;
          end
        end else if (wlast) begin
          w_lens.push_back(beat);  // data before its address: legal (A3.3)
          beat = 0;
        end
      end
    end
  end
endmodule

module tb_top;
  logic       aclk = 1'b0;
  logic       aresetn = 1'b0;
  logic       awvalid = 1'b0;
  logic       awready = 1'b0;
  logic [7:0] awlen = '0;
  logic       wvalid = 1'b0;
  logic       wready = 1'b0;
  logic       wlast = 1'b0;
  logic       bvalid = 1'b0;
  logic       bready = 1'b0;
  int unsigned pass_n;
  int unsigned fail_n;

  always #5 aclk = ~aclk;  // rising edges at 5, 15, 25, ... ns

  axi_wburst_checker chk (.*);

  // The bench plays master and slave; every drive lands just after an edge.
  default clocking cb @(posedge aclk);
    output aresetn, awvalid, awready, awlen, wvalid, wready, wlast, bvalid, bready;
  endclocking

  task automatic send_aw(input logic [7:0] len_m1);
    cb.awvalid <= 1'b1;
    cb.awlen   <= len_m1;
    @(cb);                  // AWREADY is high, so the handshake is this edge
    cb.awvalid <= 1'b0;
  endtask

  // beats W transfers, with WLAST on beat last_on (0 means never)
  task automatic send_w(input int unsigned beats, input int unsigned last_on);
    for (int unsigned i = 1; i <= beats; i++) begin
      cb.wvalid <= 1'b1;
      cb.wlast  <= (i == last_on);
      @(cb);
    end
    cb.wvalid <= 1'b0;
    cb.wlast  <= 1'b0;
  endtask

  task automatic send_b();
    cb.bvalid <= 1'b1;
    @(cb);                  // BREADY is high
    cb.bvalid <= 1'b0;
  endtask

  task automatic check_case(input string name, input int unsigned before,
                            input int unsigned want);
    int unsigned got;
    @(cb);                  // the checker has now evaluated the last handshake edge
    got = chk.errors - before;
    if (got == want) pass_n++;
    else fail_n++;
    $display("case %s: errors=%0d expected=%0d %s", name, got, want,
             (got == want) ? "ok" : "MISMATCH");
  endtask

  initial begin
    int unsigned before;
    cb.awready <= 1'b1;     // READY high by default, as A3.2.2 recommends for AW
    cb.wready  <= 1'b1;
    cb.bready  <= 1'b1;
    repeat (2) @(cb);       // reset low for the first two edges
    cb.aresetn <= 1'b1;
    @(cb);

    before = chk.errors;    // legal: address first, 4 beats, WLAST on the 4th
    send_aw(8'd3); send_w(4, 4); send_b();
    check_case("legal AW then W", before, 0);

    before = chk.errors;    // legal: both data beats before the address
    send_w(2, 2); send_aw(8'd1); send_b();
    check_case("legal W before AW", before, 0);

    before = chk.errors;    // Drill 2: four beats, no WLAST
    send_aw(8'd3); send_w(4, 0);
    check_case("bug no WLAST", before, 1);

    before = chk.errors;    // BVALID after WLAST but before the AW handshake
    send_w(1, 1); send_b(); send_aw(8'd0); send_b();
    check_case("bug early BVALID", before, 1);

    before = chk.errors;    // WLAST on beat 2 of a 4-beat burst
    send_aw(8'd3); send_w(2, 2); send_b();
    check_case("bug early WLAST", before, 1);

    $display("PASS=%0d FAIL=%0d", pass_n, fail_n);
    $finish;
  end
endmodule
```

**Why the bench drives through a clocking block.** Each drive matures just after the edge at which it was made (IEEE 1800-2023 §14.16), so the checker, which samples at the rising edge, always sees the values from before that edge, like a flop. ### How to answer any clinic prompt, step by step

1. **Restate the question as a rule.** Find the clause before you answer (use the rules table).
2. **Say the verdict and the clause first,** then the reason.
3. **Name the risk:** which component hangs, misroutes or corrupts data.
4. **Give the smallest proof:** the assertion, scoreboard rule or test, and where it lives.
5. **Volunteer two edge cases,** including one legal case a naive check would flag (W before AW, a waited IDLE becoming NONSEQ, a WRAP at a page edge).
6. **For a design prompt, draw the blocks** (decode, arbitration, ID extension, W routing, ordering, buffers), then size them with Little's law.
7. **For a plan prompt, use the four columns:** stimulus, check with clause, coverage.
8. **Score yourself** with the rubric and fix the weakest criterion.

## Push Further

### Expert: same ID, different slaves

When one master sends reads with the same `ARID` to different slaves, the interconnect must make sure the master receives the data in the order it issued the addresses (IHI0022E §A5.3.1). Same-AWID writes must likewise complete in issue order (§A5.3). The specification states the result, not the mechanism. The two designs are a **stall** (hold a new same-ID request while an earlier one is outstanding to another slave: cheap, but one slow slave blocks the ID) and a **reorder buffer** (forward everything and hold early responses: fast, but its storage grows with depth × burst length). A verification plan must create the race on purpose: same ID, two slaves, the second one faster. [B-AXI-3](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-3_AXI_Ordering_and_IDs/index) covers the mechanisms in depth.

### Expert: write data order when masters merge

When the interconnect merges writes from several masters into one slave, it must forward the write data in the order of the addresses (§A5.3.2), and AXI4 has no write interleaving (§A5.4). An interconnect that grants AW from master A then B to a slave, but lets B's W through first because it was ready earlier, breaks the rule; the slave either corrupts data or waits forever for A's data. When the same masters also write to a second slave in the opposite order, the two W paths can wait on each other: the classic crossbar deadlock that [B-AXI-5](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AXI-5_AXI_Pitfalls_Interconnect_Deadlocks/index) analyses. The test: two masters, two slaves, AW grant orders that differ per slave, and W readiness that disagrees with both.

### Expert: an undefined-length INCR through an AHB-to-AXI bridge

An AHB undefined-length INCR does not announce its length, but AXI needs `AWLEN` up front and no component may end a burst early (§A3.4.1). The bridge must either buffer the AHB burst before issuing the AW, or issue bounded AXI bursts (or singles) as data arrives, accepting lower efficiency. Because AHB incrementing bursts never cross 1KB (IHI0033B.b §3.5), more than 256 beats needs byte or halfword transfers, so the 256-beat AXI limit bites only for narrow transfers. For reads, a bridge that guesses a long AXI burst for an undefined AHB INCR should not do so to a read-sensitive target: the AXI master must complete every transfer of the burst, and the reads it discards can lose data from a FIFO-like device (§A3.4.1, Note).

### Expert: an AXI4 master in front of an AXI4-Lite slave

AXI4-Lite has burst length 1, full-width accesses and no exclusives (§B1.1). Connecting an AXI4 master needs **ID reflection**: the AXI4-Lite side has no IDs, so the adapter returns the ID of each request with its response (§B1.2.1). If the master can issue bursts, the system needs one of three techniques (§B1.4): **conversion** (break every burst into single transactions, §B1.3), **protection** (discard non-compliant transactions and return an error), or **detection** (let them through and notify software). An interview answer names the choice and its test: a burst, a narrow write and an exclusive sent to the Lite slave, with the expected response for the chosen technique.

### Expert: corner cases in a coherent-subsystem plan

Three corners separate a strong coherency plan from a list of transactions. **Snoop crossing a WriteBack:** if a snoop arrives while the snooped master is writing the same line back to memory, the master must either answer without passing dirty data or ownership, or delay its response until the write completes (IHI0022E §C5.2.3). **Snoop progress:** a cached master must complete snoops before its own reads can be guaranteed to complete, and may wait only for its WriteNoSnoop, WriteBack, WriteClean, WriteEvict or Evict (§C5.2.5). **CHI retry:** a Completer may answer a request with RetryAck, and the Requester reissues it only after it has both the RetryAck and a protocol credit (PCrdGrant) for it (IHI0050E.b §2.11, §2.11.3). The PCrdGrant may arrive *before* the RetryAck (§2.11.3), so a checker must accept both orders. Snoops are never retried (the snoop flit has no AllowRetry field, Table 13-8 in §13.9.3). Each needs a directed race test and a check in the line-state model. See [B-AMBA-F2](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-F2_Future_Protocols_ACE_CHI/index).

### Expert: how do you know your checker is right?

A checker that never fails proves nothing; a checker that fails legal traffic wastes days. Methodology (not a standard rule): (1) **run it on legal corner traffic** that a naive version would flag (W before AW, a waited IDLE becoming NONSEQ, a WRAP at a page edge); (2) **mutate** the DUT or the stimulus and require exactly the expected failures, as the bench in Make It Work does; (3) **cover the antecedents**: a `cover property` (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.14.3) on each assertion's trigger shows the check actually started. Without it a pass may be **vacuous**: the trigger never happened, so the assertion "passed" without checking anything. B-AHB-2's "Expert: vacuity and the coverage of a checker" shows this for AHB.

### Kata: rapid-practice prompts, scored with the rubric

Answer each prompt out loud or in writing, in the four-part shape, before you open the model answers. Then score yourself 0 to 3 on each rubric criterion.

1. Design an AXI write-data steering block when W beats can arrive before or after AW arbitration.
2. Explain how you would detect an AHB slave that gives a one-cycle ERROR response.
3. Write the check for whether an AXI INCR burst crosses 4KB.
4. Describe a scoreboard for AXI reads with IDs 0 to 15.
5. Explain how an AHB-to-AXI bridge maps AXI SLVERR and DECERR to AHB.
6. Describe the difference between AXI4 and AXI4-Lite without saying "one is simpler".
7. Explain why an ACE or CHI checker must model ownership, not only data.

<details>
<summary>Model answers</summary>

1. **Rule:** W follows AW order per slave (§A5.3.2) and per master (§A5.4); W may arrive first (§A3.3). **Design:** per master, a queue of AW destinations in issue order; per slave, a queue of granted masters in AW order. A master's W burst goes to the destination at the head of its queue; a slave takes W only from the master at the head of its queue; both pop on `WLAST`. Hold `WREADY` low (or buffer) until the route is known. **Risk:** a W burst forwarded out of order corrupts data or deadlocks. **Proof:** the W-before-AW checker from Make It Work on every slave port, plus a two-master, two-slave test with opposite grant orders.
2. **Rule:** an ERROR takes two cycles, the first with `HREADY` low (IHI0033B.b §5.1.3). **Check:** B-AHB-3's `p_error_second_needs_first`: `(HRESP && HREADY) |-> $past(HRESP && !HREADY)`. **Edge case:** the forward property alone (first cycle implies second) never starts on a one-cycle ERROR, so it passes vacuously; you need both directions, and a cover on the antecedent.
3. **Rule:** no burst crosses 4KB; only INCR needs arithmetic (§A3.4.1). **Check:** for INCR only, `aligned = (addr >> size) << size`, then `aligned[11:0] + ((len + 1) << size) <= 4096`, evaluated at the address handshake and in at least 32 bits. **Edge cases:** ending exactly at `0xFFF` is legal; a WRAP at a page edge must not be flagged. B-AXI-2's `p_incr_no_4kb` is this check.
4. **Rule:** same ID in order, different IDs in any order and interleaved (§A5.3.1). **Design:** an array of 16 ordered queues of expected reads; push at the AR handshake (or from the reference model), match each R beat to the head of `exp_q[RID]`, check `RRESP` per beat and `RLAST` on beat `ARLEN + 1`, pop on `RLAST`. **End of test:** every queue empty. **Edge case:** interleaved beats of two IDs need a beat counter per ID.
5. **Rule:** AHB has one error response, a two-cycle ERROR (§5.1.3). **Mapping:** both SLVERR and DECERR become ERROR on the AHB transfer they answer, for a non-posted write or a read (read responses can differ per beat, so map per beat, §A3.4.4). A posted write has already completed OKAY on AHB, so the bridge reports the error another way (interrupt or status register; design choice). **Edge case:** an error on one split of a split burst. B-AMBA-F1 owns the details.
6. AXI4-Lite has burst length 1, full-width data (32 or 64 bits), no exclusive accesses, all accesses Non-modifiable and Non-bufferable, and no `AxLEN`, `AxSIZE`, `AxBURST`, `AxLOCK` or `AxCACHE` signals (§B1.1). It has no IDs, so an AXI4 master in front of it needs ID reflection (§B1.2.1), and bursts need conversion, protection or detection (§B1.4).
7. **Rule:** coherency is defined by states: a Unique line exists in no other cache, and a Dirty line's owner must write it back (IHI0022E §C1.2.3). A data-only checker sees the right value today and misses a second Unique copy that will diverge on the next store, or a dirty line dropped by an invalidation. **Check:** one line-state model across all caches plus a latest-data model, fed by every interface.

</details>
