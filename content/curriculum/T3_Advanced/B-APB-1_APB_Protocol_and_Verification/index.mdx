## Quick Take

- **What it is:** **APB** (Advanced Peripheral Bus) is the simplest bus in Arm's AMBA family. One **requester**, usually a bridge from an AXI or AHB bus, reads or writes one register of one peripheral at a time. Every **transfer** (one read or one write of one address) has a **setup phase** that lasts exactly one clock cycle and an **access phase** that the peripheral can stretch with **wait states** (it holds `PREADY` low) and can end with an **error response** (`PSLVERR` high). This lesson follows the current specification, Arm IHI 0024E (APB2 to APB5).
- **Why it matters:** Most configuration registers in a chip sit behind an APB bridge, so an APB agent is usually the bus your register model drives (milestone M7) and the configuration agent of a block testbench (M5). APB is also small enough to check completely: every rule fits in a few concurrent assertions. That makes it the cleanest first *complete* protocol agent: driver, monitor, sequences, assertions and a register adapter.
- **The Analogy:** Handing a form across a service counter. First you lay the form on the counter so the clerk can read it (setup: address, direction and data are valid); one moment later you ring the bell, which means "act on it now" (access: `PENABLE`). The clerk may raise a hand, "wait", for as long as needed (`PREADY` low), and while the hand is up you must not touch the form. When the clerk stamps it the visit is over, and the stamp may say "rejected" (`PSLVERR`). *Where it breaks:* at a real counter a rejected form usually means nothing happened, but an APB write that ends with an error may still have changed the register (IHI 0024E §3.4). And a counter has one clerk, while one APB requester has a separate select line (`PSELx`) for each peripheral.

**You will be able to:**
1. **Predict**, on a waveform, the setup and access phases of an APB transfer (`PSEL`, `PENABLE`), its wait states (`PREADY` low), the edge where it completes, and its error response (`PSLVERR`).
2. **Write** an APB requester driver, a monitor and the protocol assertions that keep address and control stable from setup to completion, and **debug** a failing transfer from the assertion that fires.
3. **Explain** what APB3, APB4 and APB5 add (`PREADY` and `PSLVERR`; `PPROT` and `PSTRB`; wake-up, user, parity and RME signals) and **decide** which signals a given interface may carry.
4. **Connect** the agent to a register model as its bus: write the adapter (`PSTRB` from byte enables, `PSLVERR` to status) and feed an explicit register predictor from the monitor.

### Words you need first

Terms from earlier lessons are linked; the new ones are defined here.

- **Requester and completer:** the requester starts transfers (on APB, almost always a **bridge**: a block that turns AXI or AHB transfers into APB transfers). The completer is the peripheral that answers. IHI 0024E uses these words (§1.1). Older issues, and A-UVM-7, say master and slave; later AXI and AHB issues say manager and subordinate. They name the same two roles.
- **Transfer:** one read or one write of one address. APB has no other kind.
- **Rising edge, sampled value:** every APB signal is timed against the rising edge of the clock `PCLK` (Table 2-1). In this lesson's waveforms, the value shown at edge *k* is the value **sampled at that edge**: what the completer's flip-flops and a clocking block read there ([F4C](/curriculum/T1_Foundational/F4C_Clocking_Blocks/index)).
- **Wait state:** an extra access cycle the completer adds by holding `PREADY` low.
- **Byte lane:** one 8-bit slice of a data bus. Lane *n* of a 32-bit bus is bits `[8n+7:8n]`.
- **Agent, driver, monitor, sequencer, sequence:** the UVM pieces that drive and observe one interface ([A-UVM-7](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-7_VIP_Construction/index)). The driver drives pins through the interface's clocking block; the monitor only samples and publishes one transaction per transfer on an analysis port.
- **Concurrent assertion:** a property checked at every clock edge, written in SystemVerilog Assertions (SVA): `|->` checks the consequent at the same edge, `|=>` at the next one; `$stable`, `$rose` and `$past` compare sampled values across edges (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.9.3); `disable iff` turns a property off, here during reset ([I-SV-4A](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-4A_SVA_Fundamentals/index)).
- **Register model terms:** the register model (RAL) holds a **mirrored value** per register, its best knowledge of what the design under test (DUT) contains. An **adapter** translates between the model's generic bus operation and your bus's sequence item, and a **register predictor** updates the mirror from transfers a monitor saw ([A-UVM-4A](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-4A_RAL_Fundamentals/index), [A-UVM-4B](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-4B_Advanced_RAL_Techniques/index)).

### Where APB sits, and who drives what

APB "is not pipelined and is a simple, synchronous protocol. Every transfer takes at least two cycles to complete" (IHI 0024E §1.1). It is designed for the programmable control registers of peripherals, which usually reach the main bus through an APB bridge. The bridge is the requester; each peripheral is a completer with its own select line.

These are the signals of an APB3 or APB4 interface (Table 2-1; the "Since" column is from Table B-1):

| Signal | Driven by | Meaning | Since |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PCLK` | clock source | every signal is timed against its rising edge | APB2 |
| `PRESETn` | system reset | reset, active low | APB2 |
| `PSELx` | requester | one per completer: "you are selected, a transfer is required" | APB2 |
| `PENABLE` | requester | marks the second and later cycles of a transfer | APB2 |
| `PWRITE` | requester | 1 for a write, 0 for a read | APB2 |
| `PADDR` | requester | byte address, at most 32 bits wide | APB2 |
| `PWDATA` | requester | write data, 8, 16 or 32 bits | APB2 |
| `PRDATA` | completer | read data, the same width as `PWDATA` | APB2 |
| `PREADY` | completer | extends a transfer while low | APB3 (optional) |
| `PSLVERR` | completer | error at the end of a transfer | APB3 (optional) |
| `PPROT` | requester | protection type: privileged, non-secure, instruction | APB4 (optional) |
| `PSTRB` | requester | write strobes, one per byte lane | APB4 (optional) |

Two facts from §2.1 shape everything that follows. APB has separate read and write data buses, but transfers cannot overlap, because the data buses "do not have their own individual handshake signals" (§2.1.2). And an unaligned `PADDR` is permitted, but the result is UNPREDICTABLE: the completer may use the unaligned address, the aligned one, or answer with an error (§2.1.1). This lesson's sequence items therefore constrain addresses to word alignment.

### Three states: IDLE, SETUP, ACCESS

**Debrief.** No: from ACCESS with `PREADY` high, the bus "moves directly to the SETUP state if another transfer follows" (§4.1). `PSEL` then stays high, and `PENABLE` drops for the one SETUP cycle. Three rules come straight from the state machine:

- **SETUP is exactly one cycle, with `PENABLE` low.** The interface "only remains in the SETUP state for one clock cycle and always moves to the ACCESS state on the next rising edge of the clock" (§4.1).
- **`PENABLE` is high only in ACCESS.** It "indicates the second and subsequent cycles of an APB transfer" (Table 2-1).
- **Only the completer ends ACCESS**, by driving `PREADY` high. While `PREADY` is low the interface stays in ACCESS.

**Misconception: "`PENABLE` is asserted in the first cycle."** It is not. The first cycle of every transfer is SETUP, with `PENABLE` low. A requester that raises `PSEL` and `PENABLE` together has skipped SETUP; the waveform later in this lesson shows what that does to a monitor.

**Debrief.**
- **The write completes at edge 3** (55 ns), the first edge where `PSEL`, `PENABLE` and `PREADY` are all 1. It has **one** wait state, edge 2. Edge 1 is SETUP, not a wait state, so the write takes three cycles and the read two.
- **Everything is frozen during the wait state.** While `PREADY` is low in a write's ACCESS state, `PADDR`, `PWRITE`, `PSEL`, `PENABLE`, `PWDATA`, `PSTRB`, `PPROT` and the APB5 signals `PAUSER` and `PWUSER` "remain unchanged" (§3.1.2). For a read the list is `PADDR`, `PWRITE`, `PSEL`, `PENABLE`, `PPROT` and `PAUSER` (§3.3.2). The same request signals must not change between SETUP and ACCESS either (§4.1).
- **`PREADY` outside ACCESS means nothing.** It "can take any value when `PENABLE` is LOW" (§3.1.2), which is why a completer with a fixed two-cycle access can tie it high. Here it is 1 at edges 0 and 5 and 0 at edge 4, and none of those values matter.
- **The read completes at edge 6** with `PSLVERR` 1. The completer "must provide the data before the end of the read transfer" (§3.3.1), but after an error the data may be invalid (§3.4): the 0 on `PRDATA` is not a value read from anywhere.
- **Any number of wait states is legal**, "from zero upwards" (§3.3.2). The protocol has no timeout; a bound is something your testbench chooses (see the assertions below).

**Misconception: "a transfer completes when `PENABLE` rises."** `PENABLE` rising starts ACCESS (edge 2 here); completion needs `PREADY` as well (edge 3). A monitor that publishes at the first `PENABLE` edge reports writes that have not happened yet and reads with data that is not valid yet.

### The error response: `PSLVERR`

`PSLVERR` lets a completer report that a read or a write failed (§3.4). Its rules are short and often misremembered:

- **It counts only at completion.** `PSLVERR` "is only considered valid during the last cycle of an APB transfer, when `PSEL`, `PENABLE`, and `PREADY` are all HIGH" (§3.4). A 1 in a wait state means nothing.
- **It is recommended, not required, to be low elsewhere.** This lesson's completer drives it low whenever it is not sampled.
- **An error does not mean "nothing happened".** A transfer that receives an error "might or might not have changed the state of the peripheral"; for a write, the register may have been updated anyway (§3.4). A read with an error can return invalid data, and the requester may still use it.
- **It is optional.** A completer without `PSLVERR` has the requester's input tied low (§3.4); Table B-1 gives its default as 0.
- **Bridges pass it up.** An AXI-to-APB bridge maps an APB error to `RRESP` for reads and `BRESP` for writes; an AHB-to-APB bridge maps it to `HRESP` (§3.4.3).

Typical reasons for an error are an unmapped address, a write to a read-only register, or an access the completer's protection rules forbid. Which of these produce `PSLVERR` is defined by each completer's own specification, not by APB. This lesson's completer errors on unmapped addresses and on writes to its read-only `ID` register.

### No bursts, no overlap, but back-to-back transfers

**Misconception: "APB supports bursts."** It does not. APB has no burst signals, no transfer IDs and no pipelining: every transfer is one SETUP plus at least one ACCESS cycle (§1.1, §4.1), and two transfers never overlap (§2.1.2). Copying 16 words to an APB peripheral takes 16 separate transfers, so at least 32 cycles.

What APB does allow is **back-to-back** transfers: when another transfer follows, the bus goes from ACCESS straight to SETUP, with `PSEL` staying high and `PENABLE` low for one cycle (§3.1.1, §4.1). That saves only the idle cycle between transfers; each transfer still needs its two cycles. This lesson's driver always idles one cycle, which keeps it simple; the expert section shows a driver that does not.

### APB4: write strobes and protection

APB4 (issue C of the specification) added two optional requester signals (Preface).

**`PSTRB`, write strobes (§3.2).** One bit per byte lane: `PSTRB[n]` is high when byte lane *n* of `PWDATA` holds data to write. A write with `PSTRB = 4'b0011` on a 32-bit bus changes only bits `[15:0]` of the register; this is a **sparse write**. Two rules matter for every agent:
- "For read transfers, the Requester must drive all bits of `PSTRB` LOW" (§3.2).
- `PSTRB` is optional on both sides. A completer without it simply does not support sparse writes; a requester without it connected to a completer with it has the completer's `PSTRB` inputs tied to `PWRITE`, so every write is full width (Table 3-1).

**`PPROT`, protection type (§3.5).** Three bits that let the completer or the interconnect refuse illegal accesses:

| Bit | LOW means | HIGH means |
|---|---|---|
| `PPROT[0]` | normal access | privileged access |
| `PPROT[1]` | secure access | non-secure access |
| `PPROT[2]` | data access | instruction access (a hint, not always accurate) |

"The primary use of `PPROT` is as an identifier for Secure or Non-secure transactions" (§3.5). A requester without `PPROT` can be connected to a completer that has it only if fixed protection attributes are functionally correct (Table 3-3). This lesson's sequence items default to `3'b000` (normal, secure, data); the expert section shows how a register access chooses another value.

### APB5: wake-up, user signals, parity and RME (awareness)

APB5 (issue D, 2021) added optional features that a VIP for a modern SoC must at least recognise; issue E (2023) added RME support and "remains APB5" (Preface). Each feature exists only when an interface **property** (a configuration parameter of the interface, such as `Wakeup_Signal` or `Check_Type`) enables it, and each can only be added to APB5 interfaces:

- **Wake-up signalling (§3.7):** `PWAKEUP`, from the requester, tells a clock or power controller that the interface is about to be used. It must be glitch-free, may rise before, during or after `PSELx`, and must stay high until `PREADY` if it was high together with `PSELx`. A completer may wait for `PWAKEUP` before it raises `PREADY`, so a requester that never asserts it can deadlock the interface.
- **User signalling (§3.8):** `PAUSER` and `PWUSER` (requester) and `PRUSER` and `PBUSER` (completer) carry user-defined attributes. APB does not define their meaning, and the specification recommends not using them.
- **Parity protection (chapter 5):** with `Check_Type` set to `Odd_Parity_Byte_All`, odd-parity check signals such as `PADDRCHK`, `PCTRLCHK` and `PWDATACHK` cover the interface signals for safety-critical designs (§5.2, §5.5).
- **RME support (§3.6):** `PNSE`, with `PPROT[1]`, selects one of four physical address spaces (Secure, Non-secure, Root, Realm) for Arm's Realm Management Extension.

Which signal can appear on which revision (Table B-1, abridged; "optional" also covers Arm's conditional entries, which depend on a property):

| Signal | APB2 | APB3 | APB4 | APB5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PCLK`, `PRESETn`, `PADDR`, `PSELx`, `PENABLE`, `PWRITE`, `PWDATA`, `PRDATA` | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| `PREADY` (default 1), `PSLVERR` (default 0) | no | optional | optional | optional |
| `PPROT` (default `0b000`), `PSTRB` | no | no | optional | optional |
| `PWAKEUP`, `PAUSER`, `PWUSER`, `PRUSER`, `PBUSER`, `PNSE`, check signals | no | no | no | optional |

### When each signal must be valid

Appendix A of IHI 0024E lists when each signal must be valid, that is, driven to a known, meaningful value. It is exactly what a protocol checker may rely on:

| Must be valid | Signals |
|---|---|
| always | `PSEL`, `PWAKEUP` |
| when `PSEL` is high | `PADDR`, `PPROT`, `PNSE`, `PENABLE`, `PWRITE`, `PAUSER`, `PSTRB`, `PWDATA` (enabled write lanes only), `PWUSER` (writes only) |
| when `PSEL` and `PENABLE` are high | `PREADY` |
| when `PSEL`, `PENABLE` and `PREADY` are high | `PRDATA` (reads only), `PSLVERR`, `PRUSER` (reads only), `PBUSER` |

"It is recommended that signals which are not required to be valid are driven to zero" (Appendix A). A recommendation is not a rule: a checker must not fail a design because `PADDR` holds an old value in IDLE, as in edge 4 of the waveform above.

**Debrief.** A passive agent builds no `sqr` and no `drv` (`get_is_active()` returns `UVM_PASSIVE`, IEEE 1800.2-2020 §13.4.2.2), so `seq` and the map's frontdoor accesses have nowhere to go. Everything on the observing side stays: `mon`, the assertions in `bus`, and `predictor`. The mirror therefore still follows the bus, because it is updated from what `mon` saw, not from what the test asked for. That is the reason to use **explicit prediction** (a predictor fed by the monitor) on any bus another master can also drive.

The parts, one line each:
- **`apb_if`** holds the signals, two clocking blocks (`drv_cb` drives the request and samples the response; `mon_cb` only samples) and the protocol assertions.
- **`apb_item`** is the sequence item: the request fields (`write`, `addr`, `wdata`, `pstrb`, `pprot`) and the response fields the driver or the monitor fills in (`rdata`, `slverr`, `wait_cycles`).
- **`apb_driver`** turns one item into one transfer. **`apb_monitor`** turns one transfer into one new item. **`apb_sequencer`** is a plain `uvm_sequencer #(apb_item)`.
- **`apb_agent`** builds the monitor always, and the sequencer and driver only when active.
- **`apb_rw_seq`** runs one transfer with the fields the caller chooses. **`apb_reg_adapter`** connects the register model.

### The driver: phases become clocking-block drives

A clocking block with the default output skew drives a signal just after the clocking event in which the drive executes (IEEE 1800-2023 §14.16), so a value driven at edge *k* is sampled at edge *k*+1. The driver uses that to place each phase:

| Time | `apb_driver` | What the next edge samples |
|---|---|---|
| 25 ns (an edge, reset released) | drives SETUP: `PSEL` 1, `PENABLE` 0, `PWRITE` 1, `PADDR` 0, `PSTRB` `1111`, `PWDATA` `A5` | 35 ns: SETUP |
| 35 ns (the SETUP edge) | drives `PENABLE` 1 | 45 ns: ACCESS |
| 45 ns (first ACCESS edge) | reads `PREADY` 0: counts a wait state, drives nothing | 55 ns: ACCESS again |
| 55 ns (completion edge) | reads `PREADY` 1 and `PSLVERR` 0; drives `PSEL` 0, `PENABLE` 0, `PSTRB` 0; calls `item_done()` | 65 ns: IDLE |

Two details make it correct. First, the driver **starts a transfer only just after an edge**. An item can arrive in the middle of a cycle; a synchronous drive made then still takes effect at the *next* clocking event (§14.16), and the `PENABLE` drive made at that event would take effect at the same time, skipping SETUP. That is why `run_phase` waits for an edge, and for reset to be released, after `get_next_item()`. Second, **it drives `PSTRB` to 0 for reads** whatever the item says (§3.2).

Here is the bug the first detail prevents. A driver variant raises `PSEL` and `PENABLE` together for a write to this lesson's completer. **Predict** which assertion fires, at which edge, and what the monitor publishes.

### The monitor: one new item per completed transfer

`apb_monitor` follows the state machine from the outside:
1. At every edge, look for SETUP (`PSEL` 1, `PENABLE` 0, reset released) and copy the request from `mon_cb`.
2. At each following edge, give up if reset is asserted or the bus left ACCESS (the assertions report why); count a wait state while `PREADY` is 0.
3. At the completion edge, copy `PRDATA` (reads only) and `PSLVERR`, and publish the item on `ap`.

It creates a **new object for every transfer**, because subscribers such as the predictor or a scoreboard may keep the handle. Because it returns at the completion edge and looks again at the very next edge, it also catches back-to-back transfers, whose SETUP edge immediately follows a completion edge. A monitor that waits for `PSEL` to fall before it looks for the next SETUP would miss them.

### Protocol assertions

Rules about signal timing live in the interface as concurrent assertions, so they check every transfer whether the agent is active or passive. Each one in `apb_if` maps to a rule above:

| Assertion | Checks | IHI 0024E |
|---|---|---|
| `a_setup_to_access` | SETUP is followed by ACCESS, with `PADDR`, `PWRITE`, `PPROT`, `PSTRB` and (writes) `PWDATA` unchanged | §4.1 |
| `a_access_from_setup` | ACCESS is entered only from SETUP | §4.1, Table 2-1 |
| `a_wait_hold` | while `PREADY` is 0, `PSEL`, `PENABLE` and the request stay unchanged | §3.1.2, §3.3.2 |
| `a_enable_falls` | `PENABLE` is low after a completion | §3.1.1 |
| `a_pstrb_read` | `PSTRB` is `0000` during reads | §3.2 |
| `a_psel_known`, `a_req_known`, `a_wdata_known`, `a_pready_known`, `a_resp_known` | each signal is known whenever Appendix A says it must be valid | Appendix A |
| `a_wait_bound` | the transfer completes within `MAX_WAIT` wait states | **not an APB rule**: testbench policy |

Points to notice in the code:
- **`a_wait_bound` is a policy.** APB allows any number of wait states (§3.3.2). The bound comes from the completer's own specification or from the test, and exists to turn a hang into an error message. Label such checks so nobody mistakes them for the protocol.
- **`a_wdata_known` checks only enabled lanes.** It masks `pwdata` with the strobes, because an X AND 0 is 0 (IEEE 1800-2023 §11.4.8), matching Appendix A's "active write data lanes only".
- **The cover properties** (`c_no_wait`, `c_one_wait`, `c_error`, `c_back_to_back`; §16.14.3) tell you whether a run exercised what the assertions check. An assertion that never had a wait state to look at proves nothing about wait states.
- **They report through UVM.** Each `else` branch calls `` `uvm_error ``, so a failure counts in the UVM report summary; with a bare `$error` it would not ([A-UVM-7](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-7_VIP_Construction/index) explains why). `checks_enable` lets an error-injection test turn them off for one interface instance.

### The agent as the register model's bus

The register map calls the adapter to turn each register access into an `apb_item` and back (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §19.2.1). The generic side is `uvm_reg_bus_op` (§19.1.2):

| `uvm_reg_bus_op` | `apb_item` | APB signal |
|---|---|---|
| `kind` (`UVM_WRITE` or `UVM_READ`) | `write` | `PWRITE` |
| `addr` | `addr` | `PADDR` |
| `data` | `wdata` (writes), `rdata` (reads) | `PWDATA`, `PRDATA` |
| `byte_en` | `pstrb` (writes only) | `PSTRB` |
| `status` | `slverr`, plus an X test on `rdata` | `PSLVERR` |

Three decisions in `apb_reg_adapter`, each with a reason:
1. **`reg2bus()` copies `byte_en` to `PSTRB` for writes only.** The uvm-core 2020.3.1 map (`uvm_reg_map.svh`) fills `byte_en` for reads too, so an adapter that copies it unconditionally drives `PSTRB` high during reads, which §3.2 forbids and `a_pstrb_read` reports.
2. **`bus2reg()` sets `byte_en` from `PSTRB` for writes.** The predictor passes `byte_en` to the register's prediction, so a sparse write updates only the fields in the written byte lanes. Leave it out, and the predictor's default (all lanes, uvm-core `uvm_reg_predictor.svh`) predicts the whole register.
3. **`PSLVERR` becomes `UVM_NOT_OK`.** The frontdoor access returns that status to the test, and the register prediction is skipped (expert section).

`supports_byte_enable = 1` declares that the bus can carry byte enables (§19.2.1.2.2), and `provides_responses = 0` says the driver writes the response into the request item, which is what `bus2reg()` then reads (§19.2.1.2.3).

**Explicit prediction.** `env` turns auto-prediction off (`set_auto_predict(0)`, §18.2.5.2) and connects the agent's analysis port to `uvm_reg_predictor #(apb_item)` (§19.3), whose `map` and `adapter` it sets in `connect_phase`. ### Debugging: symptom, cause, fix

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `[APB_SVA] ... ACCESS without a SETUP cycle` on every transfer; the monitor publishes nothing; mirror checks fail | the driver raises `PENABLE` with `PSEL`, or drives SETUP in the middle of a cycle | start each transfer just after a clocking-block edge; drive `PENABLE` only after the SETUP edge |
| `[APB_SVA] ... request changed during a wait state`, only on some transfers | the driver changes a request signal inside its wait loop; transfers with no wait state hide it | drive the request once, in SETUP, and hold it to completion |
| `[APB_SVA] ... PSTRB active during a read` on register reads | the adapter copies `byte_en` to `PSTRB` for reads | drive `PSTRB` 0 for reads in `reg2bus()` and in the driver |
| `RegModel` mismatch after a sparse write: the mirror shows bytes that were not written | `bus2reg()` does not set `byte_en` from `PSTRB`, or a field spans written and unwritten lanes | set `rw.byte_en`; give byte-lane fields their own `uvm_reg_field` |
| `UVM_WARNING ... [PREDICT_NOK]` after an access, and a mirror that no longer matches | the access ended with `PSLVERR`, so prediction was skipped | expected; decide from the completer's specification whether the register changed, and mirror-read it if in doubt |
| the run hangs, or `a_wait_bound` fires | the completer never raises `PREADY` (on APB5, possibly because it waits for a `PWAKEUP` that never comes, §3.7.2) | fix the completer or the requester; the bound only makes the hang visible |
| `read()` returns `UVM_HAS_X`, or `a_resp_known` fires | `PRDATA` has X at completion: an undriven bit or a missing default in the completer's read mux | drive every `PRDATA` bit at completion |

### Recap

- A transfer is SETUP (exactly one cycle, `PENABLE` low) plus ACCESS (until `PREADY` is high); it completes at the edge where `PSEL`, `PENABLE` and `PREADY` are all 1.
- While `PREADY` is low, everything the requester drives stays frozen. `PSLVERR` counts only at completion, and an errored write may still have changed the register.
- No bursts, no overlap; back-to-back transfers go from ACCESS straight to SETUP.
- APB3 added `PREADY` and `PSLVERR`; APB4 `PPROT` and `PSTRB` (low on reads); APB5 wake-up, user, parity and RME signals.
- The agent's driver starts transfers just after an edge; the monitor publishes a new item at each completion; the interface's assertions check every rule, and label the wait bound as policy.
- The adapter maps `byte_en` to `PSTRB` for writes only and back again in `bus2reg()`, and `PSLVERR` to `UVM_NOT_OK`; an explicit predictor fed by the monitor keeps the mirror right.

## Make It Work

### The worked example, file by file

Five files, compiled in this order. The interface comes first because the agent's package refers to it through the virtual-interface type `apb_vif_t`.

**File 1: the interface, with its clocking blocks and protocol assertions.** The rule numbers in the comments are sections of IHI 0024E.

```systemverilog
// compile: apb_if.sv (file 1 of 5); compile uvm-core 2020.3.1 (uvm_pkg) first
`timescale 1ns/1ps
`include "uvm_macros.svh"

interface apb_if #(int unsigned MAX_WAIT = 16) (input logic pclk, input logic presetn);
  import uvm_pkg::*;  // the assertions report through UVM, so they count

  logic        psel, penable, pwrite;
  logic [31:0] paddr, pwdata, prdata;
  logic [2:0]  pprot;
  logic [3:0]  pstrb;
  logic        pready, pslverr;

  bit checks_enable = 1'b1;  // an error-injection test may clear it

  clocking drv_cb @(posedge pclk);   // the requester driver
    output psel, penable, pwrite, paddr, pprot, pstrb, pwdata;
    input  pready, prdata, pslverr, presetn;
  endclocking

  clocking mon_cb @(posedge pclk);   // the monitor drives nothing
    input psel, penable, pwrite, paddr, pprot, pstrb, pwdata;
    input pready, prdata, pslverr, presetn;
  endclocking

  // Protocol rules from Arm IHI 0024E. Every property samples at the rising
  // edge of PCLK, like the completer's flip-flops.

  // R1 (4.1): SETUP lasts one cycle and is followed by ACCESS with the same request.
  a_setup_to_access: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && !penable) |=> (psel && penable && $stable(paddr) && $stable(pwrite)
                            && $stable(pprot) && $stable(pstrb)
                            && (!pwrite || $stable(pwdata))))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: SETUP not followed by a stable ACCESS"))

  // R2 (4.1): ACCESS is entered only from SETUP.
  a_access_from_setup: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    $rose(psel && penable) |-> $past(psel && !penable))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: ACCESS without a SETUP cycle"))

  // R3 (3.1.2, 3.3.2): while PREADY is low the whole transfer is frozen.
  a_wait_hold: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && penable && !pready) |=> (psel && penable && $stable(paddr)
                                      && $stable(pwrite) && $stable(pprot)
                                      && $stable(pstrb)
                                      && (!pwrite || $stable(pwdata))))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: request changed during a wait state"))

  // R4 (3.1.1): PENABLE is deasserted at the end of a transfer.
  a_enable_falls: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && penable && pready) |=> !penable)
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: PENABLE still high after completion"))

  // R5 (3.2): PSTRB is all LOW during a read.
  a_pstrb_read: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && !pwrite) |-> (pstrb == 4'b0000))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: PSTRB active during a read"))

  // R6 (Appendix A): each signal is known whenever it must be valid.
  a_psel_known: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    !$isunknown(psel))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: PSEL is X or Z"))

  a_req_known: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    psel |-> !$isunknown({penable, pwrite, paddr, pprot, pstrb}))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: request has X or Z while PSEL is 1"))

  // Only the byte lanes PSTRB enables must be valid: X & 0 is 0.
  a_wdata_known: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && pwrite) |-> !$isunknown(pwdata & {{8{pstrb[3]}}, {8{pstrb[2]}},
                                                {8{pstrb[1]}}, {8{pstrb[0]}}}))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: enabled PWDATA lane is X or Z"))

  a_pready_known: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && penable) |-> !$isunknown(pready))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: PREADY is X or Z in ACCESS"))

  a_resp_known: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && penable && pready) |-> !$isunknown(pslverr)
                                    && (pwrite || !$isunknown(prdata)))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: response has X or Z at completion"))

  // P1: testbench policy, not an APB rule (any number of wait states is legal).
  a_wait_bound: assert property (@(posedge pclk)
    disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)
    (psel && !penable) |-> ##[1:MAX_WAIT+1] (psel && penable && pready))
    else `uvm_error("APB_SVA", $sformatf("%m: no completion within %0d wait states",
                                         MAX_WAIT))

  // Did the run exercise what the rules talk about?
  c_no_wait:  cover property (@(posedge pclk) disable iff (!presetn)
    (psel && !penable) ##1 (psel && penable && pready));
  c_one_wait: cover property (@(posedge pclk) disable iff (!presetn)
    (psel && !penable) ##1 (psel && penable && !pready) ##1 (psel && penable && pready));
  c_error:    cover property (@(posedge pclk) disable iff (!presetn)
    psel && penable && pready && pslverr);
  c_back_to_back: cover property (@(posedge pclk) disable iff (!presetn)
    (psel && penable && pready) ##1 (psel && !penable));
endinterface
```

**File 2: the DUT,** a completer with three registers: `CTRL` at `0x0` and `DATA` at `0x4` (both read-write, and both honour `PSTRB`) and the read-only `ID` at `0x8`. Every write takes one wait state; every read takes none. Unmapped addresses and writes to `ID` end with `PSLVERR`.

```systemverilog
// compile: apb_regs.sv (file 2 of 5): the DUT, an APB completer
`timescale 1ns/1ps

// A completer with three registers. A write takes one wait state, a read none.
module apb_regs (
  input  logic        pclk, presetn,
  input  logic        psel, penable, pwrite,
  input  logic [31:0] paddr, pwdata,
  input  logic [2:0]  pprot,     // accepted; this completer ignores it
  input  logic [3:0]  pstrb,
  output logic        pready, pslverr,
  output logic [31:0] prdata
);
  localparam logic [31:0] ID_VALUE = 32'h4150_4231;  // "APB1" in ASCII

  logic [31:0] ctrl_q, data_q;
  logic        wait_q;  // 1 in the cycle after a write's first ACCESS edge
  logic        bad;

  // Unmapped addresses, and writes to the read-only ID, get an error.
  assign bad     = !(paddr inside {32'h0, 32'h4, 32'h8}) || (pwrite && paddr == 32'h8);
  assign pready  = !pwrite || wait_q;
  assign pslverr = psel && penable && pready && bad;  // LOW whenever it is not sampled

  always_comb begin
    prdata = '0;
    if (psel && !pwrite)
      case (paddr)
        32'h0:   prdata = ctrl_q;
        32'h4:   prdata = data_q;
        32'h8:   prdata = ID_VALUE;
        default: prdata = '0;
      endcase
  end

  always_ff @(posedge pclk or negedge presetn) begin
    if (!presetn) begin
      ctrl_q <= '0;
      data_q <= '0;
      wait_q <= 1'b0;
    end else begin
      wait_q <= psel && penable && !pready;
      if (psel && penable && pready && pwrite && !bad)
        for (int n = 0; n < 4; n++)
          if (pstrb[n]) begin       // only the enabled byte lanes change
            if (paddr == 32'h0) ctrl_q[8*n +: 8] <= pwdata[8*n +: 8];
            else                data_q[8*n +: 8] <= pwdata[8*n +: 8];
          end
    end
  end
endmodule
```

**File 3: the agent's package:** the item, the driver, the monitor, the agent, a one-transfer sequence and the register adapter. It knows nothing about this particular DUT, so it can be reused on any APB4 interface.

```systemverilog
// compile: apb_pkg.sv (file 3 of 5): the APB4 agent and its register adapter
`timescale 1ns/1ps
`include "uvm_macros.svh"

package apb_pkg;
  import uvm_pkg::*;

  typedef virtual apb_if apb_vif_t;

  class apb_item extends uvm_sequence_item;
    `uvm_object_utils(apb_item)
    // Request: chosen by a sequence, driven by the requester.
    rand bit        write;
    rand bit [31:0] addr;
    rand bit [31:0] wdata;
    rand bit [3:0]  pstrb;
    rand bit [2:0]  pprot;
    // Response: filled in by the driver (and by the monitor on its own copy).
    logic [31:0]    rdata;        // 4-state, so an X on PRDATA stays visible
    bit             slverr;
    int unsigned    wait_cycles;

    constraint c_align { addr[1:0] == 2'b00; }   // unaligned: UNPREDICTABLE (2.1.1)
    constraint c_strb  { !write -> pstrb == 4'b0000;          // 3.2
                         write -> { soft pstrb == 4'b1111; } }
    constraint c_prot  { soft pprot == 3'b000; }  // normal, secure, data (3.5)

    function new(string name = "apb_item");
      super.new(name);
    endfunction

    virtual function string convert2string();
      return $sformatf("%s addr=%0h %s=%0h strb=%b prot=%b waits=%0d slverr=%0b",
                       write ? "WR" : "RD", addr, write ? "wdata" : "rdata",
                       write ? wdata : rdata, pstrb, pprot, wait_cycles, slverr);
    endfunction
  endclass

  typedef uvm_sequencer #(apb_item) apb_sequencer;

  class apb_driver extends uvm_driver #(apb_item);
    `uvm_component_utils(apb_driver)
    apb_vif_t vif;  // set by apb_agent

    function new(string name, uvm_component parent);
      super.new(name, parent);
    endfunction

    virtual task run_phase(uvm_phase phase);
      // IDLE, with the other request signals at zero (IHI 0024E Appendix A).
      vif.drv_cb.psel    <= 1'b0;
      vif.drv_cb.penable <= 1'b0;
      vif.drv_cb.pwrite  <= 1'b0;
      vif.drv_cb.paddr   <= '0;
      vif.drv_cb.pprot   <= '0;
      vif.drv_cb.pstrb   <= '0;
      vif.drv_cb.pwdata  <= '0;
      forever begin
        seq_item_port.get_next_item(req);
        // Start just after a clock edge, never in the middle of a cycle or in reset.
        do @(vif.drv_cb); while (vif.drv_cb.presetn !== 1'b1);
        drive_transfer(req);
        seq_item_port.item_done();
      end
    endtask

    protected task drive_transfer(apb_item it);
      // SETUP: these drives land just after this edge; the next edge samples them.
      vif.drv_cb.psel    <= 1'b1;
      vif.drv_cb.penable <= 1'b0;
      vif.drv_cb.pwrite  <= it.write;
      vif.drv_cb.paddr   <= it.addr;
      vif.drv_cb.pprot   <= it.pprot;
      vif.drv_cb.pstrb   <= it.write ? it.pstrb : 4'b0000;  // LOW for reads (3.2)
      if (it.write) vif.drv_cb.pwdata <= it.wdata;
      @(vif.drv_cb);                        // the SETUP edge
      vif.drv_cb.penable <= 1'b1;           // ACCESS from the next edge on
      it.wait_cycles = 0;
      @(vif.drv_cb);                        // the first ACCESS edge
      while (vif.drv_cb.pready !== 1'b1) begin
        it.wait_cycles++;                   // PREADY low: a wait state
        @(vif.drv_cb);
      end
      // Completion edge: PSEL, PENABLE and PREADY are all 1.
      if (!it.write) it.rdata = vif.drv_cb.prdata;
      it.slverr = vif.drv_cb.pslverr;
      vif.drv_cb.psel    <= 1'b0;           // back to IDLE; PADDR, PWRITE and
      vif.drv_cb.penable <= 1'b0;           // PWDATA keep their values
      vif.drv_cb.pstrb   <= 4'b0000;
    endtask
  endclass

  class apb_monitor extends uvm_monitor;
    `uvm_component_utils(apb_monitor)
    apb_vif_t                     vif;  // set by apb_agent
    uvm_analysis_port #(apb_item) ap;   // one new item per completed transfer

    function new(string name, uvm_component parent);
      super.new(name, parent);
      ap = new("ap", this);
    endfunction

    virtual task run_phase(uvm_phase phase);
      forever begin
        @(vif.mon_cb);
        if (vif.mon_cb.presetn === 1'b1 && vif.mon_cb.psel === 1'b1
            && vif.mon_cb.penable === 1'b0)
          collect();                        // this edge is a SETUP edge
      end
    endtask

    protected task collect();
      apb_item tr;
      tr = apb_item::type_id::create("tr");  // a new object for every transfer
      tr.write = vif.mon_cb.pwrite;
      tr.addr  = vif.mon_cb.paddr;
      tr.wdata = vif.mon_cb.pwdata;
      tr.pstrb = vif.mon_cb.pstrb;
      tr.pprot = vif.mon_cb.pprot;
      forever begin
        @(vif.mon_cb);
        if (vif.mon_cb.presetn !== 1'b1) return;               // reset: discard
        if (vif.mon_cb.psel !== 1'b1 || vif.mon_cb.penable !== 1'b1) return;
        if (vif.mon_cb.pready === 1'b1) break;                 // completion edge
        tr.wait_cycles++;
      end
      if (!tr.write) tr.rdata = vif.mon_cb.prdata;
      tr.slverr = vif.mon_cb.pslverr;
      `uvm_info("APB_MON", tr.convert2string(), UVM_MEDIUM)
      ap.write(tr);
    endtask

    endfunction

    virtual function uvm_sequence_item reg2bus(const ref uvm_reg_bus_op rw);
      apb_item it;

      it.addr  = rw.addr;
      it.wdata = it.write ? rw.data : '0;
      // The map fills byte_en for reads too; PSTRB must stay LOW on reads (3.2).
      it.pstrb = it.write ? rw.byte_en[3:0] : 4'b0000;

    virtual function void bus2reg(uvm_sequence_item bus_item, ref uvm_reg_bus_op rw);
      apb_item it;
      if (!$cast(it, bus_item)) begin
        `uvm_fatal("NOT_APB", "bus2reg: the item is not an apb_item")
        return;
      end
      rw.kind    = it.write ? UVM_WRITE : UVM_READ;
      rw.addr    = it.addr;
      rw.data    = it.write ? it.wdata : it.rdata;  // X and Z bits become 0
      rw.byte_en = '1;                             // reads: every lane
      if (it.write) rw.byte_en = it.pstrb;         // writes: the lanes PSTRB enabled
      if (it.slverr)
        rw.status = UVM_NOT_OK;                    // PSLVERR
      else if (!it.write && $isunknown(it.rdata))
        rw.status = UVM_HAS_X;
      else
        rw.status = UVM_IS_OK;
    endfunction
```

### How to build an APB agent, step by step

1. **List the signals** your interface has, from the completer's and the bridge's specifications (APB3, APB4 or APB5; which optional signals exist), and the wait-state bound the test may assume.
2. **Write the interface:** the signals, a clocking block for the driver (outputs: the request; inputs: the response and reset) and one for the monitor (inputs only), and one concurrent assertion per rule, reporting through `` `uvm_error `` and guarded by `disable iff (!presetn || !checks_enable)`. Add cover properties for 0, 1 and many wait states, errors and back-to-back transfers.
3. **Write the item** with request fields, response fields and the protocol's constraints (aligned address, `PSTRB` 0 on reads).
4. **Write the driver:** after `get_next_item()`, wait for a clock edge with reset released; drive SETUP, then `PENABLE` at the SETUP edge; wait for `PREADY`; capture `PRDATA` and `PSLVERR` at the completion edge; return to IDLE (or go straight to the next SETUP); call `item_done()`.
5. **Write the monitor:** find the SETUP edge, count wait states, publish a new item at the completion edge, and discard a transfer cut short by reset.
6. **Write the agent:** always the monitor; the sequencer and driver only when `get_is_active()` is `UVM_ACTIVE`; promote the monitor's analysis port.
7. **Write the adapter:** `reg2bus()` with `PSTRB` from `byte_en` for writes only; `bus2reg()` with `byte_en` from `PSTRB`, `PSLVERR` to `UVM_NOT_OK`, and X detection on reads.
8. **Connect it in the environment:** `set_sequencer(agt.sqr, adapter)`, `set_auto_predict(0)`, a `uvm_reg_predictor` whose `map` and `adapter` you set, fed from the agent's analysis port.
9. **Prove it:** run directed transfers with known waits and errors, then random traffic against a completer with random waits (the kata), and check the cover properties.

## Push Further

### Expert: back-to-back transfers and a zero-idle driver

The specification lets the bus go from ACCESS straight to SETUP when another transfer follows (IHI 0024E §4.1, §3.1.1). `apb_driver` never does: after `get_next_item()` it always waits for an edge, so there is at least one IDLE cycle between transfers, and `c_back_to_back` is never hit. That is legal, but a bridge does issue back-to-back transfers, and a completer bug that shows only then (for example, wait-state logic that does not reset between transfers) needs a driver that can produce them:

```systemverilog
// snippet: apb_driver::run_phase for back-to-back transfers. drive_transfer()
// must not drive PSEL 0 at completion; drive_idle() drives PSEL 0, PENABLE 0, PSTRB 0.
drive_idle();
do @(vif.drv_cb); while (vif.drv_cb.presetn !== 1'b1);  // now just after an edge
forever begin
  seq_item_port.try_next_item(req);    // zero time: still in this edge's time slot
  if (req == null) begin
    drive_idle();                      // IDLE from the next edge
    seq_item_port.get_next_item(req);  // may return between two edges
    @(vif.drv_cb);                     // so realign before driving SETUP
  end
  drive_transfer(req);                 // returns in the completion edge's time slot
  seq_item_port.item_done();
end
```

- **Why it works.** `try_next_item()` "returns immediately" with an item or `null` (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §15.5.2.4). Called in the completion edge's time slot, a SETUP drive made after it takes effect just after that edge (IEEE 1800-2023 §14.16), so the next edge samples SETUP: ACCESS, then SETUP, with `PSEL` never low.
- **When it does not.** In uvm-core 2020.3.1 (`seq/uvm_sequencer.svh`), `try_next_item()` waits for the nonblocking-assignment region once (the default `wait_for_sequences_count`) for a sequence to request, and once more for it to supply its item. A sequence that does anything time-consuming between items, or a layered sequencer, misses that window, and the driver falls back to an idle cycle. Back-to-back traffic is therefore something to *measure* with `c_back_to_back`, not to assume.
- **No glitches.** If two synchronous drives to one clocking output mature in the same Re-NBA region, only the last value is driven (§14.16.2). Driving `PSEL` 0 at completion and 1 again for the next SETUP in *different* iterations of the scheduling loop could glitch it, which is why `drive_transfer()` leaves `PSEL` alone at completion and only `drive_idle()` lowers it.

Interview angle: "How many cycles do 16 APB writes to a zero-wait completer take?" At least 32 back-to-back, about 48 with this lesson's one-idle driver, plus any wait states.

### Expert: what `PSLVERR` means for the register mirror

IHI 0024E §3.4 allows a write that ends with an error to have changed the register or not, and a read with an error to return invalid data. The register layer has to pick one behaviour:
- In uvm-core 2020.3.1, `uvm_reg::do_predict()` (`reg/uvm_reg.svh`) predicts only when the status is `UVM_IS_OK`; otherwise it warns `PREDICT_NOK` ("status UVM_NOT_OK; skip prediction."). With this lesson's adapter, an errored write therefore leaves the mirror at its old value, and an errored read does not update it either.
- The frontdoor `write()` returns `UVM_NOT_OK` to the caller (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §18.4.4.9). Tests must check `status`; `apb_demo_test` does it with `check_ok()`.
- If the completer's specification says erroring writes *do* take effect (some peripherals report a protection error after updating), the mirror is now wrong. Either mirror-read the register after the error, or add a `uvm_reg_cbs::post_predict()` callback (§18.11.2.6) or a predictor `pre_predict()` override (§19.3.3.2) that encodes the completer's documented behaviour. Never encode it in the adapter, which is shared by every register on the bus.
- At SoC level the error travels on: an AXI-to-APB bridge returns it as `RRESP` or `BRESP`, an AHB-to-APB bridge as `HRESP` (§3.4.3), so the same register access shows a different error encoding on each side of the bridge. Bridge checking is [B-AMBA-F1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AMBA-F1_Bridges_and_System_Integration/index)'s topic.

### Expert: byte enables in uvm-core register prediction

Three uvm-core 2020.3.1 details decide whether sparse writes are predicted correctly:
- **The predictor's default is "all lanes".** `uvm_reg_predictor::write()` (`reg/uvm_reg_predictor.svh`) sets `rw.byte_en = -1` before it calls `bus2reg()`, "in case they forget to set byte_en". An adapter that does not set it makes every observed sparse write look like a full write.
- **Prediction is per field, by the field's lowest byte lane.** `uvm_reg::do_predict()` passes each field the enables shifted down to the field's lowest byte, and `uvm_reg_field::do_predict()` (`reg/uvm_reg_field.svh`) skips the field only when the enable of its lowest byte is 0 ("Assume that the entire field is enabled"). A 32-bit field written with `PSTRB = 0011` is predicted *as a whole*. Model registers whose software uses sparse writes with byte-lane-aligned fields, as `DATA` does with `LO` and `HI`.
- **`supports_byte_enable` gates field-level frontdoor access.** `uvm_reg_field::is_indv_accessible()` refuses individual field access when the adapter does not support byte enables; it also requires the field to be configured as individually accessible and to be alone in its bus-width word. With 32-bit registers on a 32-bit APB that last condition rarely holds, so `field.write()` usually writes the whole register.

### Expert: choosing `PPROT` per register access

The adapter cannot see the test, but it can see the register access that produced the bus operation: `get_item()` returns the `uvm_reg_item` being translated, only inside `reg2bus()` (IEEE 1800.2-2020 §19.2.1.2.7), and that item carries the `extension` object the caller passed to `write()`, `read()`, `mirror()` or `update()` (§19.1.1.2.12, §18.4.4.9):

### Expert: APB5 in a VIP

What a VIP must add for an APB5 interface, each feature gated by the interface property that enables it (Table B-3):
- **`PWAKEUP` (§3.7.2):** an assertion that `PWAKEUP` stays high until `PREADY` once it was high with `PSELx`; a check that `PWAKEUP` itself is never X (Appendix A: always valid); and a completer model option that waits for `PWAKEUP` before raising `PREADY`, which is permitted and is how a requester bug becomes a deadlock. The recommendation to assert it at least one cycle before `PSELx` is a coverage item, not an error.
- **User signals (§3.8):** `PAUSER` and `PWUSER` are request signals, so they join the stability checks (same value in SETUP and every ACCESS cycle); `PRUSER` and `PBUSER` are checked only at completion. Their widths are properties, so the interface is parameterized by them, which changes the virtual-interface type ([A-UVM-7](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-7_VIP_Construction/index) shows the consequences).
- **Parity (chapter 5):** odd parity over at most 8 payload bits per check bit; each check signal must be correct in every cycle its Check Enable term is true (Table 5-1: for example, `PADDRCHK` whenever `PSEL` is high, `PRDATACHK` only at a read's completion). A VIP checks parity on what it receives and, for error-injection tests, can corrupt what it drives; what the receiver then does is not prescribed (§5.4).
- **RME (§3.6):** `PNSE` must be valid with `PSEL` and needs `PPROT`; with `PPROT[1]` it selects Secure, Non-secure, Root or Realm (Table 3-5). A requester with `RME_Support` cannot be connected to a completer without it (Table 3-6).

### Expert: recommendations are not rules

Several APB statements are recommendations, and they have changed between issues. IHI 0024C (APB4, 2010) recommended that "the address and write signals are not changed immediately after a transfer, but remain stable until another access occurs" to save power (§3.1.2, Note); IHI 0024E recommends that signals not required to be valid are driven to zero (Appendix A). `PSLVERR` low outside completion is recommended in both. A checker that turns a recommendation into an error will fail correct designs built to the other issue. Keep the rule assertions strict, express recommendations as cover properties or warnings, and state in the VIP's documentation which issue it checks. The same discipline applies to `a_wait_bound`: it is the test's contract with one completer, so make the bound a parameter and put it in the test plan.

### Kata: random `PREADY` waits, checked by assertions

**Goal.** Prove that this lesson's agent holds every request signal through any number of wait states, with evidence that the waits really happened, then watch the assertions catch two planted problems.

**Tasks.**
1. **Write a completer with random wait states:** a module `apb_rand_completer #(int unsigned MAX_WAIT = 7)` with the same ports as `apb_regs`. It holds four words at `0x0` to `0xC` (honouring `PSTRB`), answers `PSLVERR` above `0xC`, and, for every transfer, draws a number of wait states from 0 to `MAX_WAIT` at the SETUP edge with `$urandom_range` (IEEE 1800-2023 §18.13.2).
2. **Write the stimulus and the evidence:** `apb_random_seq`, 200 transfers with random direction, data and word-aligned addresses from `0x0` to `0x14`; and `apb_wait_stats`, a `uvm_subscriber #(apb_item)` on the agent's analysis port that counts transfers by wait states and errors, and reports a `UVM_ERROR` in `report_phase` unless it saw 0, 1 and at least one larger number of wait states.
3. **Run it** (`tb_top` with `apb_rand_completer` as `dut`, test `apb_kata_test`) and **predict** before you look: which assertions fire, and which cover properties are hit?
4. **Mutant A:** in `apb_driver::drive_transfer()`, add `vif.drv_cb.paddr <= it.addr + 4;` inside the wait loop. Predict which assertion fires, at which edge relative to the first wait state, and on which transfers it never fires.
5. **Mutant B:** restore the driver and set the completer's `MAX_WAIT` to 20 (the interface's `MAX_WAIT` stays 16). Predict what fires, and decide whether it is a protocol bug.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

**1. The completer.** `pready` is combinational from two counters, so it can be high in the very first ACCESS cycle (zero wait states):

```systemverilog
// compile: apb_rand_completer.sv (replaces apb_regs as dut in tb_top for the kata)
`timescale 1ns/1ps

module apb_rand_completer #(int unsigned MAX_WAIT = 7) (
  input  logic        pclk, presetn,
  input  logic        psel, penable, pwrite,
  input  logic [31:0] paddr, pwdata,
  input  logic [2:0]  pprot,
  input  logic [3:0]  pstrb,
  output logic        pready, pslverr,
  output logic [31:0] prdata
);
  logic [31:0] mem [4];      // words at 'h0, 'h4, 'h8, 'hC
  int unsigned target;       // wait states drawn for this transfer
  int unsigned count;        // wait states served so far
  logic        bad;

  assign bad     = (paddr > 32'hC);
  assign pready  = psel && penable && (count == target);
  assign pslverr = pready && bad;
  assign prdata  = (psel && !pwrite && !bad) ? mem[paddr[3:2]] : '0;

  always_ff @(posedge pclk or negedge presetn) begin
    if (!presetn) begin
      target <= 0;
      count  <= 0;
      foreach (mem[i]) mem[i] <= '0;
    end else if (psel && !penable) begin        // SETUP edge: draw the waits
      target <= $urandom_range(MAX_WAIT, 0);
      count  <= 0;
    end else if (psel && penable && !pready) begin
      count <= count + 1;                       // one more wait state served
    end else if (pready && pwrite && !bad) begin
      for (int n = 0; n < 4; n++)               // completion of a write
        if (pstrb[n]) mem[paddr[3:2]][8*n +: 8] <= pwdata[8*n +: 8];
    end
  end
endmodule
```

At the SETUP edge it draws `target`; at each ACCESS edge `pready` is 1 only when `count == target`, so a transfer with `target = w` completes at the (*w*+1)th ACCESS edge. `PREADY` is 0 outside ACCESS, which §3.1.2 allows.

```systemverilog
    if (!n_by_waits.exists(t.wait_cycles)) n_by_waits[t.wait_cycles] = 0;
    n_by_waits[t.wait_cycles]++;
    if (t.slverr) n_err++;
  endfunction

    string s;
    foreach (n_by_waits[w]) s = {s, $sformatf(" %0d:%0d", w, n_by_waits[w])};

              UVM_LOW)
    if (!n_by_waits.exists(0) || !n_by_waits.exists(1) || n_by_waits.size() < 3)
      `uvm_error("WAIT_STATS", "goal not met: need 0, 1 and more wait states")
  endfunction
```

**3. What you should see.** No `APB_SVA` error: the driver drives the request once, in SETUP, and the wait loop drives nothing. `WAIT_STATS` reports eight wait counts, 0 to 7, each on roughly an eighth of the 200 transfers, and `errors=` on roughly a third (addresses `0x10` and `0x14` are two of the six word addresses the sequence can draw); the exact numbers depend on the seed, which is tool-dependent. `c_no_wait`, `c_one_wait` and `c_error` are hit. `c_back_to_back` is not: `apb_driver` always idles one cycle, which the expert section's driver changes. The evidence matters as much as the silence: an assertion that never saw a wait state proves nothing about wait states.

**4. Mutant A.** `a_wait_hold` fails. Its antecedent holds at the first wait-state edge; the driver's drive made there lands just after that edge, so at the next edge `$stable(paddr)` is false: the failure is reported one edge after the first wait state, once per transfer that had at least one wait state. Transfers with zero wait states never execute the loop body, so they never fail, which is about one transfer in eight here and *every* transfer against a zero-wait completer. That is the reason for random waits. Note also what the completer did: it completed the transfer at `addr + 4`, while the monitor's item says `addr`, so a scoreboard would have reported a mysterious data mismatch somewhere else.

**5. Mutant B.** `a_wait_bound` fires on transfers with 17 or more wait states, and nothing else does. This is **not** a protocol bug: APB allows any number of wait states (§3.3.2). It is a mismatch between the testbench's policy (16) and this completer. Raise the interface's `MAX_WAIT` to match the completer's specification, or, if that specification promises at most 16, report the completer.
