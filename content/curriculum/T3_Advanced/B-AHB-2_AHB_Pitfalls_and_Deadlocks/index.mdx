## Quick Take

- **What it is:** The AHB failures that cost the most debug time: a slave that never finishes, write data used on the wrong edge, control that changes during a wait, a one-cycle ERROR, locks that starve other masters, bursts over a 1KB boundary and bad reset values. For each one you get the waveform symptom and a SystemVerilog Assertions (SVA) check that catches it.
- **Why it matters:** A check that is too weak passes a broken slave; the classic case is an ERROR check that never runs. A check that is too strict flags behaviour the specification requires, such as a cancel after ERROR or BUSY turning into SEQ, and the team switches it off. Both leave bugs in silicon.
- **The Analogy:** Tuning a smoke detector. Too sensitive, and people pull the battery after the third false alarm while making toast. Too dull, and it sleeps through a real fire. A good AHB checker is tuned the same way: it knows exactly which "smoke" is legal. *Where it breaks:* a smoke detector has one threshold you can turn up or down. Protocol rules are not tunable at all; only integration budgets, such as the number of wait states or the length of a lock, are yours to set.

**You will be able to:**
1. Diagnose from a waveform a slave stuck with `HREADYOUT` low, write data acted on at the wrong edge, and an illegal control change during a wait state, and tell each apart from the changes the specification allows (IHI0033B.b §3.6).
2. Write an assertion pair for the two-cycle ERROR response that also catches a one-cycle ERROR, and explain why the one-directional check passes vacuously.
3. Classify each check as a protocol rule or an integration policy (wait-state budget, lock length), and report the two at different severities.
4. Write a per-beat 1KB check that applies only to incrementing bursts, and an alignment check for every transfer, both of which accept legal WRAP bursts.
5. Diagnose reset faults (`HTRANS` not IDLE or `HREADY` low during reset), and treat an arbiter's grant during reset as integration policy.

## Build Your Mental Model

**How to read this lesson.** The core path is the eight pitfalls, each with a picture and a check, then the recap table. Practitioner depth follows: the lab experiments, where the checks live in a testbench, and a symptom-to-check debugging table. Make It Work assembles every check into one checker and runs it on six directed traces. Push Further is the expert layer.

**Words you need first.** You met these in [B-AHB-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-1_AHB_Design_Timing_Mechanics/index) and [I-SV-4A](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-4A_SVA_Fundamentals/index); here is a one-line reminder of each.

- A **master** starts transfers and a **slave** answers them. The AHB specification issue IHI0033B.b uses these words; the later issue IHI0033C calls them Manager and Subordinate. This lesson cites IHI0033B.b section numbers; the table in References gives the IHI0033C numbers.
- Every AHB **transfer** has an **address phase**, one cycle in which the master drives the address (`HADDR`) and control (`HTRANS`, `HWRITE`, `HSIZE`, `HBURST`), and a **data phase**, one or more cycles in which write data (`HWDATA`) or read data (`HRDATA`) moves. The address phase of one transfer overlaps the data phase of the previous one (§3.1).
- `HTRANS` is the **transfer type**: IDLE (no transfer), BUSY (a pause inside a burst), NONSEQ (a single transfer or the first beat of a burst) or SEQ (a later beat of a burst) (§3.2, Table 3-1). A **burst** is a run of transfers to related addresses; each transfer in it is a **beat**.
- A slave drives **`HREADYOUT`** low to ask for more time. The **interconnect**, the logic between masters and slaves, routes the selected slave's `HREADYOUT` onto **`HREADY`**, which every component sees. An edge where `HREADY` is low is a **wait state**: nothing completes, and both the current data phase and the next address phase are held (§3.1).
- `HRESP` is the **response**: low for OKAY, high for ERROR (§5.1, Table 5-1).
- The **decoder** turns the address into one select signal, `HSELx`, per slave, and the **multiplexor** returns the selected slave's `HREADYOUT`, `HRESP` and `HRDATA` to the master (§4.2, §4.3). In a system with several masters, an **arbiter** in the interconnect decides whose transfer goes next.
- A **concurrent assertion** checks a property at every clock edge on values sampled just before the edge. In `a |-> b` the left side is the **antecedent** and the right side the **consequent**; `|=>` checks the consequent one edge later. When the antecedent is false the attempt succeeds **vacuously**: it passes without testing anything. `$past(x)` is the value of `x` at the previous edge, `$stable(x)` is true when `x` did not change, and `disable iff (c)` switches the check off while `c` is true.

### Rule or budget? Tagging every check

Every check in this lesson carries one of two tags.

- **[Protocol §x]**: a rule in the AHB specification. A failure is a bug in the component that broke it, and the checker reports it as an error.
- **[Integration policy]**: a budget your system chooses, such as how many wait states a slave may insert or how long a lock may last. A failure means "slower than we planned", not "illegal", and the checker reports it as a warning.

| Situation | Tag | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A waited NONSEQ changes its address | [Protocol §3.6.2] | The specification forbids it (Pitfall 3) |
| A one-cycle ERROR | [Protocol §5.1.3] | ERROR always takes two cycles (Pitfall 4) |
| A slave inserts 40 wait states | [Integration policy] | The specification asks only that each slave has a predetermined maximum (§5.1.2 Note); 40 may be fine for a boot ROM |
| A locked sequence lasts 60 cycles | [Integration policy] | A lock may not be broken (§3.3), but how long one may last is your budget |
| The arbiter keeps its default grant for a cycle after reset | [Integration policy] | IHI0033B.b defines no grant at all; arbitration belongs to the interconnect (§4.1) |

Mixing the two is the most common reason good checkers get switched off: a slow but legal slave reported as a protocol violation trains the team to ignore the checker.

### Pitfall 1: a slave that never finishes

**Symptom.** `HREADY` falls and never rises. On a single-layer bus, where all masters share one path to the slaves, the whole bus freezes. On a multi-layer interconnect, masters that use other slaves may keep going; that design is outside IHI0033B.b (§4.1).

**Cause.** The slave has no bound on its wait. This version drops `HREADYOUT` whenever its memory is busy and has no limit at all:

```systemverilog
// snippet
// BUG: unbounded wait. If slow_memory_busy sticks high, so does the wait.
assign hreadyout = !(data_phase && slow_memory_busy);
```

**What the specification says.** "In general, every slave must have a predetermined maximum number of wait states" before it completes a transfer, so that the maximum bus latency can be calculated (IHI0033B.b §5.1.2 Note). The same Note recommends at most 16 wait states, but exempts devices such as a serial boot ROM. There is no timeout in the protocol: a slave with 40 wait states is legal if 40 is its designed maximum.

**The fix is a design choice.** A slave can count its wait states and finish at a limit, with OKAY or with the two-cycle ERROR of Pitfall 4. That limit, like the check below, is [Integration policy].

**Predict first.** Slave A's data phase starts at edge 2 and `HREADY` stays low for five edges. With a budget of four wait states, at which edge should the check below warn?

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Integration policy] MAX_WAIT comes from your latency budget, not the spec.
property p_wait_budget;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    $fell(HREADY) |-> ##[1:MAX_WAIT] HREADY;
endproperty

a_wait_budget: assert property (p_wait_budget)
  else $warning("HREADY held low for > %0d cycles", MAX_WAIT);
```

### Pitfall 2: acting on write data at the wrong edge

**Symptom.** A FIFO receives the same word several times, a counter jumps by more than one, or a write-to-clear register clears early. Plain memories look fine, because writing the same value twice changes nothing.

**Cause.** During a wait state the master holds `HWDATA` stable until the transfer completes (§6.1.1). RTL that acts on every edge of the data phase acts once per wait state. The slave must act only at the edge where `HREADY` is high, and must register the address phase only at an edge where `HREADY` is high too, because a slave samples `HSELx`, address and control only then (§4.2). `HSELx` is the decoder's select signal for slave x.

```systemverilog
// snippet
// Address phase: register the transfer only when HREADY is high (§4.2)
always_ff @(posedge HCLK or negedge HRESETn) begin
  if (!HRESETn) begin
    write_sel <= 1'b0;
  end else if (HREADY) begin
    write_sel <= HSEL && HTRANS[1] && HWRITE;   // HTRANS[1]: NONSEQ or SEQ
    addr_reg  <= HADDR;
  end
end
```

```systemverilog
// snippet
// BUG: acts on every cycle of the data phase, once per wait state
always_ff @(posedge HCLK) begin
  if (write_sel) begin
    fifo[wr_ptr] <= HWDATA;
    wr_ptr       <= wr_ptr + 1;
  end
end
```

```systemverilog
// snippet
// FIXED: act only at the edge that completes the data phase
always_ff @(posedge HCLK) begin
  if (write_sel && HREADY) begin
    fifo[wr_ptr] <= HWDATA;
    wr_ptr       <= wr_ptr + 1;
  end
end
```

The two `always_ff` blocks are alternatives. Never compile both: each writes `fifo` and `wr_ptr`, and a variable assigned in an `always_ff` must not be written by any other process (IEEE 1800-2023 §9.2.2.4).

**Predict first.** A write to A gets two wait states. How many times does each version push?

### Pitfall 3: changing control during a wait state

**Symptom.** A waited transfer's address or type changes before `HREADY` rises. The slave that decoded the first address may already be working on it, and the transfer that completes is not the one the master meant.

**The rule and where it comes from.** IHI0033B.b states the hold rule in three places, and a checker combines them:
- §3.6.1: while the slave is inserting wait states, the master "must not change the transfer type", with listed exceptions; after an IDLE→NONSEQ or BUSY→SEQ change it must keep `HTRANS` constant until `HREADY` is high.
- §3.6.2: the master "can only change the address once", except during an IDLE transfer and after an ERROR response; once an IDLE has become NONSEQ, it must keep the address constant until `HREADY` is high.
- Figure 3-16: "Until HREADY goes HIGH, no more address changes are permitted."

The standard checker reading, used by `p_hold_in_wait` below, by the lab model and by B-AHB-3, is therefore: **a waited NONSEQ or SEQ holds its type, address and control.** Figure 3-16 shows the one permitted change as the IDLE becoming NONSEQ; it is not permission to retarget a waited NONSEQ.

**The exceptions, all legal:**
- IDLE may change to NONSEQ, and the address of an IDLE may change (§3.6.1, §3.6.2, Figures 3-13 and 3-16);
- in a fixed-length burst (INCR4/8/16, WRAP4/8/16), BUSY may change to SEQ (§3.6.1, Figure 3-14);
- in an undefined-length INCR, BUSY may change to any type; SEQ continues the burst, IDLE or NONSEQ ends it (§3.6.1, Figure 3-15);
- after the first cycle of an ERROR response, the master may change to IDLE to cancel the rest, and change the address (§3.5.2, §3.6.2, Figure 3-17). A cancel must be IDLE, not a new NONSEQ.

**Debrief.** The change at edge 3 is legal because the waited transfer was an IDLE. The change at edge 6 is illegal because C was a waited NONSEQ. Notice that D is then accepted at edge 6, so the slave for C never sees its transfer.

**The check, with its exceptions.** One property covers NONSEQ and SEQ; a second covers what a waited BUSY may become.

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Protocol §3.6.1, §3.6.2, §3.5.2] a waited NONSEQ/SEQ holds,
// unless the master cancels after an ERROR first cycle
property p_hold_in_wait;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (!HREADY && HTRANS inside {NONSEQ, SEQ}) |=>
        ($stable(HTRANS) && $stable(HADDR) && $stable({HWRITE, HSIZE, HBURST}))
     || ($past(HRESP) && HTRANS == IDLE);
endproperty

// [Protocol §3.6.1, §3.5.2] what a waited BUSY may change into
property p_busy_in_wait;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (!HREADY && HTRANS == BUSY) |=>
        (HTRANS inside {BUSY, SEQ} && $stable(HADDR))
     || ($past(HBURST) == INCR && HTRANS inside {IDLE, NONSEQ})
     || ($past(HRESP) && HTRANS == IDLE);
endproperty

a_hold_in_wait: assert property (p_hold_in_wait);
a_busy_in_wait: assert property (p_busy_in_wait);
```

Read `p_hold_in_wait` line by line. The antecedent picks every edge where a NONSEQ or SEQ is waiting. One edge later (`|=>`), either everything is unchanged, or the previous edge carried an ERROR (`$past(HRESP)`, which is the first ERROR cycle, because `HREADY` was low) and the master now shows IDLE. In `p_busy_in_wait`, the BUSY's address already names the next beat (Table 3-1), so a BUSY→SEQ change keeps the address.

**The classic mistake.** A shorter check such as `(!HREADY && HTRANS != IDLE) |=> $stable(HTRANS) && $stable(HADDR)` treats "control never changes in a wait" as the rule. It fires on a legal BUSY→SEQ change and on a legal cancel after ERROR. Make It Work runs it in your head against the same traces: it fails three times, and two of those are false alarms.

Bursts have more control rules than the hold rule: control is identical on every SEQ beat (Table 3-1), and a SINGLE is never followed by BUSY (§3.5.1). [B-AHB-3](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-3_AHB_Verification/index) assembles those checks into the full checker.

### Pitfall 4: the one-cycle ERROR

An ERROR response always takes **two cycles** (§5.1.3, Table 5-2). In the first, the slave drives `HRESP` high (ERROR) with `HREADYOUT` low; in the second, `HRESP` stays high and `HREADYOUT` goes high, and the transfer completes. The slave may insert OKAY wait states before the first ERROR cycle.

**Why two cycles.** Because of the pipeline, when the slave decides to answer A with ERROR, the master has already put the address of B on the bus. In the first ERROR cycle `HREADY` is low, so B is not accepted. The master samples the ERROR at the edge that ends that cycle, and can drive IDLE in the second cycle to cancel B (§5.1.3, §3.5.2). It may also continue with B, or with the remaining beats of a burst: both are allowed (§3.5.2).

**Predict first.** In the waveform below, what does `HTRANS` show in the second ERROR cycle, and is B ever accepted? It is generated from the same AHB model as the lab in [B-AHB-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-1_AHB_Design_Timing_Mechanics/index).

**Debrief.** `HTRANS` is IDLE in the second ERROR cycle, so B is cancelled: it was on the bus for two cycles, both with `HREADY` low, so it was never accepted. A master cannot cancel a transfer that has already started (§5.1), which is exactly why the first ERROR cycle has `HREADY` low.

**The bug.** A slave that drives ERROR for only one cycle, with `HREADYOUT` high, breaks §5.1.3. The master learns of the error at the same edge that accepts its next transfer, so it can no longer cancel it.

```systemverilog
// snippet
// BUG: one-cycle ERROR. HREADYOUT stays high, so there is no first ERROR cycle.
always_ff @(posedge HCLK or negedge HRESETn) begin
  if (!HRESETn)
    hresp <= OKAY;
  else if (HSEL && HREADY && HTRANS[1] && access_violation)
    hresp <= ERROR;
  else
    hresp <= OKAY;
end
```

```systemverilog
// snippet
// FIXED: first cycle HREADYOUT low, second cycle HREADYOUT high (§5.1.3)
logic err_second;

always_ff @(posedge HCLK or negedge HRESETn) begin
  if (!HRESETn) begin
    err_second <= 1'b0;
    hresp      <= OKAY;
    hreadyout  <= 1'b1;                    // HREADYOUT high during reset (§7.1.2)
  end else if (err_second) begin           // second ERROR cycle
    err_second <= 1'b0;
    hresp      <= ERROR;
    hreadyout  <= 1'b1;
  end else if (HSEL && HREADY && HTRANS[1] && access_violation) begin
    err_second <= 1'b1;                    // first ERROR cycle
    hresp      <= ERROR;
    hreadyout  <= 1'b0;
  end else begin
    hresp      <= OKAY;
    hreadyout  <= 1'b1;
  end
end
```

The fixed slave returns to normal decoding after the second cycle, so a transfer accepted at the end of the ERROR (when the master continues) is handled like any other. Again the two listings are alternatives, never one compiled unit.

**The checks: you need both directions.**

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Protocol §5.1.3, Table 5-2] first ERROR cycle -> second ERROR cycle
property p_error_first_then_second;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (HRESP && !HREADY) |=> (HRESP && HREADY);
endproperty

// [Protocol §5.1.3, Table 5-2] an ERROR completion needs a first cycle
property p_error_second_needs_first;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (HRESP && HREADY) |-> $past(HRESP && !HREADY);
endproperty

a_error_first_then_second:  assert property (p_error_first_then_second);
a_error_second_needs_first: assert property (p_error_second_needs_first);
```

The first property alone **cannot** catch the one-cycle ERROR. A one-cycle ERROR never has `HREADY` low, so the antecedent never matches and every attempt succeeds vacuously; the assertion stays silent (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.14.8). The second property starts at every ERROR completion and looks one edge back. At the first edge after reset, `$past` returns the value sampled at the last reset edge (OKAY), because `disable iff` stops evaluation but not sampling, so a one-cycle ERROR straight after reset still fails (§16.9.3).

### Pitfall 5: locks that starve other masters

**What a lock is.** A master that needs an indivisible sequence, such as the read and write of a semaphore, asserts **`HMASTLOCK`**. It tells every slave that the current sequence "must therefore be processed before any other transfers are processed" (§3.3). `HMASTLOCK` has the same timing as the address and control signals (Table 2-2). All transfers in a locked sequence must go to the same slave address region, and the master is recommended to insert an IDLE after it (§3.3).

**Who arbitrates.** IHI0033B.b defines a master interface with no request or grant signals (§2.2). A system with several masters needs an interconnect that arbitrates, and the specification leaves its design to the integrator (§4.1). The `HBUSREQ`/`HGRANT` arbiter of older AMBA 2 AHB systems, and the arbiters inside today's interconnects, are therefore integration policy.

**The starvation.** While the lock holds, other masters' transfers to that slave wait for the whole sequence, **wait states included**. On a single-layer bus they wait for the whole bus; on a multi-layer interconnect the effect depends on its design (outside IHI0033B.b, §4.1). A long lock to a slow slave starves everyone else, whatever their priority.

    { kind: "return", from: "s", to: "ic", label: "Data A, OKAY" },
    { from: "m0", to: "ic", label: "NONSEQ write A, HMASTLOCK=1" },
    { from: "ic", to: "s", label: "write A (locked)" },
    { kind: "divider", label: "more wait states" },
    { kind: "return", from: "s", to: "ic", label: "OKAY" },
    { from: "m0", to: "ic", label: "IDLE, HMASTLOCK=0: lock ends" },
    { from: "ic", to: "s", label: "M2's transfer at last" }
  ]}
/>

**Fixed priority plus a lock.** Put three masters behind a fixed-priority arbiter: M2 (high) before M1 (medium) before M0 (low). M0 starts a locked read-modify-write to a slow slave; M2 then requests the same slave and waits for the whole lock; when the lock ends, fixed priority serves M2 before M1, so M1 waits behind both. This is **not a deadlock**: M2's worst wait is bounded by the longest locked sequence plus the slave's wait states. The real failure is an *unbounded* wait, from a lock that never ends or a slave with no wait-state limit. (The Mars Pathfinder reset is sometimes quoted here; it was a priority inversion on an operating-system mutex, not a bus arbitration bug.)

**Do not revoke a lock.** Breaking a locked sequence breaks the atomicity it exists to provide. Give locks a cycle budget and report a violation from a checker instead.

**The check.** IHI0033C §3.3 makes the lock's extent precise: the bus is locked after a cycle with `HMASTLOCK` and `HREADY` high, and unlocked after a cycle with `HMASTLOCK` low and `HREADY` high. Counting by that definition includes the wait states of the last locked data phase, which a counter driven by `HMASTLOCK` alone misses, because `HMASTLOCK` is an address-phase signal.

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Integration policy] lock budget in cycles, wait states included.
// Locked after an edge with HMASTLOCK and HREADY high; unlocked after an
// edge with HMASTLOCK low and HREADY high (IHI0033C §3.3).
logic        locked;
int unsigned lock_cycles;

always_ff @(posedge HCLK or negedge HRESETn) begin
  if (!HRESETn) begin
    locked      <= 1'b0;
    lock_cycles <= 0;
  end else if (HREADY) begin
    locked      <= HMASTLOCK;
    lock_cycles <= HMASTLOCK ? lock_cycles + 1 : 0;
  end else if (locked) begin
    lock_cycles <= lock_cycles + 1;      // a wait inside the lock still counts
  end
end

a_lock_budget: assert property (@(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
                                lock_cycles <= MAX_LOCK_CYCLES)
  else $warning("Locked sequence has lasted %0d cycles", lock_cycles);
```

**In a multi-master testbench**, run a long locked sequence to a slow slave while higher-priority masters keep requesting, measure each master's worst wait, and compare it with your latency budget. That is a performance check [Integration policy], not a protocol assertion.

### Pitfall 6: slaves that answer IDLE and BUSY wrongly

**The rule.** IDLE means "no transfer" and BUSY means "the master is continuing with a burst but the next transfer cannot take place immediately". For both, slaves "must always provide a zero wait state OKAY response" and the transfer "must be ignored by the slave" (§3.2, Table 3-1). The **default slave**, which the interconnect selects for addresses no real slave owns, does the same for IDLE and BUSY, and answers NONSEQ and SEQ with ERROR (§4.2.1).

**Two bugs.**
- A slave that inserts wait states on an IDLE stalls the bus although nobody is transferring.
- A slave that acts on a BUSY (or an IDLE) updates state or pushes data for a cycle it must ignore. A BUSY carries the *next* beat's address (Table 3-1), so a slave that treats it as a transfer may write that beat twice.

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Protocol §3.2 Table 3-1, §4.2.1] IDLE and BUSY get a zero-wait OKAY
property p_idle_busy_zero_wait_okay;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (HREADY && HTRANS inside {IDLE, BUSY}) |=> (HREADY && !HRESP);
endproperty

a_idle_busy_okay: assert property (p_idle_busy_zero_wait_okay);
```

The antecedent includes `HREADY`, so it fires only for an IDLE or BUSY that was actually accepted. The check sees only the multiplexed `HREADY`, which belongs to the slave selected for that data phase, so it catches the selected slave misbehaving. The "acts on BUSY" bug has no bus-level symptom; a scoreboard catches it.

**ERROR read data.** "ERROR responses do not require valid read data" (§6.1.2). A master, monitor or scoreboard that records `HRDATA` from an ERROR-completed read stores garbage. And after a cancel, the master has "no requirement … to rebuild the burst" (§3.5.2), so a scoreboard must not wait for the cancelled beats.

### Pitfall 7: bursts over a 1KB boundary and unaligned addresses

**The rule in one line.** Masters must not start an **incrementing** burst that crosses a 1KB address boundary (§3.5); the derivation and the wrap arithmetic are in [B-AHB-1](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/B-AHB-1_AHB_Design_Timing_Mechanics/index). Because the smallest region a slave can own is 1KB, aligned on a 1KB boundary (§4.2), the rule keeps every incrementing burst inside one address decode region. WRAP bursts are not subject to it.

**What goes wrong without it.** The decoder decodes every address phase (§4.2). The beats after the boundary may go to a different slave, one that owns the next 1KB region. That slave sees a burst that starts with SEQ and has no NONSEQ, and neither slave sees the whole burst, often with no error response to flag it.

**Check every beat, not the burst start.** That also covers an undefined-length INCR, whose length is unknown at its NONSEQ.

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Protocol §3.5, §4.2] incrementing bursts never cross 1KB.
// Per beat, so undefined-length INCR is covered; WRAP bursts are exempt.
property p_incr_no_1kb_cross;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (HREADY && HTRANS == SEQ && !(HBURST inside {WRAP4, WRAP8, WRAP16})) |->
      HADDR[31:10] == $past(HADDR[31:10], 1, HREADY && HTRANS inside {NONSEQ, SEQ});
endproperty

a_incr_no_1kb_cross: assert property (p_incr_no_1kb_cross);
```

`$past(expr, 1, gate)` returns the value at the most recent earlier clock edge where `gate` was true (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.9.3): here, the previous accepted beat. `HADDR[31:10]` is the 1KB region number.

**Alignment.** Every transfer in a burst must be aligned to its size, not just the first (§3.5): a word (`HSIZE` = 0b010) needs `HADDR[1:0]` = 0b00, a halfword needs `HADDR[0]` = 0. An unaligned address makes the active byte lanes ambiguous (§6.1.1).

| `HSIZE` | Size | `HADDR` low bits that must be 0 |
|---|---|---|
| 0b000 | byte | none |
| 0b001 | halfword | `HADDR[0]` |
| 0b010 | word | `HADDR[1:0]` |
| 0b011 | doubleword | `HADDR[2:0]` |

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Protocol §3.5] every NONSEQ/SEQ transfer is aligned to HSIZE
property p_aligned;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
    (HTRANS inside {NONSEQ, SEQ}) |-> ((HADDR & ((32'd1 << HSIZE) - 32'd1)) == 32'd0);
endproperty

a_aligned: assert property (p_aligned);
```

A WRAP4 from 0x3F4 passes: each of its beats is word-aligned. IHI0033B.b also says "the address for IDLE transfers must also be aligned", or bus monitors may report spurious warnings (§3.5); IHI0033C downgrades that to a recommendation. This property deliberately skips IDLE.

### Pitfall 8: reset values

**The rule.** `HRESETn` is the only active-low signal. It may be asserted asynchronously but is deasserted synchronously, after a rising edge of `HCLK`. During reset, every master must drive `HTRANS` = IDLE with address and control at valid levels, and every slave must drive `HREADYOUT` high (§7.1.2). A component must also "define a minimum number of cycles for which the reset signal must be asserted" before its outputs reach their reset values.

**Symptom.** A master that drives NONSEQ while `HRESETn` is still low, or a slave that holds `HREADYOUT` low in reset, starts the system in the middle of an undefined transfer. (A NONSEQ in the first cycle after `HRESETn` rises is legal.)

**The check, written so that it cannot fire falsely.** A master with a synchronous internal reset may still show its old outputs at the first reset edge, which §7.1.2 allows. So the check starts only after the component's minimum reset length:

```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Protocol §7.1.2] checked DURING reset. disable iff (1'b0) keeps any
// default disable iff in scope from switching this check off (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.15).
property p_reset_values;
  @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (1'b0)
    (!HRESETn)[*MIN_RST_CYCLES] |->
      HTRANS == IDLE && HREADY && !$isunknown({HTRANS, HADDR, HWRITE, HSIZE, HBURST});
endproperty

a_reset_values: assert property (p_reset_values);
```

`(!HRESETn)[*MIN_RST_CYCLES]` matches once reset has been low for `MIN_RST_CYCLES` consecutive edges, and `$isunknown` is true if any bit is X or Z, which tests "valid levels".

**Predict first.** `MIN_RST_CYCLES` is 2. A buggy master drives NONSEQ at edge 2, while reset is still low. At which edges does `p_reset_values` check, and where does it fail?

**What the bus check cannot see.** `p_reset_values` sees only the multiplexed `HREADY`, which is the `HREADYOUT` of whichever slave the multiplexor selects (§4.3).
```systemverilog
// snippet
// [Protocol §7.1.2] per slave: HREADYOUT high during reset
a_slave_reset: assert property (@(posedge HCLK) disable iff (1'b0)
                                (!HRESETn)[*MIN_RST_CYCLES] |-> HREADYOUT);
```

**The arbiter's grant during reset.** If every slave follows §7.1.2, `HREADY` is already high during reset. Many interconnects still hold the arbiter on a default master until reset has been released for a cycle. IHI0033B.b defines no grant (§4.1), so that is [Integration policy]: check it against your interconnect's specification, not as a protocol rule.

### Integration pitfalls the specification warns about

These come from how the decoder, multiplexor and slaves are wired together, not from one component.

- **Multiplexor select not retimed.** The decoder usually also drives the multiplexor's select, and the specification stresses that this select must be retimed to the data phase (§2.4, Note). A select that follows the *address* phase routes the next slave's `HREADYOUT`, `HRESP` and `HRDATA` while the current slave is still in its data phase. Symptom: a waited transfer completes early, or read data comes from the wrong slave.
- **`HSELx` while `HREADY` is low.** `HSELx` can be high while `HREADY` is low, and the selected slave can change before the current transfer completes (§4.2). A slave that starts work on `HSELx` without waiting for `HREADY` high reacts to a transfer it may never get (the IDLE address changes of Pitfall 3 cause exactly this).
- **Slaves that assume bursts complete.** Masters may cancel after an ERROR, and a multi-layer interconnect may terminate a burst; slaves "must be designed to work correctly if the burst is not completed" (§3.5.2). A prefetching slave or a burst counter that waits for the last beat is a bug.
- **A lock spanning two slave regions.** All transfers in a locked sequence must go to the same slave region; Issue A did not have this rule, so legacy masters must be verified for it (§3.3).

### Where these checks live in a testbench

- **In an interface or a bound checker module.** Write the properties once in a module like the `ahb_pitfall_checker` of Make It Work, and instantiate it in the testbench top or `bind` it into the design. Assertions in the AHB interface of a UVM agent work the same way. [I-SV-4C](/curriculum/T2_Intermediate/I-SV-4C_Checkers/index) covers SystemVerilog `checker` units and `bind`.
- **Report through the environment.** In a UVM testbench, call `` `uvm_error `` for protocol failures and `` `uvm_warning `` for policy budgets from the action blocks, so they count in the end-of-test summary ([A-UVM-7](/curriculum/T3_Advanced/A-UVM-7_VIP_Construction/index)).
- **Make budgets parameters.** `MAX_WAIT`, `MAX_LOCK_CYCLES` and `MIN_RST_CYCLES` come from the system's latency budget and the components' datasheets; set them per instance.
- **Pair assertions with a scoreboard.** Pitfalls 2 and 6 (pushing per wait state, acting on BUSY) leave the bus legal. Only a scoreboard that counts one effect per completed transfer sees them.

### Recap

| Pitfall | Root cause | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Stuck `HREADYOUT` | No wait-state limit in the slave | `p_wait_budget` [Integration policy] |
| Data used per wait state | Not gating on `HREADY` | Scoreboard |
| Control change in a wait | Master changes a waited NONSEQ/SEQ | `p_hold_in_wait`, `p_busy_in_wait` [Protocol §3.6] |

| Lock starvation | Long lock to a slow slave | Lock-cycle budget [Integration policy] |

## Make It Work

### A checker and six directed traces

The file below has three parts. `ahb_pkg` declares the encodings from Tables 3-1 (`HTRANS`), 3-3 (`HBURST`) and 5-1 (`HRESP`). `ahb_pitfall_checker` holds this lesson's properties, reports protocol rules with `$error` and policy budgets with `$warning`, and uses `cover property`, which counts how often a condition occurs, on both ERROR antecedents. `tb_top` replays six short traces, one table row per clock edge, with no design under test: it plays the master and the slave at the same time.

The bench drives each row like a flop output: with nonblocking assignments at the rising edge, so the checker samples the old row at that edge and the new row at the next. There is no race between the bench and the checker.

```systemverilog
// compile
`timescale 1ns/1ps
// AHB pitfall checker and a directed bench (Arm IHI0033B.b). Every row of
// TRACE is what the checker samples at one rising edge of HCLK.

package ahb_pkg;
  localparam logic [1:0] IDLE = 2'b00, BUSY = 2'b01, NONSEQ = 2'b10, SEQ = 2'b11;
  localparam logic [2:0] SINGLE = 3'b000, INCR  = 3'b001, WRAP4  = 3'b010,
                         INCR4  = 3'b011, WRAP8 = 3'b100, INCR8  = 3'b101,
                         WRAP16 = 3'b110, INCR16 = 3'b111;
  localparam logic       OKAY = 1'b0, ERROR = 1'b1;
endpackage

module ahb_pitfall_checker
  import ahb_pkg::*;
  #(parameter int unsigned MAX_WAIT       = 16,  // [Integration policy] budget
    parameter int unsigned MIN_RST_CYCLES = 2)   // from the components' datasheets
  (input logic        HCLK, HRESETn,
   input logic [1:0]  HTRANS,
   input logic [31:0] HADDR,
   input logic [2:0]  HBURST, HSIZE,
   input logic        HWRITE, HREADY, HRESP);

  // [Protocol §3.6.1, §3.6.2, §3.5.2] a waited NONSEQ/SEQ holds,
  // unless the master cancels after an ERROR first cycle
  property p_hold_in_wait;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      (!HREADY && HTRANS inside {NONSEQ, SEQ}) |=>
          ($stable(HTRANS) && $stable(HADDR) && $stable({HWRITE, HSIZE, HBURST}))
       || ($past(HRESP) && HTRANS == IDLE);
  endproperty

  // [Protocol §3.6.1, §3.5.2] what a waited BUSY may change into
  property p_busy_in_wait;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      (!HREADY && HTRANS == BUSY) |=>
          (HTRANS inside {BUSY, SEQ} && $stable(HADDR))
       || ($past(HBURST) == INCR && HTRANS inside {IDLE, NONSEQ})
       || ($past(HRESP) && HTRANS == IDLE);
  endproperty

  // [Protocol §5.1.3, Table 5-2] the ERROR pair, both directions
  property p_error_first_then_second;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      (HRESP && !HREADY) |=> (HRESP && HREADY);
  endproperty

  property p_error_second_needs_first;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      (HRESP && HREADY) |-> $past(HRESP && !HREADY);
  endproperty

  // [Protocol §3.2 Table 3-1, §4.2.1] IDLE and BUSY get a zero-wait OKAY
  property p_idle_busy_zero_wait_okay;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      (HREADY && HTRANS inside {IDLE, BUSY}) |=> (HREADY && !HRESP);
  endproperty

  // [Protocol §3.5, §4.2] incrementing bursts never cross 1KB.
  // Per beat, so undefined-length INCR is covered; WRAP bursts are exempt.
  property p_incr_no_1kb_cross;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      (HREADY && HTRANS == SEQ && !(HBURST inside {WRAP4, WRAP8, WRAP16})) |->
        HADDR[31:10] == $past(HADDR[31:10], 1, HREADY && HTRANS inside {NONSEQ, SEQ});
  endproperty

  // [Protocol §3.5] every NONSEQ/SEQ transfer is aligned to HSIZE
  property p_aligned;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      (HTRANS inside {NONSEQ, SEQ}) |-> ((HADDR & ((32'd1 << HSIZE) - 32'd1)) == 32'd0);
  endproperty

  // [Protocol §7.1.2] checked DURING reset, after the minimum reset length
  property p_reset_values;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (1'b0)
      (!HRESETn)[*MIN_RST_CYCLES] |->
        HTRANS == IDLE && HREADY && !$isunknown({HTRANS, HADDR, HWRITE, HSIZE, HBURST});
  endproperty

  // [Integration policy] wait-state budget
  property p_wait_budget;
    @(posedge HCLK) disable iff (!HRESETn)
      $fell(HREADY) |-> ##[1:MAX_WAIT] HREADY;
  endproperty

  // Action blocks run in the Reactive region, after the bench's nonblocking
  // updates, so they print $sampled/$past values: what the property checked.
  a_hold_in_wait: assert property (p_hold_in_wait)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 3.6] waited transfer changed: HADDR %h -> %h",
                $time, $past(HADDR), $sampled(HADDR));
  a_busy_in_wait: assert property (p_busy_in_wait)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 3.6.1] illegal change of a waited BUSY", $time);
  a_error_first_then_second: assert property (p_error_first_then_second)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 5.1.3] first ERROR cycle without a second", $time);
  a_error_second_needs_first: assert property (p_error_second_needs_first)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 5.1.3] ERROR completed with no first ERROR cycle",
                $time);
  a_idle_busy_okay: assert property (p_idle_busy_zero_wait_okay)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 3.2] IDLE/BUSY without a zero-wait OKAY", $time);
  a_incr_no_1kb_cross: assert property (p_incr_no_1kb_cross)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 3.5] incrementing burst crossed 1KB at HADDR %h",
                $time, $sampled(HADDR));
  a_aligned: assert property (p_aligned)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 3.5] unaligned HADDR %h", $time, $sampled(HADDR));
  a_reset_values: assert property (p_reset_values)
    else $error("t=%0d [Protocol 7.1.2] bad reset values", $time);
  a_wait_budget: assert property (p_wait_budget)
    else $warning("t=%0d [Integration policy] HREADY low for more than %0d cycles",
                  $time, MAX_WAIT);

                 n_err_first, n_err_second);

    '{IDLE,   32'h000, SINGLE, 1'b1, OKAY},

    '{SEQ,    32'h028, INCR4,  1'b1, OKAY},   // SEQ held, accepted
    '{SEQ,    32'h02C, INCR4,  1'b1, OKAY},

    '{NONSEQ, 32'h084, SINGLE, 1'b1, OKAY},   // BUG: B replaced while waited
    '{IDLE,   32'h000, SINGLE, 1'b1, OKAY},
    '{IDLE,   32'h000, SINGLE, 1'b1, OKAY},

    title[5]  = "b: broken slave answers ERROR in one cycle";
    title[9]  = "c: BUSY becomes SEQ during a wait (legal)";
    title[16] = "d: master changes a waited address (illegal)";
    title[21] = "e: slave holds HREADYOUT low for 5 cycles";

    HRESETn <= 1'b1;                     // deasserted just after an edge (§7.1.2)
  end

  // previous row at this edge and the new row at the next one.
```

### How to turn a failing assertion into a root cause

1. **Read the tag first.** A [Protocol §x] failure is a bug in the master, slave or interconnect; an [Integration policy] warning is a budget question for the architect.
2. **Put the waveform cursor on the failing edge,** and step back to the edge where the attempt started: one edge for `|=>`, `MAX_WAIT` edges for the wait budget.
3. **Find who drives the failing signal.** `HTRANS` and `HADDR` come from the master; `HREADYOUT` and `HRESP` from the slave selected in that data phase; `HREADY` and the select from the interconnect.
4. **Check the exceptions before blaming the design.** Was the waited transfer an IDLE or BUSY? Was the previous edge a first ERROR cycle? Is the burst a WRAP? If yes, the checker is wrong.
5. **Fix, then prove the check ran.** Rerun, and look at the cover counts: a check whose antecedent never matched passed without testing anything.

## Push Further

### Expert: vacuity and the coverage of a checker

A property whose antecedent never matches succeeds on every attempt without testing anything. IEEE 1800-2023 §16.14.8 calls such an attempt vacuous, and warns that vacuous success on every attempt may point to a problem in the design or in the property itself. A regression (the full set of tests, run again after every change) that never produced an ERROR passes both ERROR checks vacuously.

Cover each antecedent separately (§16.14.3): `HRESP && !HREADY` for `p_error_first_then_second` and `HRESP && HREADY` for `p_error_second_needs_first`. Covering only the first proves nothing about the second. The same applies to every implication in the checker; many tools can also report vacuous attempts per assertion (tool-dependent). In sign-off, a protocol check with zero non-vacuous attempts counts as "not verified", not as "passed".

### Expert: `$past`, `disable iff` and the edges around reset

- `disable iff` stops evaluation, not sampling. `$past` keeps its history through reset, and if there is no earlier tick it returns the default sampled value of its argument (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.9.3). That is why `p_error_second_needs_first` still fails on a one-cycle ERROR at the first edge after reset.
- A `default disable iff` declared in a module applies to every concurrent assertion in that scope that has no disable clause of its own (§16.15). A reset check placed in such a scope is disabled exactly while it should be checking. Write `disable iff (1'b0)`, which the LRM's own example describes as the way to cancel the default.
- Action blocks run in the Reactive region (§16.14.1, example in §16.9.3), after nonblocking updates. Print `$sampled(x)` or `$past(x)` in a failure message, never plain `x`, or the message shows the next edge's value.

### Expert: locked sequences in real systems

- **Locked IDLEs.** A master may assert `HMASTLOCK` on IDLE transfers at the beginning, in the middle or at the end of a locked sequence. Locked IDLEs at the start or end, and a lock held over IDLEs only, are "permitted, but not recommended", because they can hurt arbitration (IHI0033B.b §3.3). A checker should warn on those cases, not fail.
- **One slave region.** Every transfer in a locked sequence must go to the same slave region. Issue A had no such rule, so legacy masters must be verified for it (§3.3). Check it by recording the 1KB region (or the `HSELx` decode) of the first locked NONSEQ and comparing every later locked NONSEQ or SEQ against it until the lock ends.
- **Who must implement it.** Most slaves can ignore `HMASTLOCK`, but a slave reachable from more than one master, such as a multi-port memory controller, must implement it (§3.3).
- **Issue C adds the exact extent:** locked after a cycle with `HMASTLOCK`, `HSEL` (if present) and `HREADY` high; unlocked after a cycle with `HMASTLOCK` low and `HREADY` high (IHI0033C §3.3). The lock counter in Pitfall 5 uses that definition.

### Expert: incomplete bursts and ERROR data

Bursts end early in two ways (§3.5.2): the master cancels after an ERROR, or a multi-layer interconnect terminates a burst so that another master can reach the slave. Masters are not allowed to end a burst early on their own, but slaves "must be designed to work correctly if the burst is not completed". Consequences for verification:

- A checker must never demand that a fixed-length burst completes after an ERROR.
- A scoreboard must not wait for cancelled beats, because the master has no requirement to rebuild the burst (§3.5.2).
- A monitor must not record `HRDATA` of an ERROR completion as read data (§6.1.2). IHI0033C §5.1.3 adds the security angle: a master that receives ERROR on a read "might still use the data", a slave "cannot rely on the ERROR response to prevent the reading of a value", and driving `HRDATA` to zero on an ERROR read is recommended.

### Expert: interconnect effects on bus-level checks

- **Arbitration stalls look like wait states.** In the generic interconnect of IHI0033C §4.4, `HREADY` to a master also stalls it "when the Manager is waiting for arbitration from within the interconnect". A wait-budget check on a master port then measures slave waits plus arbitration, so its budget must include the longest lock and the arbiter's policy (an estimate, not a figure from the specification; the §5.1.2 Note says only that a predetermined maximum lets the bus latency be calculated).
- **Several selects per slave.** One slave interface may have several `HSELx` signals, each decoding its own region of at least 1KB (§4.2.2). A 1KB crossing may therefore land in the *same* slave on a different logical interface, and still be illegal.
- **Where to bind.** A checker on a master port sees that master's view; one on a slave port sees that slave's. Pitfalls 4 and 6 are best caught at the slave port, where `HREADYOUT` and `HRESP` are not yet multiplexed.

### Expert: what "stable" means

Signals that must be stable are required to hold the same value "when sampled at different rising clock edges in an extended transfer"; they may glitch between edges (IHI0033B.b §7.1.1). `$stable` compares sampled values at edges, so it checks exactly that rule. Glitch-free behaviour between edges is the AHB5 **Stable_Between_Clock** property (§7.1.1), which no clocked SVA property can check; it needs static timing or gate-level analysis.

### Expert: AHB5 exclusive transfers as checks

AHB5 components that support the **Exclusive_Transfers** property add `HEXCL` (master) and `HEXOKAY` (slave). Both rules translate directly into assertions:
- `HEXOKAY` "must only be asserted in the same cycle as `HREADY`" and "must not be asserted in the same cycle as `HRESP`" (IHI0033B.b §8.3.1): `HEXOKAY |-> HREADY && !HRESP`.
- An exclusive transfer must be a single beat, of burst type SINGLE or INCR, with no BUSY, aligned to its size (§8.4).

The multiplexor must route `HEXOKAY` from the selected slave as well (§4.3 Note). In IHI0033C the same rules are §10.3.1 and §10.4.

### Expert: which issue are you checking against?

IHI0033C (2021) renumbers most of Chapter 3 and changes several details that a checker author meets:
- The recommendation of at most 16 wait states, and the serial boot ROM exemption, are gone from the §5.1.2 Note; only the predetermined maximum remains.
- IDLE address alignment changes from a rule ("must") to a recommendation (IHI0033C §3.6).
- A new chapter defines which signals must always be valid: `HTRANS`, `HADDR`, `HSEL`, `HMASTLOCK`, `HREADY`, `HREADYOUT` and `HRESP` (IHI0033C §8.2). An always-on `!$isunknown` check on those is a protocol check under issue C.
- Write strobes (`HWSTRB`, IHI0033C §3.5) are new, and the lock extent is defined (§3.3).

Pin the issue in your verification plan, and keep one parameter per checker that enables issue-C-only checks.

### Expert: when the 1KB guarantee does not cover a WRAP burst

The 1KB rule and the §4.2 guarantee cover incrementing bursts. A WRAP burst stays inside its beats × size block, which is at most 1KB for every size up to 512 bits. A WRAP16 of 1024-bit transfers (`HSIZE` = 0b111, 128 bytes) has a 2KB wrap block, which spans two 1KB regions (Table 3-2, §3.5). IHI0033B.b does not discuss the case; this is arithmetic, not a stated rule. On a system with 1024-bit data paths, make sure no slave region boundary falls inside a possible wrap block, or forbid that burst in the master.

### Kata: the ERROR pair against three traces

Each table gives `HREADY` and `HRESP` at consecutive rising edges; edge 1 accepts transfer A. For each trace, predict which of `p_error_first_then_second` and `p_error_second_needs_first` fails, and at which edge, before you open the answer.

| Trace | Edge 1 | Edge 2 | Edge 3 | Edge 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) `HREADY` | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| (a) `HRESP` | OKAY | OKAY | ERROR | ERROR |
| (b) `HREADY` | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| (b) `HRESP` | OKAY | OKAY | ERROR | OKAY |
| (c) `HREADY` | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| (c) `HRESP` | OKAY | ERROR | ERROR | ERROR |

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

- **(a) Legal two-cycle ERROR after one OKAY wait: nothing fails.** `p_error_first_then_second` starts at edge 3 (ERROR, `HREADY` low) and sees ERROR with `HREADY` high at edge 4. `p_error_second_needs_first` starts at edge 4 and finds the first cycle at edge 3.
- **(b) One-cycle ERROR: `p_error_second_needs_first` fails at edge 3.** Edge 3 has ERROR with `HREADY` high, but edge 2 was OKAY. `p_error_first_then_second` never starts: vacuous.
- **(c) ERROR held for three cycles: `p_error_first_then_second` fails at edge 3.** The attempt from edge 2 needs `HREADY` high at edge 3, and it is low. The attempt from edge 3 passes at edge 4, and `p_error_second_needs_first` passes at edge 4, because edge 3 was a first-cycle pattern. The specification allows OKAY waits before an ERROR, not extra ERROR cycles with `HREADY` low (§5.1.3).

Confirm (b) in the lab's **Debug: one-cycle ERROR** scenario, and in Make It Work trace b.

</details>

### Drill: name the pitfall from the waveform

Each row describes what a waveform shows. Name the pitfall, say whether it is a protocol violation or a budget question, and name the check, before you open the answers.

1. `HTRANS` is BUSY at 0x28 with `HREADY` low, then SEQ at 0x28 with `HREADY` low, then SEQ at 0x28 with `HREADY` high, in an INCR4.
2. `HREADY` stays low for 25 edges after a read to a flash controller, then the read completes with OKAY.
3. `HTRANS` is NONSEQ at 0x100 with `HREADY` low, then NONSEQ at 0x200 with `HREADY` high and `HRESP` OKAY.
4. An INCR8 of halfwords starts at 0x7FA.
5. During reset, after the minimum reset length, `HREADY` is low.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. **Legal.** BUSY→SEQ during a wait in a fixed-length burst, at the same address (§3.6.1). `p_busy_in_wait` passes; a naive stability check would fire.
2. **Budget question.** Slow but legal if 25 is within the controller's predetermined maximum (§5.1.2 Note). `p_wait_budget` warns if 25 exceeds your `MAX_WAIT` [Integration policy].
3. **Protocol violation.** A waited NONSEQ changed its address without an ERROR (§3.6.2, Figure 3-16). `p_hold_in_wait` fails.
4. **Protocol violation.** Halfwords from 0x7FA: 0x7FA, 0x7FC, 0x7FE, 0x800: the fourth beat crosses the 1KB boundary at 0x800 (§3.5). `p_incr_no_1kb_cross` fails at that beat.
5. **Protocol violation** by the slave whose `HREADYOUT` is routed: slaves must drive `HREADYOUT` high in reset (§7.1.2). `p_reset_values` fails; the per-slave check names which slave.

</details>
