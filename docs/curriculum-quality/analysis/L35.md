**Expert: absent** (no `## Push Further`). Add these `### Expert:` items:
1. **Combinational paths, register slices and what a pin-level checker cannot see.**
   - "On master and slave interfaces there must be no combinatorial paths between input and output signals" (§A3.1.1, §A3.2.1). This applies in both directions.
   - A register slice "can be inserted at almost any point in any channel, at the cost of an additional cycle of latency" (§A1.3.3).
   - Full-throughput slices (skid buffers) are design practice; label them so.
   - A combinational path is structural. Simulation SVA cannot detect it; lint, STA or structural formal checks can (tool-dependent; label it).
2. **Default READY values and their obligations (§A3.2.2).**
   - AWREADY/ARREADY default HIGH is recommended. "When AWREADY is HIGH the slave must be able to accept any valid address that is presented to it."
   - Default LOW "forces the transfer to take at least two cycles".
   - WREADY, BREADY and RREADY may default HIGH only if that side can always accept in one cycle.
   - "it must assert the RVALID signal only in response to a request for data."
3. **Why AXI4 added the B-after-AW dependency.**
   - "by issuing a write response, the slave takes responsibility for hazard checking the write transaction against all subsequent transactions" (§A3.3.1 note).
   - An AXI3 slave that responds before accepting the address is not AXI4 compliant and needs a wrapper. "Any AXI3 master complies with the AXI4 write response requirements" (§A3.3.2).
4. **Reset corner cases (§A3.1.2, Figure A3-1).**
   - Asynchronous assertion vs edge-sampled assertions: what an `@(posedge ACLK)` check can miss between edges.
   - The first edge after reset.
   - Synchronous-reset masters flagged at the first reset edge: a policy decision, label it.
   - X on VALID: `$isunknown` (IEEE 1800-2023 §20.9) as checker practice, not a spec rule.
   - Transactions abandoned by reset need a testbench flush policy; point to the proposed A-UVM-9 and label it policy.
5. **Responses and burst completion.**
   - "For a write transaction, a single response is signaled for the entire burst"; read beats carry their own response.
   - "the required number of data transfers must be performed, even if an error is reported" (§A3.4.4).
   - "No component can terminate a burst early" (§A3.4.1).
   - Consequence for monitors: count beats regardless of RRESP.
6. **Checker architecture.**
   - Counters give necessary conditions only; per-ID pairing belongs in B-AXI-6.
   - The same properties become `assume` on DUT inputs for formal (IEEE 1800-2023 §16.14.2), e.g. master-side rules assumed when proving a slave.
   - `bind` a checker (§23.11).
   - `cover property` (§16.14.3) for the handshake timing classes: READY first, back-to-back, W before AW, stall lengths.
7. **Throughput and latency.**
   - Back-to-back transfers need READY held HIGH.
   - Default-LOW READY costs a cycle per transfer (§A3.2.2 note).
   - Each register slice adds a cycle (§A1.3.3).
   - Measure with handshake counts (link B-AXI-6).
8. **Interconnect realignment of AW and W (§A3.3 note).** "When the interconnect is required to determine the destination address space or slave space, it must realign the address and write data." So W-first traffic exercises the interconnect's W buffering.
9. **Interview angles** (attempt-first, `InterviewQuestionPlayground`):
   - May READY be a combinational function of VALID? (No, at a master or slave interface.)
   - Prove that a master–slave pair obeying §A3.3.1 cannot deadlock at the handshake level, then show how an interconnect still can (B-AXI-5).
   - A slave answers B after WLAST but before AWREADY and passes an AXI3 VIP: what do you tell the designer?
   - A monitor drops writes only under random backpressure: diagnose.
   - Which handshake rules become assumptions when formally verifying a slave?
   - How do you check "no VALID in reset" when reset asserts asynchronously?

**`## Quick Take`**
- **What it is:** AXI4 carries every transaction over five one-way channels (AW, W and B for writes; AR and R for reads), each with its own VALID/READY handshake.
- **Why it matters:** every AXI driver, responder, monitor and protocol checker is built on these rules. Classic testbench bugs are breaches of them: a monitor that assumes AW comes first, an AXI3-style slave that answers before the address, a driver that waits for READY.
- **The Analogy:** reuse B-AMBA-2's relay baton.
  - Each channel is its own relay lane. The runner holds the baton out (VALID) and may not pull it back or swap it. The receiver opens a hand (READY). The baton passes at a clock edge when both are true.
  - *Where it breaks:*
    - the receiver may open and close its hand before the runner arrives;
    - a lane can pass a new baton at every edge;
    - the five lanes are independent except for three cross-lane rules;
    - a runner may never wait to see an open hand before holding out the baton.
- **You will be able to:** the 5 objectives below.
- **Before you start:** B-AMBA-2 (linked).

- **b-axi-1-8:** "May READY be asserted before VALID, and dropped again?" "Yes (§A3.2.1). A default-HIGH AWREADY/ARREADY is recommended (§A3.2.2)."
- **b-axi-1-9:** "Why must a source never wait for READY?" "Destinations may wait for VALID; if the source also waited, neither would assert: a deadlock (§A3.3.1)."
- **b-axi-1-10:** "May READY be a combinational function of VALID at an AXI slave interface?" "No: no combinatorial paths between inputs and outputs at master and slave interfaces (§A3.1.1, §A3.2.1). The slave may wait for VALID across edges."
- **b-axi-1-11:** "What are the AXI reset rules?" "ARESETn is active LOW. It may assert asynchronously and must deassert synchronously. During reset, master VALIDs and RVALID/BVALID are LOW, and a master may first drive VALID HIGH at a rising edge after ARESETn is HIGH (§A3.1.2)."
- **b-axi-1-12:** "Write the hold assertion for one channel." "`valid && !ready |=> valid && $stable(payload)`: it checks every stalled edge, including back-to-back stalls (§A3.2.1)."
- **b-axi-1-13:** "What is wrong with a slave that sends B as soon as WLAST arrives?" "In AXI4 it must also wait for the AW handshake (§A3.3.1). Such a slave needs a wrapper to be AXI4 compliant (§A3.3.2)."

That makes 13 cards.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **The spine card's objective 3 is narrower than the rule.** It reads "Explain why VALID must not depend combinationally on READY". IHI0022E forbids any dependency: "A source is not permitted to wait until READY is asserted before asserting VALID" (§A3.2.1), sequential or combinational. The no-combinational-path rule is separate and symmetric (§A3.1.1, §A3.2.1). Proposed card wording: "Explain why a source must never wait for READY (deadlock, §A3.3.1), and why master and slave interfaces allow no combinational input-to-output paths (§A3.1.1)." 2. **Arrow notation conflicts across the AMBA track.**
   - B-AXI-5 defines arrows as "the signal on the left waits for the signal on the right".
   - IHI0022E's dependency figures use the opposite: arrows point to the signal asserted after (§A3.3.1).
