## Completeness and depth gaps the analyst missed

**Must-cover: clock resolution (§16.16) and procedural assertions (§16.14.6).**
1. **Clocks carried by declarations.** These are §16.16's first, second and fourth sources (a named sequence or property with its own `@(…)`, and properties declared inside a clocking block), with rules b and f. The outline, V7 and card i-sv-4a-13 omit them. See L15-4A-04.
2. **Clock-inference condition c (§16.14.6).** Only one edge expression may qualify, and its signal must not be used elsewhere in the body. Teach the async-reset flop case with LRM example r2 (`posedge clock iff reset == 0 or posedge reset` infers `posedge clock iff reset==0`). The outline gives only "exactly one event control and no blocking timing control".
3. **Conditionals use current values (§16.14.6.1, a9/a10).** The conditionals that enclose a procedural concurrent assertion use current values, so `if (en)` queues one cycle earlier than `if ($sampled(en))`. This pitfall belongs to the "assertions in modules … vs procedural code" must-cover item.
4. **Static loop variables (§16.14.6.1).** A static loop variable is sampled in Preponed: 0 in the first cycle, then the loop-exit value. Correct depth-ladder expert item 1 (see L15-4A-05).

**Practitioner and expert layers.**
5. **The full §16.6 restriction list:** automatic, stateless functions; no `chandle`; `const ref` allowed (L15-4A-18).
6. **`default disable iff` scopes.** It is also legal in generate blocks (§16.15) and in checkers (Syntax 17-1, `checker_or_generate_item_declaration`). The index (`:136`) and the analyst's "default disable iff scope" item list only module, interface and program.
7. **Gated clocking events (§16.5.2, §16.14.6 r2).** `@(posedge clk iff en)` is the standard way to restrict attempts. It is absent from both the page and the outline.
8. **Bit-vector functions (§20.9).** `$onehot`, `$onehot0`, `$countones` and `$countbits` are everyday SVA vocabulary, for example in the one-hot check of quiz Q10. Only `$isunknown` is planned.
9. **Expert: assertion control.** The `$assertoff` family uses assertion_type 15 and directive_type 7. It therefore leaves `expect`, `unique` and `priority` alone (Table 20-6). `list_of_scopes_or_assertions` can name single assertions as well as scopes (Syntax 20-12).
10. **`always_comb` sensitivity (§9.2.2.2.2).** `always_comb` "is sensitive to expressions in immediate assertions within the procedure and within the contents of a function". This explains why the block re-runs in the deferred-assertion glitch example, and belongs in the sub-lesson.
11. **`$inferred_clock` and `$inferred_disable` (§16.14.7).** They let reusable properties default to the context's clock and reset. Expert level and optional; a link to I-SV-4C suffices.
12. **Spine §5.2 rules 10 and 14 for the worked example.** Say why the interface uses `$error` rather than UVM reporting (A-UVM-7 owns routing). Give a summary signature, or explain the intentional `FAIL=1` from the injected bug.
