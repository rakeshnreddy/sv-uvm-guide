## Completeness and depth gaps the analyst missed

Spot-check of the analyst's tables:
- The "covered" and "missing" marks hold. `restrict`, bounded vs full proofs and formal apps are absent. The constraint-vs-assumption section really is covered.
- One mark should read *yes* rather than partial. The cover-for-vacuity rule is stated twice (index.mdx:97, :150), but there is no example. Leave it partial, as the analyst did, because the example is missing.
- The analyst's concept-completeness grid is fair.

Gaps not in the analyst's lists:

1. **Formal coverage and proof quality (expert; tool-dependent).** Cone-of-influence or proof-core coverage, and mutation-based checks of whether the assertions would catch a changed design. These answer "are my assertions enough?", which sign-off needs. The analyst cites G29's list ("formal coverage") only as an interview alignment, not as content. Add it to Expert 8 (hybrid sign-off).
2. **Keeping formal-only constraints out of simulation (practitioner).** Contrast three ways to keep a formal-only constraint from firing in simulation:
   - `restrict` (§16.14.4);
   - assertion control (`$assertcontrol`, §20.11, cited by §16.14.2);
   - tool-specific defines such as `` `ifdef FORMAL `` (tool-dependent).

   This is daily hybrid-flow practice, and the analyst's Q2 relies on it.
3. **`cover sequence` vs `cover property`** (§16.14.3); see L43-EINT1-V05.
4. **Checker-construct variant of the harness.** Spine objective 1 says "bound checkers", and I-SV-4C is a prerequisite. The analyst's worked example uses only a module. Add one line, or a short variant, showing that the same properties can live in a `checker` bound with `bind` (§23.11 allows binding a checker). Free `rand` checker variables (§17.7) need the checker form anyway.
5. **Core picture of what formal does.** The analyst's outline step 1 ("What formal verification does") has no picture. Propose a small reachable-state graph: count 0→1→2→3→4 with push and pop edges from reset, under the contract. Then the same graph with `a_never_fill` cutting the 3→4 push edge, so count == 4 becomes unreachable. It makes "explores every input the assumptions allow, from reset" and "over-constraint prunes behaviour" visible before the visualizer. It can be an `<ArchitectureDiagram>` with `generic` nodes, with an aria-label and a text equivalent.
