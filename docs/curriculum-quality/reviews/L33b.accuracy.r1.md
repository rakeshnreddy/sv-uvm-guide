## Findings

| 1 | S3 | index.mdx:911 | The page recommends checking the same-region rule "by recording the 1KB region (or the `HSELx` decode) of the first locked NONSEQ and comparing every later locked NONSEQ or SEQ against it". §3.3 requires the same **slave address region**, and a slave may own many contiguous 1KB blocks (§4.2 gives only the minimum). A 1KB-block comparison therefore fires on a legal locked sequence that spans two 1KB blocks of one slave, for example locked transfers at 0x3FC and 0x400 to a 4KB slave. The lesson's own principle is not to flag legal traffic. | Compare the decoded slave region: the `HSELx` (or slave index) from the address map, taken at the first locked transfer. Mention the 1KB block only as a conservative proxy, labelled as one that can raise false alarms. | IHI0033B.b §3.3, §4.2 |
| 2 | S4 | index.mdx:414 | Quotation marks around IDLE "no transfer". Table 3-1 reads "Indicates that no data transfer is required." The BUSY quote in the same sentence is exact. | Write: IDLE "indicates that no data transfer is required". Or drop the quotation marks around "no transfer". | IHI0033B.b §3.2 Table 3-1 |
| 3 | S4 | index.mdx:480 | "An unaligned address makes the active byte lanes ambiguous (§6.1.1)." §6.1.1 says nothing about unaligned addresses. It says only that the master drives the appropriate byte lanes for narrow transfers, and it refers to Endianness (§6.2). The claim is a fair inference, but the citation does not support it. | "Byte-lane selection (§6.1.1, §6.2) assumes an aligned address, so an unaligned one has no defined lanes." Or drop the citation. | IHI0033B.b §6.1.1, §6.2 |
| 4 | S4 | index.mdx:383 (and 388–389) | The page gives the IHI0033C §3.3 lock extent as "locked after a cycle with `HMASTLOCK` and `HREADY` high". C adds "HSEL asserted if present", and the expert item at :913 states that correctly. The two statements of the same definition disagree. | Add "(and `HSEL`, if present)" at :383. Note that the counter is a master-side check, where no `HSEL` exists. | IHI0033C §3.3 |
| 5 | S4 | index.mdx:927 | "Pitfalls 4 and 6 are best caught at the slave port, where `HREADYOUT` and `HRESP` are not yet multiplexed." The properties as written use the multiplexed `HREADY`. The page does not say this, so a reader who binds the properties unchanged at a slave port checks the wrong signal. | Add one sentence: "At a slave port, replace `HREADY` with `HREADYOUT` in the response terms, and gate the antecedent with `HSELx && HREADY`." | IHI0033B.b §4.2, §4.3, Table 5-2 |
