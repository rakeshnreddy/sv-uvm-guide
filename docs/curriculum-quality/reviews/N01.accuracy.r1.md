| 1 | S3 | `index.mdx:127` | "If your DUT resets asynchronously, assert reset at any time but release it the same way", that is, with `<=` just after the active edge. That is race-free in zero-delay RTL simulation. But F4C, the page it links, tells learners to release reset "away from the active clock edge" because of recovery and removal checks (F4C `index.mdx:334`). A release on the edge is exactly what those checks flag at gate level and in hardware. The two modules contradict each other for an asynchronous reset. | Reword: "If your DUT resets asynchronously, you may assert reset at any time, but release it between clock edges, away from the active edge, as [F4C] explains (recovery and removal)." Alternatively, say that a `<=` release is race-free in RTL simulation but lands on the clock edge, which a real asynchronous-reset flop forbids. | F4C `index.mdx:329-336`; IEEE 1800-2023 §4.9.4 |
