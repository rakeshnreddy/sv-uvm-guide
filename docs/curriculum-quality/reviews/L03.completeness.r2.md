## Findings

### S3

**S3-1. Four of the six interview playgrounds still give the key away by length.**
- **Where:** `index.mdx:956` (P1 key 179 chars vs distractors 54–84), `:965` (P2, 160 vs 36–88), `:988` (P4, 177 vs 40–60), `:999` (P5, 195 vs 55–63).
- **Problem.** In each of these the correct option is about twice as long as the longest distractor and is the only one with a multi-clause "do X, then Y, and Z" shape. Position no longer cues the key, but length does: a learner who picks "the long one" scores 4/4 on these without weighing the distractors, which defeats the attempt-first design that spine §5.3/§5.8 asks for. The author deferred this because the keys are model answers to open questions; that is true, but the extra detail can live in the feedback, which the learner reads right after answering.
- **Fix.** For each of P1, P2, P4 and P5, cut the key to its decision (about 80–110 characters) and move the rest into its `feedback`. For example, P1: "`bit [31:0] exp_by_id [int][$]`: pop per ID, delete empty keys, fail unless pending is 0" with the push/pop/delete detail in the feedback; P5: "Matched entries are never deleted; delete on every match and check for leftovers at the end". Alternatively, lengthen one distractor per item with a plausible misconception of similar length.
- **Source checked:** spine §5.5 (the answer must not be guessable; distractors from real misconceptions), §5.8 (interview angles are attempt-first); README rubric R6; scratch parse of option lengths.
