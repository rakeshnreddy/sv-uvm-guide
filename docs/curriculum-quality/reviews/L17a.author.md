## Lab `ipc-deadlock`

**Design.** One file, `tb_top`, runs two parts in sequence, each with its own watchdog inside an isolation wrapper (`fork begin fork … join_any; disable fork; end join`, §9.6.3). Helpers `take_key()`/`give_key()` record who waits for and who holds each key, which a semaphore does not record. Part 1: a sender drops corrupt item 2 through an early `return` that skips `give_key(BUS)` (BUG 1). Part 2: the mover takes table A then B, the auditor B then A (BUG 2). Harness checks per part: nothing blocked at the deadline; never two holders of one key; exactly one key per resource at the end (missing and surplus).

**Traced logs** (by hand against §9.3.2, §9.6.1, §9.6.3, §15.3; every same-time pair is causally ordered, so no line depends on §4.7 order). The exhaustive-order model (scratchpad `l17a/scen_lab.py`) gives one log per configuration, equal to the README: starter 36 orders, one fix 36, both fixes 144.
- Starter: Part 1 prints items 0–1 and reads 0–1, drops item 2 at 100 ns, then the sender (item 3, from 110 ns) and the receiver (read 2, from 95 ns) wait for the bus key held by `sender (item 2)`; report at 300 ns. Part 2 deadlocks at 303 ns with no line printed; report at 400 ns; `PASS=0 FAIL=2`, then `$fatal`. Without the watchdogs the run would end quietly at 110 ns (§9.6.1).
